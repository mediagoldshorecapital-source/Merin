05 — Production spec

How to build the creative

Derived from the twelve winning ads. The rules are unintuitive and they are the whole point.

  1. Write to qualify, not to attract

    In your account, hook rate, hold rate and CTR all correlate negatively with return, in every month measured. Losing ads buy 36% more clicks and convert them 39% worse. Every line should give a non-buyer a reason to scroll past.

  2. Use the winning sentence architecture

    [Symptom question, second person]? "It's not just [dismissal she's been given]…" "Your [named mechanism] is [committed verb: collapsing / plummeting]!" "[Strong verb] it with [product] and get back [b1], [b2] & [b3]." "[Explicit CTA] →"

    Rotate: symptom · dismissal · benefit triad order · CTA verb. Four variables give you dozens of testable variants from one proven skeleton.

  3. Video: 20 seconds, hook in 3, captions always

    She watches Facebook Reels in a recliner with the TV on. Sound is often off. Burn captions in. Open on the symptom, not the product. Show a woman who looks 58–68, not 45 — the research is explicit that she is comparing herself to her peer group.

  4. Drop "75% OFF TODAY ONLY" from headlines

    It lost on conversions-per-click in five months out of five. Her buying decision is driven by the per-bottle anchor on the landing page ($29.98 → $16.66), not by a shouted percentage in the ad. Lead with the per-bottle math instead.

  5. Judge new creative on conversions per click

    ≥ 4.0% → scale 3.0–4.0% → hold and iterate < 2.8% → kill

    Give each new ad ~40 clicks (≈$60 at your $1.50 CPC) before deciding. Read it from Meta — your Triple Whale checkout event only fires on about 63% of purchases.

  6. Fix your ad naming before you scale production

    - Video - Ad -2, - Video - Ad - 2 and Video - Ad - 2 are currently three separate rows. Adopt [Product]_[Angle#]_[Format]_[Variant]_[Date] so angle-level reporting works without manual cleanup.

08 — The $1M question

Six months is realistic. Three is not.

You asked how to reach $1M in the next 3–6 months. Here is the honest arithmetic, because the answer changes what you should do this week.

Current run rate: ~$118K/month (August pacing). $1M cumulative over 6 months needs an average of $167K/month — a 41% lift. Over 3 months it needs $333K/month — a 183% lift. The 3-month version can only be bought with spend, and at a 1.43 ROAS against a ~1.70 breakeven, buying it would destroy the margin that funds everything else. Chase the 6-month number.
Read the shape, not the decimals. The point of the model is that the growth comes from the recurring block, not the acquisition block. You do not need more traffic to approach $1M — you need the subscription base to compound and your average order to get bigger. Both are cheaper than media. Treat these as directional: the retention assumptions rest on two months of history.

09 — Blind spots

What you're missing that isn't in either document

  1. You have no idea what your subscription churn actually is

    The entire business now rests on 0.59 re-bills per subscriber, and you have two months of history. If first-cycle retention slips below 60%, scaling acquisition makes losses worse, not better. Build a weekly cycle-1 / cycle-2 / cycle-3 retention report before anything else in this document.

  2. You are not measuring the thing your model depends on

    You optimise ads on ROAS and engagement. Neither predicts re-bills. Start tracking CAC-to-second-order rate by creative — which ads produce customers who stay. Two ads with identical ROAS can have very different retention, and right now you cannot see it.

  3. No creative speaks to the subscription at all

    97% of orders are subscriptions and re-bills are your only profitable order type, yet not one ad in six months mentions replenishment, "never run out", or the ongoing relationship. Every ad sells the one purchase you lose money on.

  4. Three of four products have no cost data

    ASTA, Collagen and Calcium Balm all have null COGS. You are making bundle and promotion decisions on products whose margin you cannot calculate. Calcium Balm carries a 40.9% discount into that blind spot.

  5. You are single-channel, single-product, single-country

    100% Meta, 94% one SKU, 99.96% US. Any one of an ad-account restriction, a supply problem, or a payment-processor issue takes revenue to zero. Nothing in a 3–6 month sprint fixes this, but it should be on the plan.

  6. Your April data is still missing

    27 days of orders (6 Apr – 2 May) never made it into Triple Whale — roughly $90–100K. Until it's backfilled, every trend, cohort and year-over-year comparison you build will be wrong in the same place.

If you do only four things

1. Rewrite every ad running the hedged "Feeling more tired…" construction into the committed version — same insight, working sentence. 2. Fix Klaviyo and ship the Day 0–95 flow; it is $25–35K/month sitting on a list you already own. 3. Rebuild the bundle ladder so the 6-bottle tier is the default again. 4. Stop discounting re-bills. None of these requires a dollar of additional ad spend, and together they are most of the distance to $1M.
