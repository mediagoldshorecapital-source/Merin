## The winning DNA — eight elements that repeat

Ranked by how many of the nine they appear in. **Anything you build should carry at
least four of these.** They are the load-bearing walls; everything else is decoration.

| # | Element | In | Why it holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The decay statistic** — "half your NAD by 50", "60% gone by your 50s" | V1 V3 V5 V7 V8 | One number that explains a decade of symptoms. It is the single most repeated line across all nine. |
| 2 | **The molecular-size / bioavailability argument** — NAD+ is too large to enter the cell; NMN has transporters | V2 V6 V8 (+V3) | Our most-repeated *argument*. It disqualifies the entire NAD+ shelf without naming a competitor. |
| 3 | **The 10-in-1 stack, read aloud** — Trans-Resveratrol, Apigenin, Vitamin C, CoQ10, Grape Seed, Glutathione, Astaxanthin, TMG, Quercetin | V3 V6 V7 V8 | Listing takes 8 seconds and does three jobs: value, differentiation, and poly-pill elimination. |
| 4 | **A credential in the first 8 seconds** — naturopath, nurse practitioner, doctor, 10 years in pharma | V2 V3 V5 V8 | Buys permission to make a mechanism claim. Ads without it have to earn attention twice. |
| 5 | **Price de-anchoring** — "$100–200 a month elsewhere" → "$16.66 a bottle" | V1 V4 V6 | Never defends price. Moves the reference point first, then states ours. |
| 6 | **Scarcity as a report, not a threat** — "it took me 4 weeks to get my second jar", "if you see the orange cart, it's back in stock" | V4 V7 V8 V9 | Framed as the creator's own inconvenience. Reads as reporting, not pressure. |
| 7 | **The authority ladder** — celebrity → Harvard scientist → third-party observer | V1 V7 | Three different kinds of proof stacked so no single one has to carry the claim. |
| 8 | **Visible-symptom barrage** — jowls, hooded eyes, puffy eye bags, brain fog, achy joints, "back into your high school jeans" | V3 V7 | Rapid-fire specific, visible symptoms. The specificity is the mechanism, not the length of the list. |

## What the winners tell us that the competitor set didn't

1. **NAD+ vs NMN is our strongest argument, not a niche one.** It carries three of nine winners (V2, V6, V8) and supports a fourth. The competitor set treated it as one angle among many. For us it is the spine of the whole MOFU/BOFU library.
2. **Credentialed talent outperforms our un-credentialed talent.** Four of the nine open with a credential. Casting a real practitioner is the highest-leverage production decision we make.
3. **A 20-second BOFU offer ad is a legitimate creative.** V9 has no education, no mechanism, no problem statement — just offer mechanics and a UI directive — and it won. That format was entirely absent from the 31 competitor ads.
4. **Post-purchase content converts as an ad.** V5 speaks to people who already own the bottle, and it works as paid media. That audience is bigger than it looks: everyone who bought a competitor's NMN is in it.

## Two things to fix before the next batch ships

**1. The protocol contradiction.** V1's concept copy says *"two capsules first thing in
the morning on an empty stomach."* V5 says *"take it with food, because you can get
nausea, diarrhea, gas and bloating if you don't buffer it."* Both are live. A customer
who sees both ads gets contradictory instructions from the same brand, and one of them
will blame the product when it goes badly.

Lock one protocol and use it in every asset. Everything in `07-variations/` uses:
> **Two capsules in the morning. With food if you're sensitive. Not after 2 PM.**

Change it if the formulation team says otherwise — but change it *everywhere*.

**2. Three red-lane claims are running.** These stop scrolls, and they are also the
three most likely to draw a platform rejection or a regulatory letter:

| Live claim | Problem | Substitute of equal force |
|---|---|---|
| "I'm literally aging in reverse" (V3) | Age-reversal as a product claim | "I look better at 54 than I did at 44, and I can show you the same lamp in both photos." Proof, not claim. |
| "You might genetically change" (V4) | Genetic-modification claim | "You might stop recognising the tired version of yourself." Same ironic beat, no genetics. |
| "The FDA banned it because Big Pharma wanted it" (V4) | Unsubstantiated regulatory narrative | "It spent two years in regulatory limbo and it's back. Here's what actually happened." Same intrigue, factually defensible. |

The variations that follow keep the structure of the winner and swap the claim.
Where that happens it's flagged inline — the swap is never a deletion, always a
substitute that does the same persuasive work.
