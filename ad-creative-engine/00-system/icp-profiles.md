# ICP Profiles & the Awareness Ladder

Every brief names one ICP and one awareness level. Ads that try to speak to both ICPs
at once test worse than either ad aimed at one — the hook has to sound like it was
written about *one specific person's Tuesday*.

---

## ICP 1 — "Linda" (primary volume driver)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Woman, 52–68. Retired or semi-retired, or still working and tired of it. Grandkids. Walks the dog. Watches TikTok/Reels in the evening. |
| **Buying trigger** | A specific, daily, physical humiliation — not an abstract fear of aging. |
| **Primary pain** | Wakes up unrested. Hits a wall between 2:00 and 3:00 PM. Stiff before her feet hit the floor. Sugar cravings mid-afternoon. Sits down "for a minute" and loses the evening. |
| **Secondary pain** | Skin looks slack, especially jaw and neck. Doesn't recognize herself in photos. Was told it's "just menopause" / "just your age" and hates that answer. |
| **What she has already tried** | Coffee (2–4 cups), collagen, a multivitamin, B12, maybe a cheap NMN from Amazon that did nothing. |
| **Deepest objection** | *"I've bought supplements before and felt nothing. Why is this different?"* |
| **Second objection** | *"Is this safe? Is it going to interact with my blood pressure meds?"* |
| **Third objection** | *"Is this a scam? Who actually makes this?"* |
| **What moves her** | Peer-aged women who look like her (not 28-year-old models, not celebrities). Before/afters with visible skin change. Being told her fatigue is **not her fault** and is **not permanent**. |
| **What repels her** | Bro-science, hard hype, jargon with no translation, obviously-paid celebrity endorsement. |
| **Creator casting** | 55–70 female, warm natural light, kitchen / front porch / garden, casual dress, no studio. |
| **Winning hook archetypes** | H05 Symptom Qualification, H08 Third-Party Observation, H07 Visual Age Contrast, H09 Comment Reply |
| **Best-fit formats** | F01 Talking-Head Symptom Diagnosis, F04 Peer Testimonial Montage, F05 Front-Porch UGC, F07 Before/After Reveal |

### Linda's internal monologue (use these as literal script lines)
- "I'm so tired of being tired."
- "It's not that I'm lazy — I physically cannot get back up once I sit down."
- "Everyone keeps telling me this is just what 60 is. I refuse to believe that."
- "I don't want to look 25. I want to look like myself, rested."
- "I already take five things. I don't want a sixth bottle."

## ICP 2 — "Robert" (higher AOV, lower volume, bundle buyer)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Man, 48–66. Still active — gym, workshop, golf, job. Reads labels. Watches Huberman/Rogan clips. |
| **Buying trigger** | Evidence that his current protocol is *underspecified* — wrong dose, missing cofactor, no lab test. |
| **Primary pain** | Recovery takes days instead of hours. Afternoon brain fog he has to caffeine through. Strength plateau. |
| **Secondary pain** | Suspects he's been wasting money on underdosed supplements for years. |
| **What he has already tried** | Creatine, a pre-workout, fish oil, and 250 mg NMN that "did nothing." |
| **Deepest objection** | *"Prove the dose and the purity. Show me the COA."* |
| **Second objection** | *"Why NMN and not NAD+ directly? Why your complex and not the raw powder?"* |
| **Third objection** | *"What's the per-serving cost versus buying the ingredients separately?"* |
| **What moves him** | Numbers. Third-party testing. Named researchers. Mechanism diagrams. Direct competitor spec comparison. Bundle math. |
| **What repels him** | Emotional testimonial with no data. Vague "supports wellness" language. Anything that hides the label. |
| **Creator casting** | 50–65 male, gym / garage / kitchen counter, holds the bottle and reads the panel on camera. Or a credentialed presenter (pharmacist, NP, MD) delivering spec. |
| **Winning hook archetypes** | H06 Diagnostic Failure, H10 Category Confusion, H03 Authority Name-Drop, H14 Practitioner Credential |
| **Best-fit formats** | F02 Mechanism Explainer, F03 Authority/Podcast Reaction, F06 Spec Comparison Teardown, F08 Founder Direct |

### Robert's internal monologue
- "I took NMN for three months and felt absolutely nothing. Was it the dose?"
- "250 mg is a fairy-dusted label. Nobody in the studies used 250 mg."
- "If NMN burns methyl groups, taking it alone is a half-measure."
- "Show me the certificate of analysis or I'm not interested."
- "What's it cost per gram if I buy four bottles?"

## The awareness ladder

Awareness level dictates **how much of the ad is education and where the product may enter.**
Getting this wrong is the single most common reason a well-shot ad fails.

| Level | What they already believe | Ad's job | Product may appear at | Typical hook |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | "I'm just getting older." | Reframe a symptom they've normalized as a *fixable deficit* | ≥ 60% mark | H01 Forbidden, H04 Celebrity, H08 Observation |
| **Problem-Aware** | "My energy is genuinely wrong." | Name the mechanism (NAD+ decline) and make it the villain | 50–60% mark | H05 Symptom Qualification, H12 Silent Threat |
| **Solution-Aware** | "NMN/NAD+ is the category." | Disqualify the bad version of the solution they'd otherwise buy | 35–50% mark | H06 Diagnostic Failure, H02 Elite In-Group |
| **Product-Aware** | "I know NMN. Which one?" | Win the spec fight: dose, cofactors, testing, price-per-day | 15–30% mark | H10 Category Confusion, H14 Practitioner, H13 Quantified Proof |

**Portfolio rule:** roughly 50% of the weekly slate at Problem-Aware (the biggest cold
pool), 30% Solution-Aware, 10% Unaware (cheap reach, high variance), 10% Product-Aware
(retargeting, highest ROAS, lowest ceiling).

## The objection stack — every ad must handle at least two

Ranked by how often it kills the sale, per the competitor teardown and category norms:

1. **"I've tried supplements and felt nothing."** → Answer with *mechanism of failure*: underdose, missing methyl donor, no cofactors, poor absorption. This is the highest-leverage objection in the category and the one competitor winners hit hardest.
2. **"It's too expensive."** → Never defend price. Re-anchor it: NAD+ IV drip at a clinic vs. two months of capsules; or the five bottles it replaces.
3. **"Is it safe / real / tested?"** → Third-party lab COA, made in USA, transparent label, no proprietary blends.
4. **"Will it actually work *for me*?"** → Peer-demographic proof at the same age, with a specific timeline ("give it 30 days").
5. **"Another bottle to remember."** → Frictionless protocol: one capsule, morning, empty stomach. Retire the shelf.
