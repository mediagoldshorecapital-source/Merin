# Family E — Usage Intercept
**Parent:** V5 "No-gatekeeping doc"
**What's carrying the parent:** it talks to *owners*, not shoppers. The "I don't gatekeep —
this works even if you bought a different brand" move dismantles sales resistance
completely, and the formulation comparison that follows lands as help rather than as a
pitch. It is also the only winner that turns post-purchase content into paid media.

**Why this family matters commercially:** the audience isn't just our buyers. Everyone who
bought a *competitor's* NMN is standing in the same kitchen with the same question — and
they've already paid for the category, which means they've already cleared the hardest
belief hurdle we have.

## E2 — "Three Mistakes, and the Second One Is Why You Felt Nothing"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Creative name** | `PI_ROB_SA_F01_A02_H16_9x16_v1` |
| **Parent** | V5 |
| **Transform** | **Negation flip** — protocol reframed as mistakes |
| **Format / Angle / Hook** | F01 Talking Head · A02 Underdosing · H16 |
| **ICP / Awareness** | Robert · Solution-Aware |
| **Runtime** | 45 s |
| **Bucket** | Adjacent |
| **Priority** | **Ship first** — cheapest in the family, and the transform is the one most likely to change how we brief everything else |

**Hypothesis:** "here's how to take it" is useful; "here's what you're doing wrong" is
urgent. Same content, and the numbered-mistake structure creates an open loop on mistake
two that pulls viewers through the middle of the ad — the exact place our talking-head
creatives lose people.

doesn't feel like a sales argument at all. Watch the retention curve at 0:14; if there's
no lift there, the numbered-loop device isn't doing what we think it is.
