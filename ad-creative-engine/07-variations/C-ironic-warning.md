# Family C — Ironic Warning
**Parent:** V4 "NMN Side Effects"
**What's carrying the parent:** negativity bias. A warning gets read where a promise gets
scrolled, and the reveal — the "side effects" are the benefits — is a small joke that buys
goodwill before any pitch arrives.

**The problem with the parent:** two of V4's biggest lines are red-lane. *"You might
genetically change"* is a genetic-modification claim, and *"the FDA banned it because Big
Pharma wanted it"* is an unsubstantiated regulatory narrative. All three variations keep
the structure and swap those lines for substitutes that do the same work. **C3 goes
further and tests whether a *true* warning outperforms an ironic one** — if it does, this
family becomes both safer and better.

a genuine limitation out loud should raise CTR, not lower it.

carries the same stopping power, is completely defensible, and doubles as a proof of
