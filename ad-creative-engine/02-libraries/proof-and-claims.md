# Proof & Claims Guardrails

This is the file that keeps the engine from producing ads that get the account
restricted or the brand a warning letter. **Not legal advice** — it's the operating
standard the creative team works to, and everything flagged `LEGAL` gets reviewed by
counsel before it is briefed, not after it is shot.

Longevity/NMN is a high-scrutiny category on both fronts: platform policy (Meta/TikTok
health-and-wellness rules) and regulator (dietary supplements are limited to
structure/function claims, and may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent
disease).

---

## The three-lane claim system

Every line in every script gets sorted into one of three lanes before the brief ships.

### 🟢 GREEN — write freely
Structure/function language about normal biology, plus verifiable product facts.
- "Supports cellular energy production."
- "NAD+ levels decline with age." *(a statement about biology, not about the product)*
- "1,000 mg per serving." *(if the label says so)*
- "Third-party lab tested." *(if a COA exists for the batch)*
- "Made in the USA." *(if true and substantiated)*
- "I felt more energy in the afternoon." *(a real customer's real experience, disclosed)*

### 🟡 AMBER — allowed with a specific fix
Usable only when the listed condition is met. No condition, no line.

| Line type | The fix |
|---|---|
| Personal transformation ("my skin tightened") | Real customer, signed release, disclosed as individual experience, no typicality implied |
| Before/after imagery | Same lighting, same lens, no retouching, documented dates, release on file |
| Named third-party expert or podcast clip | Commentary on the *molecule*, never on the brand. Licensed or defensible fair use. No implied endorsement |
| Celebrity reference | Commentary only. Never in the same frame as the product. No fabricated quotes. Never in OST beside the CTA |
| Competitor comparison | Unnamed and unidentifiable. "Typical shelf products", blurred labels |
| Price comparison to clinical NAD+ therapy | Use a sourced, dated public price range and say it's a range |
| Practitioner on camera | Real, current, verifiable credential; disclosure on screen; not presented as medical advice |
| Timeframe promise ("give it 30 days") | Frame as a trial suggestion, not a guaranteed outcome |

### 🔴 RED — never, in any format, in any ad
- Any disease claim: treats, prevents, reverses, cures — osteoporosis, dementia, cancer, diabetes, heart disease.
- "Reverses aging" / "turns back your biological clock" as a **product** claim. (A customer saying "I feel younger" is amber; the brand asserting age reversal is red.)
- "Clinically proven" without a trial on **this product**.
- "FDA approved" — supplements are not FDA approved. Ever.
- Guaranteed results, or any implication that results are typical without substantiation.
- Personal-attribute targeting language: "you're overweight", "your diabetes", "as a menopausal woman" — this violates platform policy and gets accounts restricted, independently of whether it's true.
- Fabricated reviews, invented testimonials, actors portrayed as clinicians, composite before/afters.
- Fake native UI: fake comment sections, fake DMs, fake news chyrons, fake platform notifications.
- Countdown timers or stock counts that aren't real.

## The forbidden/regulatory angle (A09) — how to run it safely

Competitors lead with "the government made this illegal." That framing is doing real
work and it is also where the category's legal exposure concentrates. Two rules:

1. **Be factually accurate about regulatory history.** Verify the actual status and
   timeline before writing it, and write only what you can source. Do not repeat a
   competitor's version of events because it sounds good — competitor ads in this
   category are frequently wrong, and inheriting their error inherits their liability.
2. **Never let regulatory intrigue imply efficacy.** "It was contested" ≠ "it works."
   Keep the intrigue in the *story* beat and the product claim in the *structure/function*
   beat, and don't let them touch.

Marked `LEGAL` in every brief that uses it.

## Proof asset inventory

The scarcest input in the engine. Build it deliberately — most of the formats that
convert best (F04, F07, F13-style) are gated on proof assets you don't have yet.

| Asset | Have? | Owner | Gates which formats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Batch COAs, current | | | F05, F06, F08 |
| Supplement facts panel, hi-res | | | F02, F06 |
| Manufacturing facility footage | | | F08 |
| 5+ customer video testimonials, peer-aged | | | F04, F07 |
| Before/after photo sets with releases | | | F07 |
| Written reviews cleared for on-screen use | | | F04, F10 |
| Founder on camera | | | F08 |
| Real customer comment screenshots | | | F09, F10 |
| Licensed third-party clip(s) | | | F03 |

**A standing task in the weekly cadence:** every shoot week, collect one new proof asset.
Creative quality plateaus at the level of your proof inventory, not your writing.

## Pre-ship checklist (30 seconds, every ad)

- [ ] Every claim sits in a lane, and amber claims have their fix applied.
- [ ] No red-lane language anywhere — spoken, on-screen text, or caption.
- [ ] No personal-attribute targeting in copy ("you" statements about health status).
- [ ] Disclosures present and legible for at least 2 seconds.
- [ ] All proof assets have releases on file.
- [ ] The landing page makes the same claims as the ad. (Mismatch is the most common cause of a rejection that looks like it came from nowhere.)
- [ ] Any `LEGAL`-flagged element has a sign-off recorded in the brief.
