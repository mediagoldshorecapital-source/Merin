# Hook Bank — 14 Archetypes

Every hook in the 31-ad teardown collapses into one of 14 shapes. Learn the shapes and
you never write a hook from scratch again — you pick a shape, drop in the angle
variables, and write five versions in ten minutes.

---

## The 3-second law

An ad has three seconds. In those three seconds the viewer answers one question:
**"Is this about me, and is it about to tell me something I don't know?"**

A hook must do **three jobs simultaneously** — verbal, visual, and textual:

| Layer | Requirement | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| **Verbal** | The first *spoken* words are the hook. No "hey guys", no "so", no brand name. | Warm-up words. Every syllable before the hook is a scroll. |
| **Visual** | Frame 1 is already mid-action or mid-anomaly — a face mid-sentence, a bottle already in hand, a split-screen already split. | Static logo, empty room, someone walking into frame. |
| **Textual** | On-screen text carries the hook *independently* — 80%+ of the feed watches muted at first. | OST that is a caption of the audio instead of a headline. |

### Five properties of hooks that held up across the 31 winners

1. **Specificity beats intensity.** "2:30 PM" outperforms "in the afternoon". "1,000 mg" outperforms "a clinical dose". "41 years old, I turn 42 on April 15th" outperforms "I'm in my forties."
2. **A hook is a debt, not a claim.** It opens a loop the body must close. "Here's what nobody told you" is a debt. "Our NMN is great" is a claim, and claims don't hold.
3. **Forbidden / restricted framing is the highest-stop-rate device in this category.** Five of the 31 use it. It converts regulatory friction into proof of efficacy.
4. **Disqualification outperforms persuasion.** "If your bottle isn't 1,000 mg you're wasting your money" makes the viewer audit their own shelf. Self-audit is the strongest form of engagement.
5. **Third-person observation lowers defences.** "My dad crashed at 3 PM" is heard; "You crash at 3 PM" is resisted — until the viewer has already agreed with the premise.

### The banned list
Never open with: the brand name · "Hey guys" · "Are you tired of…" · a stock B-roll pan ·
a logo sting · "As a busy mom…" · any sentence whose first four words could open any ad
in any category.

## The 14 archetypes

### H01 — Forbidden / Regulatory
**Device:** Authority tried to restrict it → therefore it works.
**Why it stops the scroll:** anti-establishment bias + curiosity gap in one sentence.
**Template:**
> "The government made this illegal because it's like [BIOMARKER] on crack."
> "The supplement the FDA tried to ban is legal again — but if you're taking it and feeling nothing, there's something nobody told you."
> "They said you can't take this without a prescription. So what's the first thing you're going to do? Get the prescription."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "This got pulled off shelves for two years. It's back — and most people are still taking it wrong."
- "When something gets restricted, that's usually the tell that it worked."
- "They tried to make this prescription-only. Ask yourself why a supplement scares anyone."

**Guardrail:** frame as *regulatory history*, not as a claim of suppressed efficacy. See `proof-and-claims.md`.

---

### H02 — Elite / In-Group Mimicry
**Device:** the people with unlimited money and information take *this specific version*.
**Template:**
> "You've heard the billionaires take [CATEGORY] — but what makes *theirs* work is [MISSING VARIABLE]."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "Longevity clinics charge $800 for the IV. The oral protocol they send you home with costs about a dollar a day."
- "Every biohacker takes NMN. Almost none of them take it with the one cofactor that makes it convert."
- "There's a reason the $2,000-a-month longevity crowd never buys single-ingredient NMN."

---

### H03 — Authority Name-Drop / Podcast Reaction
**Device:** borrow a credentialed third party's face and voice, then react.
**Structure:** 0:00 clip plays (2–4 s) → 0:04 creator reacts → 0:08 mechanism.
**Template:**
> "Why is nobody talking about what this [CREDENTIAL] just said? Listen."
> "[NAME] explains why [MOLECULE] is a borderline-illegal supplement you can use to [OUTCOME]."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "A Harvard biologist said your NAD+ drops by half before you turn 50. Nobody's acting like that's a big deal."
- "Rewind that. He just said the dose in the studies was a full gram — and your bottle has 250 milligrams."

**Guardrail:** third-party commentary on the *molecule* is not an endorsement of the *brand*. Never imply one. Clip usage must be licensed or fair-use commentary.

---

### H04 — Celebrity Pattern-Interrupt / Gossip
**Device:** pop-culture soundbite, high stop-rate, category-adjacent.
**Template:**
> "[CELEB] just said this on live TV and people are losing it. Listen to this."
> "[CELEB] looks like she's 20. It's unbelievable."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "She's 45 and the internet decided it had to be surgery. It wasn't."
- "Every red carpet this year had the same rumour attached to it, and it isn't Botox."

**Guardrail:** no implied endorsement, no name in the OST next to the product, no fabricated quotes. Commentary only.

---

### H05 — Symptom Qualification (highest-volume workhorse for Linda)
**Device:** describe a hyper-specific daily symptom so precisely the viewer feels watched, then invalidate the excuse they've been using.
**Template:**
> "If you're [SYMPTOM, exact time] and [SECOND SYMPTOM], it's not [FALSE CAUSE] — it's [MECHANISM]."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "If you wake up unrested and hit a wall at 2:30 every afternoon, that's not poor sleep. Your cells are out of fuel."
- "Stiff before your feet even hit the floor? That's not arthritis. That's a coenzyme your body stopped making at 40."
- "Raise your hand if you're tired of crashing out by 3 PM. If you're over 35, watch this whole thing — spoiler: it's not your sleep."
- "Third coffee by 2 PM and still staring at the screen. More caffeine isn't the fix — you're not low on caffeine."
- "If you sit down after dinner and that's the end of your evening, this is for you."

**Why it works:** it hands the viewer a diagnosis that removes blame. Linda has been told for a decade that this is her fault or her age.

---

### H06 — Diagnostic Failure ("you're doing it wrong")
**Device:** the viewer already bought the category and got nothing. Blame the *formula*, never the buyer.
**Template:**
> "Taking [CATEGORY] and feeling nothing? Here's exactly why."
> "Why didn't your [CATEGORY] work? It's not you. It's the formula."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "You took NMN for three months and felt nothing. That's not a you problem — that's a 250-milligram problem."
- "If your NMN has no methyl donor in it, you're filling a bucket with a hole in the bottom."
- "Most NMN bottles fail one of three tests. Go grab yours, I'll wait."

**Why it works:** it converts a *category loser* — a person who already spent money and is now cynical — into your most motivated buyer. This is the single highest-leverage archetype for Solution-Aware traffic.

---

### H07 — Visual Age Contrast / Before-After
**Device:** the proof *is* the hook. Frame 1 is a split screen or a held-up photo.
**Template:**
> "[AGE], [AGE+N]. I was [AGE] in this picture. I'm [AGE+N] now."
> "This is how I went from [STATE] to [STATE] in my [DECADE]."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "Sixty-one in the left photo. Sixty-three on the right. I'll tell you exactly what changed."
- "This is my face a year ago. Same lighting, same phone, no filter, no work done."

**Guardrail:** real customers, unretouched, consistent lighting, documented timeline, written release. Never composite. Never a stock before/after.

---

### H08 — Third-Party Observation (defence-lowering)
**Device:** the story is about someone else, so the viewer isn't being accused.
**Template:**
> "By [TIME] every day, my [RELATION] was completely drained. We thought it was just his age."
> "I thought my [RELATION] was just getting older. Until he changed one thing."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "My mother stopped coming on walks with us. She said she was fine. She wasn't fine."
- "I watched my dad give up his workshop. Six months later he was back in it."

**Why it works:** best-performing shape for **Unaware** traffic, because the viewer agrees with the diagnosis before realising it's about them.

---

### H09 — Comment Reply / Native In-App
**Device:** looks like the platform, not like an ad. Screenshot a real comment, answer it.
**Template:**
> "[NAME] asked: '[REAL QUESTION].' Great question — here's the answer."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "Michael wrote: 'I drink four coffees a day and still crash by noon.' Michael, the coffee is the problem."
- "'Do I take it with food or on an empty stomach?' — this one actually matters, and most brands won't tell you."

**Why it works:** highest native-feel of any archetype; lowest ad-blindness; doubles as social proof. Also the cheapest to produce.

---

### H10 — Category Confusion Resolver
**Device:** resolve a genuine confusion in the category. Pure value, zero hype.
**Template:**
> "[TERM A] or [TERM B]? Let me clear this up — they work at completely different levels."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "NMN or NAD+? One is the raw material, one is the finished product, and only one survives your stomach."
- "You don't need both. Here's the one you actually need and why."

**Why it works:** best archetype for **Product-Aware** retargeting. Whoever answers the question owns the purchase decision.

---

### H11 — Anthropomorphic Duel / Skit
**Device:** externalise the internal conflict as a two-character argument. The false solution defends itself and loses.
**Template:**
> [FALSE SOLUTION]: "You think you can replace me?"
> [PRODUCT]: "Replace you? You're the reason they crash at three."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- Energy drink vs. NMN bottle. Coffee mug vs. capsule. "Just push through it" vs. the product.
- Two bottles on a shelf: the 250 mg and the 1,000 mg, arguing about who's actually working.

**Why it works:** it dismantles the competing habit without lecturing the viewer, and it is one of the few formats that survives a rewatch. Highest share rate; hardest to produce well.

---

### H12 — Silent Threat / Stakes
**Device:** name a consequence that is severe, invisible, and about autonomy — not vanity.
**Template:**
> "[CONDITION] is the only disease that can end your independence without a single symptom first."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "You won't feel your bone density dropping. You'll feel the fall."
- "Nobody notices the decade their mitochondria quit. They notice the year they stop driving."

**Guardrail:** the strongest and most dangerous archetype. Fear of losing independence converts hard — and disease-prevention claims are prohibited. Frame the *stakes* emotionally; keep the *product claim* to structure/function language. Legal review mandatory.

---

### H13 — Quantified Reversal Proof
**Device:** a measured, third-party-verifiable number with a timeframe.
**Template:**
> "I reversed [MEASURED METRIC] in [TIMEFRAME] after [DURATION] of it getting worse. Follow these [N] steps and you can too."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "Six months, one change, and my energy is not a rounding error — I went from two naps a day to none."
- "Ninety days. Same job, same sleep, same schedule. The only variable was one capsule."

**Guardrail:** any metric shown must be a real document from a real customer with a release. No implied medical outcomes.

---

### H14 — Practitioner Credential Callout
**Device:** the credential *is* the pattern interrupt, delivered in the first four words.
**Template:**
> "I'm a [CREDENTIAL]. If you're taking [CATEGORY] and not feeling anything, here's exactly why."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "I'm a pharmacist. I'm 47. And I stopped recommending three of the four NMN brands on the shelf."
- "Nurse practitioner here — this is the label question I get every single week."

**Guardrail:** the credential must be real and verifiable, the person must actually hold it, and the disclosure must be present. Never cast an actor as a clinician.

---

### H15 — Ironic Warning / "Side Effects"
*(Learned from our own winner V4 — not present in any of the 31 competitor ads.)*
**Device:** open with the grammar of a warning, then reveal the "side effects" are the benefits.
**Why it stops the scroll:** negativity bias. A warning gets read where a promise gets scrolled — and the reveal is a small joke, which buys goodwill before the pitch.
**Template:**
> "Here are the side effects of [CATEGORY] nobody's talking about. Number one, and probably the biggest one: [BENEFIT STATED AS A COMPLAINT]."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "Side effects of taking NMN nobody warns you about. Number one: you'll stop recognising the tired version of yourself in photos."
- "Three complaints we actually get about this. The first one is that people run out because they started giving it to their husband."
- "Warning — do not take this after 2 PM. I'm serious, and the reason is annoying."

**Variants:** the *true warning* version (real cautions: timing, take with food) is more defensible than the ironic one and tests comparably. Prefer it where legal exposure matters.
**Guardrail:** the "side effects" must be benefits or genuine cautions — never a health claim smuggled in as a joke. "You might genetically change" is red-lane however it's framed.

### H16 — Post-Purchase Usage Intercept
*(Learned from our own winner V5.)*
**Device:** speak to the person who already bought — theirs or a competitor's — and is standing in the kitchen with a bottle they don't know how to use.
**Why it stops the scroll:** it addresses a state almost nobody targets. Everyone advertises to shoppers; this talks to owners, and owners already believe the category works.
**Template:**
> "So [CATEGORY] got shoved down your throat by every [SOURCE], you got yours in the mail, and now you don't know how to take it. I'll teach you, because I'm a [CREDENTIAL] and I don't gatekeep."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "You bought the NMN. It's been on your counter for three weeks. Here's how to actually take it."
- "Three mistakes people make with NMN, and the second one is why you felt nothing for a month."
- "Even if you didn't buy ours — take it in the morning, take it with food, don't take it after two."

**Why it works:** the "I don't gatekeep, this works even if you bought a different brand" move disarms sales resistance completely, and then the formulation comparison lands as help rather than as a pitch.
**Best audiences:** post-purchase retargeting, competitor-brand audiences, and anyone who engaged with a competitor's ad. Cheapest high-intent traffic available.

---

### H17 — Physical Lineup Elimination
*(Learned from our own winner V6.)*
**Device:** several real bottles on a counter, and a question. Elimination happens on camera, by hand.
**Why it stops the scroll:** it's a visual game the viewer wants to win. They're guessing before the second sentence. Needs no talking head, no credential, and no script beyond nine words.
**Template:**
> "Okay — which one of these is actually the best?"
> *(picks up each in turn)* "This one has [FLAW]. Same problem. Same problem. This one is the only one that [MECHANISM]."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "Which one of these four is the only one that gets into the cell?"
- "Go and get your bottle. I'll wait. Now check three things on the back of it."
- "Three of these cost more than ours and none of them list a dose on the front."

**Why it works:** it is the shortest path to a spec argument — the comparison is made physically before a single claim is spoken, so the claim arrives as a conclusion rather than an assertion.
**Guardrail:** competitor labels blurred or turned away. Compare to "typical shelf products", never to a named brand.

---

### H18 — Deal Arbitrage / Native Offer
*(Learned from our own winner V9.)*
**Device:** no problem, no mechanism, no education. An insider showing you how to get the price down, plus a physical UI instruction.
**Why it stops the scroll:** for an in-market buyer, price mechanics *are* the value. Twenty seconds, no persuasion, highest ROAS per second in the library.
**Template:**
> "Today you can get [PRODUCT] on a double discount. Let me show you how. Tap [UI ANCHOR] — that activates [DISCOUNT 1], plus [DISCOUNT 2]. This ends [DEADLINE]."

**Prime Ingredients versions:**
- "Buy two, get two free works out at $16.66 a bottle. That's twenty-eight cents a day. Tap the cart."
- "Two discounts stack on this and most people only use one. Here's the order you tap them in."

**Why it works:** it removes every cognitive step between wanting it and buying it — no code to find, no page to read, one physical instruction repeated twice.
**Guardrail:** every deadline and stock statement must be **true**. A fake countdown or invented stock count is red-lane, and in this format the offer mechanics are the entire ad — if they're false there's nothing else holding it up.
**Where it belongs:** Product-Aware and Most-Aware only. On cold traffic it has nothing to say.

## Hook multiplier: turning 1 hook into 5

Take any winning hook and run it through these five transforms. This is the mechanical
source of the 60% iteration budget.

| Transform | Operation | Example (from "You crash at 3 PM") |
|---|---|---|
| **Person swap** | 2nd person → 1st → 3rd | "I crashed at 3 PM." / "My dad crashed at 3 PM." |
| **Specificity crank** | Vaguer → sharper number, time, place | "2:47 PM, at my desk, third coffee." |
| **Negation flip** | Assert → deny the false cause | "It's not your sleep. It's not your age." |
| **Stakes escalation** | Symptom → what the symptom costs | "You didn't lose energy, you lost your evenings." |
| **Format transposition** | Same claim, different archetype | Deliver it as a comment reply (H09), or a duel (H11). |

Five transforms × 14 archetypes × 16 angles is a space you will never exhaust. The
constraint on the engine has never been ideas — it's the discipline to test them one
variable at a time.
