# KNOWLEDGE 05 · Winning Patterns, Hooks & the Metric That Decides

Everything here is measured against **1,220 ads and $381,106 of spend, 8 Feb – 7 Aug 2026**, with a 68-ad
deep-dive on everything over $700. This is the file that answers *"why should this script win?"*

---

## 1 · ⚠ THE INVERSION — the single most counter-intuitive finding

Across all seven months measured, **hook rate correlates NEGATIVELY with ROAS. Never once positive.** `[M]`

| Month | Ads | Hook ↔ ROAS | Hold ↔ ROAS | CTR ↔ ROAS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| February | 16 | −0.40 | −0.05 | −0.26 |
| March | 33 | −0.48 | −0.34 | −0.29 |
| April | 32 | −0.11 | −0.08 | −0.03 |
| May | 26 | −0.05 | −0.05 | −0.10 |
| June | 29 | −0.34 | −0.14 | −0.23 |
| July | 39 | −0.30 | −0.01 | −0.13 |
| August | 9 | −0.53 | −0.71 | −0.38 |

> **A high hook rate beside a low conversion rate is not a promising ad that needs optimising. It is a warning
> that the ad is buying the wrong audience** — and it is the exact profile of the tier that costs **$58.09** per
> acquisition instead of **$31.90**.
>
> **Promote on engagement in this account and you will systematically promote losers, because losers are
> precisely what looks best on engagement.**

### Winner vs loser profile — 68 ads over $700 spend

| Tier | Ads | Spend | ROAS | Hook | Hold | CTR | **Conv/click** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Winners** | 12 | $44,112 | **1.98** | 32.1% | 31.3% | 5.29% | **4.26%** |
| Marginal | 30 | $172,155 | 1.41 | 36.7% | 36.0% | 5.72% | 3.49% |
| **Losers** | 26 | $61,980 | 1.00 | **44.9%** | **39.6%** | **7.18%** | **2.61%** |

**Perfectly monotonic, and it runs the wrong way round. Losers buy 36% more clicks and convert them 39% worse.**

**The single most predictive creative property in this account is not stopping power. It is QUALIFICATION — how
hard the ad works to filter out people who were never going to buy.**

The best single ad in six months — ROAS 2.68, CPA $24.18 — has a **3.04% CTR, roughly half the account average**,
and converts **5.57%** of the clicks it does buy.

> **Angle F is the expensive mistake.** The second-most-produced angle — 436 ads — and among high-spend ads it
> produced **ZERO winners and NINE losers.** It has an almost identical CTR to the winning angles (5.48% vs
> 5.50%), **which is exactly why it survived: it looks fine on engagement and fails on revenue.**
>
> **Angle D is worse per dollar. Retire both.** Together they account for $61,675 at an average 0.91 ROAS.
> **Whatever replaces them cannot do worse.**

### The cleanest natural experiment — E vs F. Same insight, same audience, **53% apart on ROAS.**

| WINNER · Angle E · 1.41 ROAS | LOSER · Angle F · 0.92 ROAS |
|---|---|
| Opens on a question the reader answers "yes" to internally | Question buried in a paragraph, no line breaks |
| Second person throughout — *"your NAD+ levels"* | Explains the science before earning the attention |
| **Committed verbs** — plummeting, boost, regain | **Hedged verbs** — naturally drop, helps support |
| Benefit **triad** — energy, focus, clarity | Benefits as prose, not a scannable list |
| Explicit CTA with directional cue | No CTA in the visible portion |
| Short — 220 characters | Runs long, truncated mid-benefit |
| 4 winners / 5 losers | **0 winners / 9 losers** |

**The operative difference is commitment.** *"Your NAD+ levels are plummeting"* makes a claim the reader either
accepts or rejects — **it qualifies.** *"…naturally drop… helps support those levels"* is hedged into
meaninglessness; **nobody self-selects out, so you pay for clicks from people with no urgency.**

**Hedging is the copy-level version of the same disease as high-hook creative: it maximises audience and
minimises intent.**

## 3 · The three proven templates

### TEMPLATE 1 · Direct symptom question — **7 of 12 winners**, highest-volume proven structure

```
[Symptom question, second person] + "?"
"It's not just [dismissive explanation]…"
"Your [mechanism] is [committed verb: plummeting / collapsing]!"
"[Strong verb] them with [product] and regain [benefit 1], [benefit 2] & [benefit 3]!"
"[Explicit CTA] →"
```

**Four rotatable variables → dozens of testable variants from one skeleton:**
- **Symptom** — tired / drained / foggy / slow to recover / waking up unrested / crashing at 3 / losing the word
- **Dismissal** — just aging / just stress / just a busy season / what your doctor called normal
- **Benefit-triad order** — energy·focus·clarity / clarity·energy·focus / focus·clarity·energy
- **CTA verb** — Discover / See / Learn / Watch / Read

⚠ Line 3 is the C2 assertion. **Default to the ⒝ construction** (KNOWLEDGE-02 §5): *"It's not just aging… NAD+
levels plummet with age. Mine included."*

### TEMPLATE 2 · Category-quality contrast — **3 of 12 winners, BEST CPM IN THE ACCOUNT**

```
"Not all [category] is created equal."
"Most cheap ones are [specific failure: synthetic, underdosed, unstable] — weak, ineffective, and a waste of money."
"Meet [product] — sourced from [specific origin/spec]"
```

**Why it works: it disqualifies aggressively.** A reader who does not already believe supplements vary in quality
**opts out at line one, so you stop paying for their click.**

> **It has ONLY ever been run on Astaxanthin, which is 4% of revenue. It has NEVER been tried on NMN, which is
> 93.5%. That is the single biggest untested opportunity in the account.**
>
> The arithmetic: at $98.37 CPM, Angle E's $146,474 buys 1.489M impressions. **At $57.47 it buys 2.549M — 71%
> more impressions for the same money.**

### TEMPLATE 3 · Warning + named mechanism — **2 of 12 winners**, use sparingly

```
"WARNING: [symptom] is not just [dismissive explanation]!"
"Did you know your [named mechanism] [specific decline]?"
"[Strong verb] them back up with [product] and feel [triad] again!"
```

Broad *"WARNING: you're not getting older, you're just running out of energy"* variants sit at 1.31 ROAS. **The
winners are the ones that name a specific mechanism inside the warning itself.**

## 5 · The discount headline test — five months, five losses

| Month | 75% OFF conv/click | No headline conv/click |
|---|---|---|
| April | 2.50% | **3.44%** |
| May | 2.62% | **3.87%** |
| June | 2.59% | **3.03%** |
| July | 2.66% | **3.47%** |
| August | 2.49% | **2.76%** |

**Conversions per click is lower with the discount headline in five months out of five.** ROAS is worse in four of
five. The one month it wins on ROAS it also posts 7.58% CTR against 4.56% — **buying volume at the expense of
quality even there.**

**And it compounds:** Prime's prices already carry a permanent ~35% discount that is not code-driven. **You are
shouting a 75% discount at people who are being charged a 35% one, then paying for the resulting unqualified
clicks.**

→ **Lead the per-bottle anchor ($29.98 → $16.66) or the per-day (55¢). Never a percentage.**

## 6 · The promotion decision tree — conversions per click only

```
START: ad has ~40 clicks (≈$60 at $1.50 CPC)

├─ Conv/click ≥ 4.0%? ─── YES ──► SCALE (+20–25% / 48h), then iteration rung 1
│
├─ Conv/click 3.0–4.0%? ─ YES ──► HOLD. Iterate. Re-read in 72h
│
└─ Conv/click < 2.8% ───────────► KILL
   └─ NOW diagnose, to LEARN — not to rescue:

      ├─ Hook LOW (<25%) + conv low
      │    → The opening 3 seconds failed. Nobody stayed.
      │      Rung 1: hook swap. The body may be fine.
      │
      ├─ Hook HIGH (>40%) + hold LOW
      │    → The hook wrote a cheque the body couldn't cash.
      │      Rung 3: proof-object swap. The argument is thin.
      │
      ├─ Hook HIGH + hold HIGH + CTR HIGH + conv LOW
      │    → ⚠ THE CLASSIC LOSER PROFILE. This is the $58.09 ad.
      │      The creative is working and the QUALIFICATION is not.
      │      Do NOT iterate. Do NOT hook-swap. REWRITE THE PREMISE.
      │      It is appealing to people who have never felt this pain.
      │
      ├─ CTR HIGH + conv LOW + high ATC
      │    → Landing page or checkout. Not creative. Check G3.
      │
      └─ $60 spent with ZERO add-to-cart
           → Soft kill. Hook/landing mismatch, not variance.
             Flag for hook-swap rebuild; keep the concept alive.
```

**Why 4.0% — two independent derivations agree.** From performance: winners sit at 4.26%, marginal at 3.49%.
From arithmetic: at $1.36–1.50 CPC against a $36.37 first-order breakeven CPA, breakeven conv/click is
3.74–4.12%. **Two independent derivations landing on the same number is the strongest evidence available. Trust
this threshold over any category benchmark.**

⚠ **Read this metric from Meta, not from the pixel** — the Triple Whale checkout event fires on only ~63% of purchases.

| Metric | Role | **Never used for** |
|---|---|---|
| **Conversions per click** | **The only promotion criterion** | — |
| CPA | Guardrail and scale trigger | Early reads under 40 clicks |
| CPM | Efficiency diagnostic (the $57.47-vs-$98.37 spread) | Promotion |
| Hook rate | Diagnostic: did the first 3 seconds hold? | **Promotion. Ever** |
| Hold rate | Diagnostic: did the body deliver on the hook? | **Promotion. Ever** |
| CTR | Diagnostic: did the CTA land? | **Promotion. Ever** |
| Comment sentiment | The only in-flight read on G1/G2/G3 damage | Promotion |
| ASTA attach rate | The only meaningful read on the Margaret concepts | Promotion |

## 7 · The iteration ladder — one winner into 8–15 assets

Run the rungs **in order**. Each changes exactly one variable so the readout stays legible. **Do not start at rung 4.**

| Rung | Move | What changes | Assets | Read it on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Hook swap** | Five new 3-second openers cut onto the identical body and end card | 5 | 3s hold, then conv/click. Body is constant, so any delta is the hook |
| **2** | **Length ladder** | Winning hook + body cut to 45s / 30s / 15s / 6s bumper. No new footage | 4 | Thruplay and CPA by placement. Reels rewards 15–30s; in-feed tolerates 45–60s |
| **3** | **Proof-object swap** | Same script, different object held up: batch COA → capsules counted on white → the price arithmetic → the shipped bottle | 3 | CPA and comment sentiment. **This rung is where the operational gaps get answered at scale** |
| **4** | **Format transposition** | The winning argument re-shot in a different container: UGC → teardown → split-screen → whiteboard | 2–3 | Cost per ATC. **A message that survives a format change is a territory, not an ad** |
| **5** | **Persona re-cast** | Same script, different talent. Change nothing but the speaker | 2–3 | CPA by age/gender. **This is how you find out whether you built a persona or a mood** |
| **6** | **Awareness shift** | Rewrite one rung down — the Unaware winner becomes a Product-Aware cut naming the objection in second one | 2 | CPA against the lower rung's own target, not the parent's |

**Derivatives beat cold new concepts on hit rate in this account**, for the obvious reason: the argument is already
validated and only the container is being tested.

## 11 · The weekly operating rhythm

| Day | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Monday** | Read the week. Kill anything past guardrail. **Log dead hypotheses — execution, or idea?** |
| **Tuesday** | Build derivatives off last week's winners, rungs 1–3. Lowest-spend day (0.99 ROAS) |
| **Wednesday** | **Compliance and substantiation pass. Anything without a citable number gets CUT, not softened** |
| **Thursday** | 09:00 ET launch |
| **Friday** | **Hands off.** The best day in the account is not the day to touch budgets (1.25 ROAS, $48.47 CPA) |
| Weekend | Accumulate conversions and exit learning. **Sunday budget indexed down 15%** — biggest spend day, sixth-best return |

**Dayparting:** run 8 AM – midnight ET (the curve is flat; heavy dayparting isn't worth the delivery cost).
**2–5 PM** → a dedicated ad set for the 3 PM crash creative, *she is living the pain as she reads it*.
**10 PM – 1 AM** → dedicated **mechanism-only** ad set. No offer, no urgency, no countdown.
**2–7 AM** → suppress. The only genuinely dead window.

## 12 · Non-negotiable production rules — and the number behind each

| Rule | The measured number behind it |
|---|---|
| **Video: 20 seconds, hook inside 3, captions burned in, always** | She watches Facebook Reels in a recliner with the TV on and **the sound off** |
| **Talent must read 58–68** | She compares herself to her peer group. **A 45-year-old breaks the mirror — and G5 shows the audience saying so in your own comments** |
| **Plain beats polished** | **In this account winners look homemade and losers look like adverts** |
| **Never "75% OFF" in a headline** | Lower conv/click **in five months out of five** |
| **Lead the per-bottle anchor $29.98 → $16.66** | **Zero discount codes used across 4,770 orders.** She responds to bundle structure, not a percentage |
| **QC at 4:5 and 9:16 on a 10-inch tablet at arm's length** | **Tablet converts at 5.11% — the highest device** |
| **Ban "you" in the first five seconds** | Not a compliance rule — **a survival rule.** If the opening line can be rewritten as "I…", it should be |
| **One line per asset. Never stack quotes** | **A montage reads as a category ad and converts like one** |
| **Do not improve the customer's grammar** | *"Used to crash hard at my desk around 2.30pm/3"* is worse English and better copy |
| **Sell a six-month decision, made once** | 65% of orders are 4+ bottles. Creative selling *"try a bottle"* sells the wrong thing to the wrong person |
| **Shoot 9:16 as the master, crop out to 4:5 and 1:1 — never the reverse** | 94% of orders come from Facebook and Instagram |
