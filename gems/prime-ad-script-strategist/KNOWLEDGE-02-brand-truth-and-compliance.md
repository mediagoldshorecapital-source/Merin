## 3 · The claim ladder

### ✅ CLAIMABLE — usable in brand voice

Supports cellular energy · supports healthy NAD+ levels (**never** "restores", "boosts", "raises") · steady
energy / less fatigue (**always framed as subtraction**) · clarity and focus / "brain fog lift" · **exercise
recovery — the most defensible territory the brand has**, named by a hostile expert as the clearest benefit for
healthy people · mood / stress support · physical capacity (but **never** cite the 600mg 6-minute-walk trial —
manufacturer-funded and dismantled inside the corpus) · morning dosing guidance (instruction, not claim) · all
Bundle Math arithmetic **once G4 is reconciled** · comparative **on SPECIFICATION only** — dose, form, purity,
format, cost per gram.

### ⚠ TESTIMONIAL ONLY — attributed customer quote, never brand voice

Joint and old-injury pain · skin appearance, "glow", elasticity · sleep quality (bidirectional — pair every
mention with the timing warning) · strength / countable performance · verbal fluency and word-finding · HRV ·
*"pure speed to me without the jitter"* · *"Like super Brain!"* (far too hot for brand voice).

### 🔴 DO NOT USE — in any voice, including attributed

Anything naming a relative's diagnosis beside the product · anything implying replacement or reduction of a
medication · **perimenopause symptom relief as stated** (best-evidenced, worst-served segment — but it is a
medically-reviewed page, not an ad) · metabolic / insulin sensitivity · hair regrowth or greying reversal ·
eyesight · allergy, breathing, hangover, appetite · chronic-illness-adjacent language — **the depletion frame
reaches these people without a medical claim; never target by condition** · **"The supplement they tried to ban."**

### The verb ladder, for the writers' room

```
restores / reverses / boosts / treats / cures / prevents   →  REJECT
improves / increases                                       →  TESTIMONIAL ONLY
supports / helps maintain                                  →  CLAIMABLE
```

## 4 · 🔴 THE ABSOLUTE STOP — naming a diagnosis

The most powerful sentence in the entire body of research is:

> *"I'm terrified I'm losing my mind like my mother did."*

It is true. It is powerful. **And it must never run** — in any format, in any voice, attributed or not, in an ad,
a caption, a comment reply, or a creator's improvised line.

Why precisely: naming a relative's diagnosis beside a supplement is **two violations in one sentence** — a
disease claim (it names a disease) and an implied treatment claim (it positions the product against it). No
disclaimer repairs it. No attribution repairs it. **Putting it in a customer's mouth makes it worse**, because it
becomes a testimonial to a disease outcome.

**Use the observable moment instead:**

> *"The word just wouldn't come. Mid-sentence. In front of my daughter."*

The fear is fully present. Nothing actionable has been claimed.

| ✅ The moment | 🔴 The diagnosis |
|---|---|
| The witnessed blank | dementia |
| The chair | frailty |
| The stiffness getting out of bed | arthritis |
| The lie-down after work | chronic fatigue |
| The 3 PM crash | any metabolic condition |

## 5 · The C2 rule — second-person physiology assertions

**Both of these are true at once**, which is why it is a sign-off decision, not a copywriting one:

- Meta's personal attributes policy prohibits copy asserting or implying knowledge of a viewer's personal characteristic — age, health condition, physical state. The tell is almost always **second person + an assertion**.
- Second-person **committed** copy is the measured winning structure — **1.41 ROAS across $146,474, four of twelve all-time winners.** First-person story is the worst angle in the account — 0.83 ROAS, $69.54 CPA, $103.20 CPM. `[M]`

| Construction | Example | Exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Second-person **question** about a symptom she self-identifies | *"Tired of feeling tired as you age?"* | **LOW** — she answers internally; nothing is asserted |
| Second-person **assertion** about her physiology | *"Your NAD+ levels are plummeting!"* | **MATERIALLY HIGHER** — exactly what the policy catches |

### Both versions of every affected line

| # | ⒜ As currently running (measured winner, higher exposure) | ⒝ Assertion moved (lower exposure, default) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tired of feeling tired as you age? | unchanged — this half is low exposure |
| 2 | It's not just aging… Your NAD+ levels are plummeting! | It's not just aging… NAD+ levels plummet with age. Mine included. |
| 3 | Your brain runs on NAD, and yours is dropping | The brain burns more NAD+ than any organ — and NAD+ falls with age |
| 4 | Your skin's antioxidant defence is running out | Skin's antioxidant defence thins with age |
| 5 | You're losing NAD+ every year after 40 — restore it now | NAD+ declines with age. NMN is a building block the body uses to support healthy NAD+ levels |
| 6 | Your body stopped listening to you | My body stopped listening to me · **or** · Bodies stop listening. That's not in your head |
| 7 | Your NAD+ is collapsing. Rebuild it | NAD+ collapses. NMN supports it |
| 8 | Feeling your age at 60? Your NAD+ is depleted | I'm 62. Here's what my mornings used to look like |
| 9 | Your doctor said it's normal. Normal isn't the same as fine | A doctor once told me it was normal. Normal isn't the same as fine |
| 10 | Boost them with our NMN Complex and regain energy, focus & clarity! | Support them with our NMN Complex — for energy, focus & clarity |

**Every ⒝ preserves the second-person symptom question, the committed verb, the named mechanism, the benefit
triad and the explicit CTA.** The architecture that won survives intact. Only the assertion moves — a one-word
class of edit, not a rewrite.

**Recommended split: ⒝ on 70% of spend, ⒜ on 30%.** Read conversions per click over four weeks. Whoever signs off
on ⒜ signs off explicitly, in writing, with this table in front of them.

## 6 · The seven operational gates — what is blocked and why

These are **customer allegations made publicly, in the comment thread under a live ad, where the next buyer reads
them before she reaches the landing page.** That makes them revenue-affecting whether or not they are true.

| Gate | The allegation | Severity | What it blocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | Orders never arrive; customers publicly organising to report the page to Meta, TikTok, Instagram, the BBB and their banks | **CRITICAL** | All scaling. Any fulfilment or "we heard you" beat |
| **G2** | Ads appear to show **a competitor's bottle** (MicroIngredients) while the shop sells Prime — 22 comments, the largest single theme | **CRITICAL** | All video reuse. The **entire Trust & Purity pillar** |
| **G3** | Subscription appears forced at checkout — 97% of orders carry the flag while Subscribe & Save SKUs have sold **nine units total** | **CRITICAL** | Every **"cancel any time"** concept, permanently, until true |
| **G4** | Capsule-count gap — 120 advertised vs 30 received | HIGH | **All Bundle Math arithmetic**, all per-day pricing |
| **G5** | Celebrity association repelling the exact demographic the brand sells to | HIGH | **No spokesperson under 58 in any P0 asset** |
| **G6** | Unanswered medical eligibility questions — gastric bypass, dialysis, RA, osteoporosis, neuropathy, blood pressure, GLP-1 | HIGH | All perimenopause content; any concept that invites a medical reply |
| **G7** | Contradictory dosing guidance — customers correcting each other | MEDIUM | Partially blocks dosing assets. **Cheapest fix on the list** |

### The verbatim evidence, so you write in the right register

**G1 · fulfilment**
> *"Where's my order???????? It's been two weeks no tracking number nothing !!! Scam!!!!"*
> *"Scam. Stay away from this company. Also please report so they can get booted from Facebook, tik tok and instagram. **This products is great.** Ended up getting mine off Amazon after paying this company and never receiving my order"*

Read that fourth sentence. *"This products is great."* **She is not attacking the product. She is attacking the
fulfilment.** That distinction is the difference between a brand problem and an operations problem — and it is
stated by an angry customer, for free, in the brand's own comment section.

**G2 · the competitor's bottle**
> *"Why is the bottle different from the one the lady on the commercial is holding. Hers has the letter M at the top of the bottle and the one that would be purchased says Prime up top?"*
> *"The labels you have are BLUE in the video! The ones for sale on the shop now button are PURPLE! That a RED flag for me!"*

Customers are screenshotting the footage and zooming in on the cap.

**G3 · the subscription**
> *"I wanna order but I don't want a subscription and it says you can opt out below but I can't find the opt out selection or I would've purchased"* — a lost sale, stated in the past perfect. **She wanted to buy.**

**G4 · the capsule count**
> *"I got 6 bottles, barely enough pills in each bottle to cover the bottom, I literally dumped all six into one & there's still room in the bottle"*

Two complaint shapes: people who **counted**, and people who describe the **fill level**. The count may be correct
and the impression of abundance still offends. **Perceived deception costs more than the money did.**

**G5 · the celebrity**
> *"Because no one wants to be like the Kardashians. Be a Beth!"* — the best five words in the corpus for
> understanding this customer. She is telling you exactly who she is and who she is not.

**G7 · dosing** — and this one is an economic item, not a content item
> *"I've been taking it on an empty stomach for about 4 months now. It really keeps me energized throughout my day. I forgot to take it in the morning a few times, so I took it in the evening. I wouldn't recommend that, it led to a few sleepless nights."*

Insomnia is the #1 reported side effect in the category and the #1 cause of abandoning a product that is working.
Someone who takes it at 4 PM, sleeps badly and quits at week two **never reaches a re-bill** — and each lost
re-bill is $37.34 of contribution.

## 7 · The 60-second pre-shoot check — run on every asset, no exceptions

1. **Any disease name → DELETE.** Not soften. Delete. Including in a relative's story, a customer quote, or an OST card.
2. **cure / treat / prevent / reverse / restore / boost / clinically proven / doctor recommended** → delete or substantiate. Substantiate means the citation goes **on screen**.
3. **Every number real and current.** If you cannot cite it on screen you do not say it.
4. **Every document shown on camera is genuine.** The COA, the lab report, the batch record. **A fabricated COA is a LEGAL problem, not a compliance one** — a different category of exposure, and it is not repaired by taking the ad down.
5. **FDA disclaimer present:** *These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.*
6. **Material connection disclosed on every creator asset.** Every performance claim you echo needs its own substantiation, not the original speaker's.
7. **The competitor's bottle is not in the frame.** Until G2 closes this is **check zero**, and it is done first.
8. **No percentage discount in any headline.**
9. **Talent reads 58–68** (60–72 male for Robert).
10. **Second-person physiology assertion?** → produce both ⒜ and ⒝.

## 9 · Landmines — hard constraints on creative

**Language that gets you mocked in these threads**
- "Reverse aging" / "turn back the clock" / "fountain of youth" — explicitly ridiculed
- "Miracle" / "game-changer" / "life-changing" — reads as affiliate copy. **Their own believers say "subtle" and "nothing dramatic"**
- Biological-age reduction claims — the study behind them was dismantled in-thread as manufacturer-funded
- Blood NAD+ percentage increase as the hero claim — **the audience reads this as the tell of a weak product**
- Naming **David Sinclair** — net negative
- **Resveratrol as a hero pairing** — called "science fiction" by the man who discovered NR
- **Perfect review scores** — now actively read as evidence of fraud
- "Immediate absorption" / "faster results" / "feel the difference in days" — **manufactures the day-14 refund**
- Hedged verbs in brand voice — "naturally declines", "helps support", "may assist". **0.92 vs 1.41 ROAS**

**Structural risks**
- **Undisclosed affiliates and seeded testimonials.** This community outs them in-thread, by username. One exposure follows a brand across years of search results.
- **Condition-based targeting** — long Covid, chronic fatigue, autoimmune, arthritis, menopause-as-a-condition. Real audiences, real reported benefit, **firmly in drug-claim territory. Reach them through depletion language, never through diagnosis.**
- **Injection, IV, patch and suppository comparisons — stay out.**
- **The FDA conspiracy angle** — the breast-milk fact does the same emotional work safely.
- **Silence on side effects.** The insomnia reports are everywhere. If you don't warn people, the reviews will — and then it's a defect rather than a dosing instruction.

**The three named guardrails**
1. **Do not follow the caregiver into disease territory.** Serve that intent with gentleness, simplicity and ease-of-use messaging only. Never imply the product treats, prevents or replaces medication for any condition.
2. **Testimonials need substantiation and disclosure.** *"Results vary widely between individuals"* is not just safer here — it is truer and more persuasive than a uniform promise.
3. **"Simple" must not become "underdosed."** Make simplicity a claim about the **routine**, never about the **formula**.
