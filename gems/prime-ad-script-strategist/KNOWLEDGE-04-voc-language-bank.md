# KNOWLEDGE 04 · Voice-of-Customer Language Bank

Consolidated from 161,000 words of Reddit, 468 Meta ad comments (304 people), 189 competitor reviews across
8 brands / 17 SKUs, 110 YouTube comments, and the Triple Whale warehouse. **Every quote is verbatim.**

> **Provenance, before anything else.** Only `OWN-##` lines are Prime's own customers, and only after verifying
> the commenter placed an order. Everything else — Reddit, competitor reviews, YouTube, the "Golden" lines — is
> **research input for your own copy, never publishable social proof.** See KNOWLEDGE-02 §8.

## 1 · The six register rules

**1 · Their words are shorter and more concrete than ours.**
They don't say "afternoon energy dip," they say *"I crash hard at my desk around 2.30."* **Name the hour, not the
feeling.** *"3 PM"* outperforms "afternoon fatigue." *"Especially in bed at night"* outperforms "joint discomfort."

**2 · Benefits are described as subtraction, not addition.**
Fatigue *"washes away,"* fog *"lifts,"* slumps are *"gone."* **Almost nobody says NMN gave them energy — they say
it removed something.** Write in that direction.

**3 · Commit in brand voice. Hedge in customer voice.**
The single most-violated rule and **it is worth 53% of ROAS.** Your line says *"It's not just aging."* Her
testimonial says *"Nothing dramatic but I did notice…"* **Every top-upvoted positive review in the corpus contains
a hedge — build it in, don't edit it out.**

**4 · Benefit-as-a-behaviour-that-stopped beats benefit-as-improved-metric.**
*"I don't even take naps after work anymore."* *"I can sleep on my side again."* **A restored ability is more
believable than an improved number.**

**5 · Do not over-edit the testimonial.**
*"skin lasticity is improved"* — the typo is why it reads as real. **Polished reviews look bought.**

**6 · Write to disqualify, not to attract.**
Hook rate, hold rate and CTR all correlate **negatively** with return, in all seven months measured. Losing ads
buy 36% more clicks and convert them 39% worse. **Every line should give a non-buyer a reason to scroll past.**
This is the difference between $31.90 and $58.09 cost per acquisition.

## 3 · The fifteen golden verbatim lines

Every line is **transcription, not invention.** Flags: `T` specific time or place · `S` physical sensation ·
`F` comparison to a former self · `P` plain unbranded language · `Q` under twelve words · `E` external observation.
**The rarest flag is `F`, and the `F` lines are the hero testimonials.**

| # | The line, verbatim | Emotion | Trigger moment | Flags | ICP · use it for |

> **Two rules for using these.**
> **1 · Do not improve them.** *"Used to crash hard at my desk around 2.30pm/3"* is worse English and better copy.
> **The half-written time stamp is the credibility.**
> **2 · Do not stack them. One line per asset.** A montage of all fifteen reads as a category ad and converts like
> one. **Ten ads that each own one line beat one ad that owns fifteen.**

## 4 · The 24 crafted lines — brand-voice headlines

These commit. Use them as brand copy, not as quotes.

### Hooks & headlines
1. **"Not sick. Just… off."** — the most common entry state in the corpus and the one no competitor addresses, **because it isn't a condition.** Qualifies instantly while claiming nothing.
2. **"The 2:30 crash isn't a coffee problem."** — names a moment she can **verify today** and reframes it away from the obvious fix.
3. **"You can't get away with it like you used to."** — **accusatory in a way that flatters.**
4. **"Like running a marathon breathing through a straw."** — visual enough to carry a campaign on its own.
5. **"I do everything right. I still wake up feeling like this."** — the **injustice frame**; pre-empts the "fix your sleep first" objection before it lands.
6. **"Poor sleep. No energy. Hair falling out. Easily irritated."** — a real woman's own list, **in her own order.** ⚠ Perimenopause content is blocked on G6 until medically reviewed.
7. **"Crashing hormones."** — her phrase, not a clinician's. ⚠ Same G6 block.

### Positioning
8. **"You can't raise it above normal. You can only get back to normal."** — **the depletion model in one line.** The community's own consensus explanation, so it reads as recognition rather than persuasion.
9. **"It's not your age. It's your last ten years."** — moves the axis **from age to depletion.** Legitimately widens the market without contradicting the category's gatekeepers.
10. **"Topping up a recycling system, not adding a new one."** — sidesteps the liver-conversion objection rather than arguing with it.
11. **"One capsule. Not eleven."** — the anti-stack pillar. ⚠ Must read as a claim about the **routine**, never the formula.
12. **"Collagen rebuilds the structure. NAD+ powers the cells that build it."** — the honest cross-sell. **Collagen is the one anti-aging supplement this audience already believes in.** 🔒 Collagen SKU is do-not-promote until COGS load.

### Expectation-setting / retention — *worth more than acquisition copy in this category*
13. **"It's not caffeine. Nothing will happen on day one."** — **filters refund-seekers**, sets a survivable horizon.
14. **"Most people quit at week four. It usually shows up around week six."** — addresses the corpus's most common failure mode: **quitting just before it works.**
15. **"You'll stop feeling it. That doesn't mean it stopped working."** — pre-empts **the delta trap**, the biggest cause of churn among people for whom the product is *succeeding*.
16. **"Take it in the morning. There's a reason."** — prevents the **#1 side effect and the #1 cause of early quitting.**

### Proof & anti-placebo
17. **"Can't placebo that."** — their line, already upvoted. **Deploy only after objective evidence. Never after a feeling.**
18. **"I notice when I don't take it."** — the stop-start experiment in six words. Appears independently from at least five users, **which is why it reads as authentic.**
19. **"I didn't buy it for that."** — **the involuntary-discovery frame.** Placebo can't easily explain a benefit you weren't looking for — **and this audience knows it.**
20. **"She wouldn't have placebo effect."** — from the corpus's most-praised testimonial: a woman who dosed her limping dog before she'd try it herself. **The most powerful anti-placebo device available.** 🔒 Needs a real Prime customer with this story.

### Honesty & qualification — *counter-intuitive, and the strongest trust play available*
21. **"Fix your sleep first. Then come back."** — **agrees with the most reasonable objection in the category instead of fighting it.**
22. **"Most people should not buy this."** — then qualify: *if you're under 35, healthy, sleeping well and not drinking, you probably won't feel much.* **That paragraph would be the most trustworthy thing in the category.**
23. **"$1 a day. And here's the lab report."** — **cheap alone reads as suspicious here.** Price plus proof is the combination. **Never price alone.**
24. **"Here's the study that didn't go our way."** — **converts the biggest liability into the credibility proof.**

> 🔴 **One line never to use, however tempting: "The supplement they tried to ban."** The safe substitute with the
> same emotional payload is already in the corpus: NMN occurs naturally in **human breast milk** — *"the very first
> foods humans eat after they are born."*

## 5 · The proof hierarchy — what actually counts

This audience has an explicit, consistent evidence hierarchy. Testimonials that satisfy it get upvoted;
testimonials that don't get called placebo. The default hostile response is:

> *"Unfortunately, anecdotal evidence like this would appear even if every product available was a placebo."*

Here is what survives it, **in ascending order of power.**

| Tier | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Tracked objective metrics** | HRV from a wearable, NAD+ assay values, bloodwork. **Quoted with reverence** | *"in 6 months my HRV has gone from 91-109 average"* |
| **2 · Animals** | **The community's own placebo control, and stated as such** | *"She wouldn't have placebo effect."* (35 upvotes) |
| **3 · Involuntary discovery** | A benefit the user wasn't looking for and didn't believe in | *"I didn't buy NMN with any expectation that it would help my ankle… but 2 weeks in I noticed my foot wasn't hurting"* |
| **4 · The stop-start natural experiment** | Accidental A/B tests. **Enormously persuasive and free to prompt** | *"Stopped for 2 months. I felt tired. I felt old."* |
| **5 · Third-party observation** | Other people noticing, unprompted. Converts a feeling into an external fact | *"people have been noticing the glow in my face"* |
| **6 · Countable performance** | Numbers a reader can picture. Small, specific, checkable | *"Went from 17 chin ups to 22."* · *"I went from 9:15 to 8:15 on the mile. I'm 61."* |

**The phrase they use when they think they've cleared the bar:** *"Can't placebo that."*

> **How to manufacture this evidence at scale.** Stop asking *"how do you like it?"* Ask four questions instead:
> 1. *What changed that you weren't expecting?* → manufactures Tier 3
> 2. *What did you notice when you ran out?* → manufactures Tier 4
> 3. *Has anyone else commented?* → manufactures Tier 5
> 4. *Is there a number you track?* → manufactures Tier 1

### The trust-signal table — every proof device, ranked

| Signal | Why it lands | Status in category |
|---|---|---|
| **Lot-specific public COA** | Turns a claim into **a receipt tied to the exact bottle in their hand** | **Wide open** |
| **Expiration date on the bottle** | Its absence generated repeat one-star warnings. **A manufacture date is not an expiration date and buyers know it** | **Competitor failure** |
| **Honest dose-vs-study comparison** | Pre-empts the **fairy-dusting** accusation | **Wide open** |
| **Cost per day at the real dose** | Does the arithmetic they were going to do anyway | **Wide open** |
| **Independent rating platforms** | Reviewers quote TrustScores **to two decimal places.** An external number outranks any self-reported one | Under-used |
| **cGMP + ISO-accredited testing** | Expected. **Its absence hurts; its presence no longer differentiates** | Table stakes |
| **Clean excipients** | No dyes, colours or fillers; rice flour not wheat; no piperine | Cheap win |
| **Frictionless returns** | Two brands refused returns and it appears verbatim in their worst reviews. **The 30-day guarantee belongs above the fold, not in the footer** | **Prime advantage** |

## 7 · Words to retire

| Retire | Why |
|---|---|
| "Immediate absorption" | Explicitly called out as misleading on a competitor page |
| "Faster results" | Sets a clock you will lose |
| **"Feel the difference in days"** | **Manufactures the day-14 refund** |
| "Reverse aging" / "turn back the clock" | Triggers the hype allergy **and the regulator** |
| "Clinically proven" (unqualified) | They know the meta-analysis |
| "Extra strength" without the number | **Read as concealment** |
| **"naturally declines" / "helps support" / "may assist"** | Hedged into meaninglessness. **0.92 vs 1.41 ROAS** |
| "75% OFF TODAY ONLY" | Lost on conversions-per-click **in five months out of five** |
| "Miracle" / "game-changer" / "life-changing" | Reads as affiliate copy. **Their own believers say "subtle"** |

## 8 · The natural tone, described

**Informal, unpunctuated, typo-tolerant, mid-thought.** Sentences run on. Ellipses and a trailing *"Geesh.."* do
real emotional work. **She writes the way she'd say it to a friend in a kitchen.**

**Concrete beats abstract every time.** Not "afternoon energy dip" — *"Used to crash hard at my desk around
2.30pm/3."* **The specificity is what makes a line feel observed rather than written.**

**Four dominant emotional notes, in order:** **grief** for the person she used to be · **anger** at the unfairness
of it · **shame** at possibly being gullible · **guarded hope**.

**The tell is almost always injustice, not decline:** *"my body ached when it should not have"*, *"I should not
feel like this."* **She hasn't accepted this, which is exactly why she's still shopping.**
