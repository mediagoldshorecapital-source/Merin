wrong. Two of them measure *your account*. Four measure *the category*. Three are strategy or
research layered on top. One is a generic operating manual.

## The precedence rule

When two sources disagree, this is the order. It is the single most important instruction in the
whole synthesis, because several of these documents contradict each other on load-bearing points.

1. **S1 / S2 — your own ads data.** What returns in *this* account, on 1,220 ads and $381K.
   Nothing outranks it on the question "will this ad work here."
2. **S3 — the warehouse + comment corpus.** Highest authority on who buys, what they pay, what
   they repeat, and what they are saying in public right now.
3. **S5–S9 — the category corpora.** Excellent for *language*, *objections* and *mental models*.
   They are **not** evidence about your account's performance, and S8 in particular is competitors'
   customers — research input, never social proof you can publish.
4. **The May 20 ICP report**, where S1/S2/S3 have not already corrected it.
5. **S10's benchmarks.** Its *process* — phases, Concept IDs, gap analysis, iteration ladder,
   rituals — is sound and should be followed. Its *numeric thresholds* are generic category
   defaults and are superseded by S1/S2 wherever they conflict.

## The four conflicts that must be resolved explicitly

These are not ambiguities. They are documents flatly disagreeing, and each one changes what you
build. The synthesis document must name each, state which side wins, and say why.

### Conflict 1 — How to judge an ad *(the big one)*

| S10 says | S1/S2 measured |
|----------|----------------|
| Judge on hook rate (<20% kill / >35% strong), hold rate, CTR | Hook rate correlates **negatively** with ROAS in **all seven months**. Never once positive |
| Diagnostic tree: first bad engagement column is the problem | Winners: CTR 5.29%, conv/click 4.26%, CPA $31.90. Losers: CTR 7.18%, conv/click 2.61%, CPA $58.09 |
| — | Losers buy **36% more clicks and convert them 39% worse** |
| Kill at $85 with zero purchases | Judge on **conversions per click** after ~40 clicks (≈$60): ≥4.0% scale · 3.0–4.0% hold · <2.8% kill |

**Resolution: S1/S2 win outright.** Running S10's thresholds in this account would systematically
promote the losing ads — they are the ones that look best on engagement. Keep S10's *diagnostic
logic* (isolate which stage failed) but rebuild the column set around conversions per click.

Read from **Meta**, not Triple Whale: the Triple Whale checkout event fires on only ~63% of
purchases.

### Conflict 2 — Second person vs first person

| S10 says | S1 measured |
|----------|-------------|
| Meta prohibits copy implying knowledge of the viewer's health. Rewrite everything to first person: *"I was tired in a way sleep didn't fix."* | Second-person committed copy is the **winning** structure: *"Tired of feeling tired as you age? Your NAD+ levels are plummeting!"* — 1.41 ROAS, 4 of 12 all-time winners |
| — | First-person story (Angle D) is the **worst** angle in the account: 0.83 ROAS, $69.54 CPA |

**Resolution: this is a risk decision, not a data question — escalate it, do not silently pick.**
S10 is describing rejection *risk*; S1 is describing *return*. Both are true. The account is
currently running the risky construction and winning with it. What the synthesis must produce is a
stated risk position: how much rejection exposure the business will accept for a 53% ROAS
difference, and which specific lines sit closest to the line.

Note the two are separable in practice. *"Tired of feeling tired as you age?"* is a second-person
question about a **symptom the reader self-identifies**. *"Your NAD+ levels are plummeting"* is a
second-person **assertion about the reader's physiology** — a different and higher exposure. You can
keep the winning architecture and move only the assertion into the safer register.

### Conflict 3 — Hedged vs committed language

Not really a conflict, but the highest-value single finding across all ten documents, and it points
the opposite way to what the category corpora suggest.

- **S1:** the hedged version of the identical insight (*"naturally drop… helps support"*) returned
  **0.92 ROAS across 436 ads and zero winners**. The committed version (*"are plummeting… boost
  them"*) returned **1.41** across $146K. Same insight, same mechanism, same audience, **53% apart**.
- **S5/S6:** the Reddit corpus says this category has a hype allergy and that *restraint is a
  performance advantage* — every claim you don't make buys credibility for the ones you do.

**Resolution: both, in different places.** Commit in the ad; hedge in the proof. The ad's job is to
qualify, and hedging is what stops it qualifying. The landing page, PDP and post-purchase flow are
where honesty about the evidence buys retention. Do not import Reddit's register into the hook.

### Conflict 4 — Timing

| S10 / ICP report says | S2 measured (1 May – 7 Aug, spend-controlled) |
|---|---|
| Launch Thursday. Never Monday — 22% below Saturday | **Friday best** (1.25 ROAS, $48.47 CPA). **Tuesday worst** (0.99, $61.26). Saturday mid-pack. **Monday is fine** (1.06) |
| 11am is the single best hour | 7 PM peak (296 orders), 6 PM (291); 11 AM fifth. Curve flat 8 AM–midnight; only 2–7 AM is dead |

**Resolution: S2 wins.** The old ranking used raw order counts, which track your budget schedule
rather than her behaviour — Sunday only looks big because it is your biggest spend day, and it
returns 1.05. Launch **Wednesday or Thursday** so creative exits learning by Friday. Cut Tuesday
hardest. Reduce Sunday.

## The economics everything sits inside

State these at the top of the synthesis. They change which recommendations are even legal to make.

- Blended ROAS **1.29** against a breakeven of **~1.70**. You lose money on first order and recover
  it on re-bills.
- Acquisition: **−$21.95** per subscriber. Re-bill: **+$37.34**. Breakeven is **0.59 re-bills per
  subscriber** — that single number is what the whole retention system exists to protect.
- Robert at **1.81 ROAS** is, per the research, the only segment above breakeven on first order.
  Unverified in the warehouse — confirm in Ads Manager before moving budget.
- Revenue mix: NMN **94%** ($292,955 Jun–7 Aug) · ASTA **4%** · Multi Collagen and Calcium Balm
  negligible and margin-unknown.
- Run rate ~**$118K/month**. $1M cumulative over six months needs **$167K/month** (+41%);
  over three months, **$333K/month** (+183%) — which can only be bought with spend, at a loss.
- Refund rate **2.85%** — she keeps what she buys.
