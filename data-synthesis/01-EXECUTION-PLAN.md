## The frame: you do not have a creative problem first

Blended ROAS is **1.29** against a breakeven of **~1.70**. You lose **$21.95** acquiring a
subscriber and make **$37.34** every time they re-bill. Breakeven is **0.59 re-bills per
subscriber**.

That single number reorders the whole plan. Better ads widen the funnel, but the business is won or
lost on whether people stay to the second bill — and the system meant to keep them there is
currently not running. Klaviyo sends Triple Whale no engagement data at all and attributed **$1,028
in July** against **$128,676** of revenue. For a subscription supplement brand, email should be
20–30% of revenue: roughly **$25–35K a month** uncollected, on a list you already own, at near-zero
marginal cost.

**Fixing the Klaviyo integration is the highest-ROI task in this document, and it is not a creative
task.**

## Measurement — the part that inverts

This is the single most counter-intuitive section, and getting it wrong costs more than every other
decision combined.

**Across all seven months measured, hook rate correlates *negatively* with ROAS. Never once
positive.**

| Tier | Ads | ROAS | Hook | Hold | CTR | **Conv/click** | **CPA** |
|------|-----|------|------|------|-----|----------------|---------|
| Winners | 12 | 1.98 | 32.1% | 31.3% | 5.29% | **4.26%** | **$31.90** |
| Marginal | 30 | 1.41 | 36.7% | 36.0% | 5.72% | 3.49% | $43.57 |
| Losers | 26 | 1.00 | 44.9% | 39.6% | 7.18% | **2.61%** | **$58.09** |

Losers buy **36% more clicks and convert them 39% worse**. Every engagement metric moves the wrong
way. The best single ad in six months — 2.68 ROAS, $24.18 CPA — has a **3.04% CTR**, roughly half
the account average, and converts **5.57%** of the clicks it does buy.

**So: judge on conversions per click.**

```
≥ 4.0%    → scale
3.0–4.0%  → hold and iterate
< 2.8%    → kill
```

Decide after ~**40 clicks (≈$60** at a $1.50 CPC). Read it from **Meta** — your Triple Whale checkout
event fires on only about **63%** of purchases.

Engagement metrics still earn a place, but only as **diagnostics**: they tell you *which stage* of
the ad failed once you already know it failed. A high hook rate beside a low conversion rate is a
warning, not a win. If you take one thing from this plan, take that sentence.

**The honest caveats**, so you know where the rule is soft: these are Meta-reported conversions, not
Triple Whale attribution, so they rank ads against each other and do not set an absolute profit bar.
Eleven of the twelve winners launched February–April when the whole account performed better, so
account decay is mixed into the split — which is why the correlation was re-tested *within* each
month, where decay cancels. And two small-spend winners break the pattern with high CTR. The rule is
strong, not absolute; treat it as a spend gate, not a creative veto.

## The copy rule worth more than any targeting change

The hedged and committed versions of the *same insight* ran side by side:

| | Copy | Ads | Spend | ROAS | CPA |
|---|------|-----|-------|------|-----|
| **Won** | *"Tired of feeling tired as you age? It's not just aging... Your NAD+ levels are plummeting! Boost them with our NMN Complex and regain energy, focus & clarity! Discover how →"* | 146 | $146,474 | **1.41** | $44.36 |
| **Lost** | *"Feeling more tired as you age? It's not 'just aging.' Your NAD+ levels naturally drop — the molecule your cells need... Our NMN Complex helps support those levels..."* | 436 | $54,582 | **0.92** | $67.72 |

Same insight, same mechanism, same audience. **53% apart.** The loser produced **zero winners and
nine losers** — and survived because its CTR (5.48%) is indistinguishable from the winners' (5.50%).
It looks fine on engagement and fails on revenue. That is the entire thesis of this plan in one row.

Commit in the ad. Hedge in the proof — the landing page and post-purchase flow are where honesty
about the evidence buys retention, and the category corpora are emphatic that overclaiming there
costs you credibility.

**Also drop "75% OFF TODAY ONLY" from headlines.** Lower conversions per click in five months out of
five. You are shouting a 75% discount at people already being charged a permanent ~35% one, then
paying for the unqualified clicks it buys. Lead with the per-bottle anchor: **$29.98 → $16.66**.

## Rituals

**Weekly — 60 minutes, Thursday** (decide and ship in the same session, ahead of Friday).

| Min | Step |
|-----|------|
| 0–10 | Update the scorecard: spend, **conversions per click**, CPA by concept |
| 10–25 | Diagnose every underperformer — which stage failed, and the fix code |
| 25–35 | Kill anything under 2.8% conv/click after 40+ clicks |
| 35–45 | Scale +20–30% on anything ≥4.0% |
| 45–55 | Brief three iterations of the week's best concept |
| 55–60 | Mine five new quotes from this week's ad comments |

That last ten minutes is the compounding step. Your ad comments are a free, continuously refreshing
source of the exact objections your current, colder audience holds — and, as the dossier showed, the
place where operational problems surface long before they reach the revenue line. Most teams never
read them.

**Monthly — 2 hours.** Four pivots: spend / CPA / conv-per-click by pillar, by awareness level, by
format, by creator. All four are one filter away *if* the naming convention is fixed; none are
answerable if it isn't. Then re-run the Gap Analysis, re-rank the seven angles on current data,
refresh the Objection Ledger, and recalculate the offer math against the 1.70 breakeven.

## What actually determines whether this works

**Eight new concepts a month.** At a ~1-in-6 hit rate that is 1.3 new winners per month — just enough
to outpace fatigue. At three a month you ship half a winner and the account declines regardless of
how good the strategy document is.

If capacity is short: cut iterations before net-new concepts, shift toward static and VO formats that
need no creator booking, and cut Wave 3 depth before Wave 1 gap-fillers. **Never cut the three Wave 1
priorities.**

## The four failure modes

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| No pipeline | Same four ads carrying 70%+ of spend; everything feels like "creative fatigue" | Testing under 20%. Get to eight concepts a month |
| Volume without strategy | Twenty new ads a month, hit rate under 1-in-12 | Skipping research. Every ad fills a named grid cell |
| Winner abandonment | Winners found, never iterated | Run the ladder. 8–15 assets per win |
| **Optimising for attention** | Engagement dashboards look great, ROAS falls | **The one this account is actually exposed to.** Losing ads are the ones people like most. Judge on conversions per click |
