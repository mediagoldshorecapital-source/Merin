# 10 — Iteration & Scaling: How One Winner Becomes Twelve

Most beginners find a winner, ride it until it dies, and then panic. Professionals treat a winner as a **template** and immediately manufacture a family from it.

---

## Part A — The winner multiplication protocol

The moment a creative hits 🟢 on the scorecard, run all seven of these within 7 days. Every one is cheap because the hard part — proving the concept — is already done.

### 1. Hook swaps (highest value, lowest cost)
Keep the body and CTA identical. Attach 3–4 different hooks from a *different archetype*. If your winner used H2 (swap this for that), test H7 (confession), H11 (counter-intuitive question), and H36 (visual multiples) on the same body.
**Why it works:** hooks fatigue far faster than bodies. Swapping the first 3 seconds resets the audience without rebuilding the ad.

### 2. Avatar port
Linda winner → build the Robert version and the Professional version. Same structure, same script skeleton, different creator, different specifics (grandkids → recovery time → workday fog).
**Robert first, always.** He converts at 1.81 ROAS — the most efficient demographic in the account — and is underweighted in spend.

### 3. Format port
- UGC winner → make a static of its single strongest line (layout S-8)
- UGC winner → make a review-screenshot static of its best unscripted moment (S-5)
- Static winner → make a 15s video where the static's headline is the spoken hook
- 20s winner → make a 45–60s VSSL version that adds the mechanism section

### 4. Length variants
15s cutdown (hook + product + CTA), 20s original, 45s extended. Different placements and different scroll moods reward different lengths.

### 5. Ratio variants
Every survivor in both 9:16 and 4:5, built natively. Tablet is your highest-converting device at 5.11% and Feed 4:5 is where tablets live.

### 6. CTA variants
Same ad, three endings: bundle-led ("$16.66 a bottle"), guarantee-led ("90 days to change your mind"), urgency-led ("while the six-bottle bundle is on"). CTA swaps are ten minutes of editing and routinely move CTR 15–30%.

### 7. Awareness ladder
Take a ToF winner and build the MoF and BoF versions of the *same idea*. If "the 3 PM crash" wins cold, then a 3 PM-crash review compilation and a 3 PM-crash offer static should be in retargeting.

**Output: one winner → 12–15 new assets.** This is why professional accounts appear to produce endless creative. They aren't inventing more; they're multiplying better.

## Part B — Diagnosing and fixing a fatiguing winner

### The signals, in the order they appear

```
Week 1–2   Hook rate at peak, CPA at target        → scale
Week 3–4   Hook rate begins drifting down          → build variants NOW
Week 5     Frequency > 2.5, CPA +20%               → refresh the hook
Week 6+    Hook rate 25%+ below peak, CPA +40%     → retire it
```

**Act at week 3, not week 6.** By the time CPA visibly degrades, you've already lost two weeks of efficiency. Hook rate decay is the early-warning system — it moves before CPA does because it has thousands of data points instead of a dozen.

### Fatigue vs. a bad ad — telling them apart

| Symptom | Fatigue | Genuinely bad creative |
|---|---|---|
| Started strong, declined | ✅ | ❌ |
| Frequency > 2.5 | ✅ | usually not |
| Hook rate fell but CTR *of those who watch* held | ✅ | ❌ |
| Never performed at any point | ❌ | ✅ |

**Fatigue is fixed with a new hook on the same body.** A bad creative is fixed by throwing it away. Confusing the two is expensive in both directions.

## Part C — The creative fatigue calendar

Plan against decay instead of reacting to it.

```
MONTH 1   Launch 8–12 concepts. Expect 2–3 winners (a ~20–25% win rate is healthy).
MONTH 2   Scale the winners. Launch 8–12 new concepts.
          Multiply winner #1 into 12 variants.
MONTH 3   Winner #1 begins fatiguing → its variants are already running and take over.
          Launch 8–12 new concepts.
          Multiply winner #2.
```

**The rule:** you must always have the *next* winner in test before the current one fatigues. That's why testing budget is permanently 15–25% of spend, even when things are going well. Especially when things are going well.

## Part D — Post ID consolidation (free social proof)

When you duplicate an ad, Meta creates a new post — and it starts at zero likes, zero comments, zero shares. You've thrown away your social proof.

Instead, **use the existing post ID**:
1. Find the winning ad's post ID: Ads Manager → the ad → Preview → open the post → the ID is in the URL, or use the Facebook Page's Ad Posts view.
2. When creating the new ad, choose **"Use existing post"** and paste the post ID.
3. All duplicates now accumulate likes, comments, and shares on the *same* post.

**Why this matters enormously for this buyer.** Linda has been scammed before. An ad with 400 comments and 1,200 reactions reads as *legitimate*; the identical ad with 3 reactions reads as *suspicious.* Social proof is not a vanity metric for a sceptical 62-year-old — it's the trust signal that gets her to tap.

**And moderate those comments daily.** One unanswered "this is a scam" comment sitting at the top of a high-spend ad will cost you more than the ad costs to run. Reply publicly, factually, without defensiveness. Hide spam and MLM recruiters.

## Part E — The retention layer (where the real money is)

The customer intelligence contains the most valuable finding in either document, and it isn't about creative at all:

> Repeat rate: **1.0% (Jan) → 2.4% → 3.0% → 3.9% → 7.8% (May)** — doubling monthly.
> Natural reorder window: **90–120 days.** VIPs spend $260–$424 across 3–7 orders.
> And the one-page card is explicit: *"If you email her at Day 75, she'll reorder a 6-bottle bundle for $106. If you don't, she'll forget and buy from someone else."*

**Every dollar of CAC you're paying is worth substantially more if the reorder flow exists.** Build it before you scale spend, not after.

### The reorder sequence (Klaviyo)

| Day | Channel | Message |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Email | "Here's how to take it, and when to expect something" — **set the week-three expectation explicitly.** This single email prevents the week-two "it's not working" refund. |
| 21 | Email | "Around now is when most people notice something. What are you seeing?" — reply-driven; harvests testimonials |
| 60 | Email | Education: the mechanism, the research, why consistency matters |
| **75** | **Email + SMS** | **"How are you feeling? Running low?"** — the highest-value message in the entire business |
| 85 | Email | "Reorder and get 15% off" |
| 95 | Email + Meta retargeting ad | "Don't lose your momentum — your supply runs out this week" |
| 110 | Meta retargeting | Win-back creative for lapsed 90–180 day purchasers |

### Retargeting ad sets to build

| Ad set | Audience | Creative |
|---|---|---|
| **Reorder 75–120d** | Purchasers 75–120 days ago | "Running low?" + B3G3 upgrade |
| **Win-back 120–180d** | Purchasers 120–180 days ago | "Here's what happens when you stop" |
| **Cross-sell ASTA** | NMN purchasers 30d+ | "You handled the energy. Now the mirror." — ASTA is your only meaningful second product at $12.5K |

**The cross-sell is under-exploited.** The intelligence report is explicit: *"She buys NMN for energy, then adds ASTA for the mirror."* That's a stated, evidenced sequence and almost no creative is being built for it.

## Part G — What to do when nothing is working

If you've run 10 creatives and none hit 🟢, work through this in order. Do not just make more ads.

1. **Check tracking first.** Is the Pixel firing? Is CAPI live? Is Purchase deduplicating? A tracking failure looks exactly like a creative failure and wastes months.
2. **Check the landing page.** Site CVR should be 4.2–4.4%. If it's below 2%, the problem is the page, not the ad, and no creative will save it.
3. **Check hook rates.** If *every* creative is under 20%, your hooks are too generic. Go back to `02-research-sop.md` and collect 100 more verbatim quotes. You are writing from imagination.
4. **Check your creative diversity.** Are your 10 "different" ads actually 10 versions of one idea? Count the hook archetypes. If you used two, that's the problem.
5. **Check the offer against competitors** in the Ad Library. Is B3G3 still competitive?
6. **Check spend depth.** Did each creative genuinely get ~$350 over 7 days, or did you spread $200 across ten ads and learn nothing about any of them?
7. **Only then** conclude the creative is wrong.

In that order. Nearly every "our ads stopped working" emergency is number 1 or number 6.
