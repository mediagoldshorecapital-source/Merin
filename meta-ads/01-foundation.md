## Part A — The seven laws

These are distilled from the three teachers in the source deck. They are not opinions; they are the constraints your creative has to satisfy.

### Law 1 — The creative *is* the targeting

Meta's modern delivery system (Andromeda) reads your video transcript, your on-screen text, and your visual cues, and uses them to find the person the ad is *about*. You no longer choose the audience with interest checkboxes — **you choose it with your first three seconds.**

Practical consequence: a hook that says *"If you're 62 and can't get up off the floor with your grandkids"* is a targeting instruction. A hook that says *"Feel your best!"* is a targeting instruction that means "show this to nobody in particular." Vague creative gets vague delivery.

> **Rule:** Every creative must be aimed at exactly one person having exactly one bad moment. If you can't name the moment and the hour it happens, the concept isn't ready.

### Law 2 — Human psychology converts; machine learning only delivers

The algorithm places the ad. It cannot make someone care. Every winning creative does three things in order:

1. **Attention** — a visual *and* an audible hook inside 3 seconds. Both, not one.
2. **Curiosity + emotion** — open a loop, show vulnerability, build rapport.
3. **Clarity over aesthetics** — a clean direct-response message beats a glossy studio commercial every single time.

### Law 3 — Concepts are extracted, never invented

Never open a blank doc and "think of an angle." Every angle must be lifted from something a real person actually wrote or said: Reddit threads, Amazon reviews, your own site reviews, your ad comments. See `02-research-sop.md`. Linda's exact words outperform your paraphrase of Linda's words, always.

### Law 4 — Match the format to her awareness level

| Awareness | She is thinking | Formats that work | Job of the ad |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top of funnel** — problem-aware / unaware | *"Why am I so tired all the time?"* | Educational UGC, street interview, founder story, VSSL (visual-sound-script-loop), listicle | Name the problem she can't name |
| **Middle of funnel** — solution-aware | *"Does NMN actually work? Is this one real?"* | Creator testimonial, 60-day review, product demo, podcast clip, mashup, mechanism explainer | Prove the mechanism and prove *you* |
| **Bottom of funnel** — purchase-ready | *"Is this worth $100? Can I get my money back?"* | High-contrast static, review compilation, offer/urgency graphic, comparison table | Remove price and risk friction |

A bottom-of-funnel offer graphic shown to an unaware scroller is wasted spend. A top-of-funnel education video shown to someone with the product in cart is a lost sale.

### Law 5 — The six psychological levers

Every strong creative pulls at least one. The best pull two.

1. **Taboo & vulnerability** — say the thing she thinks but doesn't say out loud. Not shock value; *recognition*. ("I stopped getting on the floor with my grandson because I wasn't sure I could get back up.")
2. **Selfish desire + permission structure** — give her a rational reason to justify an emotional purchase. Cost-per-day math. "Health investment," not "spending."
3. **Armchair investigation / drama** — investigative comparisons and fairness framing that make people argue in the comments. Comments are free reach.
4. **Extreme "prove it" demonstration** — undeniable visual proof of a claim inside 60 seconds. Lab certificate on camera. Purity test. Side-by-side.
5. **Us vs. them + visual multiples** — 1 bottle vs. a pile of 6. The brain processes quantity instantly, before it processes words.
6. **Credible explainer** — a doctor, scientist, pharmacist, or founder in their natural environment, stating the solution plainly.

### Law 6 — The three-part script block

```
[0–3s]   TARGETED HOOK        visual + audible, names the person and the moment
[3–16s]  BODY                 relatable journey → mechanism → proof
[16–20s] CTA + EXPECTATIONS   what to do, what she gets, what happens if it doesn't work
```

Twenty seconds is the default for this buyer's evening scroll. Educational VSSLs may run 45–90s when the story earns it — but only after a 20s version has proven the hook.

### Law 7 — Speed beats polish

Film with real 55–65-year-old creators in real sunlit kitchens and backyards. Do not over-edit. Do not use cheap template transitions. The thing that makes an ad feel trustworthy to a 62-year-old woman is that it looks like her neighbour filmed it, not like an agency did.

**Volume target:** 8–12 *new concepts* per month, each in 3 variants ≈ 24–36 assets/month. Below that, you cannot learn fast enough to beat creative fatigue.

## Part B — The three avatars

The single biggest mistake a beginner makes is writing one ad for "women 48–65." That audience does not exist as a person. Split it.

### Avatar A — **Linda** (primary · ~60% of budget)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 58–68, peak 62 |
| **Who** | Retired or semi-retired. Former teacher, admin, nurse, small-business owner. Husband around 66. Two adult children, three-to-four grandchildren. |
| **Where** | Sunbelt / retirement corridors. CA (588 orders), FL (396), TX (391), NY (219), IL (167), OH (162), GA (162), AZ (157). Cities: LA, Phoenix, Houston, San Antonio, Las Vegas, Tucson, San Diego, Miami. **Arizona is the premium market** — Tucson AOV $82, Phoenix $61. |
| **Device** | iPhone in hand, iPad in the chair. Tablet converts highest at **5.11%**; mobile 4.21%; desktop a dismal 1.86%. |
| **Digital fingerprint** | ~40% legacy email (AOL, Yahoo, Comcast, Juno). Adopted email 25 years ago and never switched. Loyal, habitual, not an early adopter. |
| **When she buys** | **11 AM EST is the single best hour (299 orders/hr).** 7 PM EST second (293/hr). Saturday is the best day (742); Monday the worst (575, −22%). |
| **When she hurts** | 6:30 AM (wakes unrested after 8 hours) and 2–4 PM (the crash — and she's ordering 229–252/hr right through it) |
| **What she buys** | Bundles. B3G3 (6 bottles, ~$100–106) is ~40% of orders. Single bottles only ~10%. She's not buying a supplement; she's buying a 3–6 month commitment. |

**Her top 5 emotional drivers, in her words:**

1. *"I'm becoming invisible."* — She used to run everything. Now she sits while others do. Her body is betraying the person she still believes she is.
2. *"Am I losing my mind?"* — She watched her mother or aunt decline. Every forgotten name is a cold wave of fear.
3. *"I can't keep up with my grandchildren."* — Not fitness. **Purpose.** She wants to be the grandmother who gets on the floor.
4. *"My body stopped listening to me."* — Menopause as betrayal. Her doctor says "it's normal." Normal doesn't make it less terrifying.
5. *"I've been scammed before."* — Collagen, CoQ10, turmeric. She's ashamed of how gullible she was. **She is actively reading your ad looking for a reason not to buy.**

**Also live in her head:** *"I can't afford to waste money"* (fixed income, every dollar budgeted), *"I'm falling behind my friends"* (the walking group comparison), *"My husband doesn't understand"* (nobody validates that this is real), *"I'm running out of time."*

**The single most important insight in the whole dataset:** she is ordering between 2 and 5 PM at 229–252 orders/hour — *while she is physically experiencing the crash your product addresses.* Creative that names the 3 PM crash, delivered in the afternoon, is talking to someone living the pain in real time.

### Avatar B — **The Perimenopause Professional** (~25% of budget)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 48–57 |
| **Context** | Still working. Full-time or part-time. Managing a household and possibly ageing parents. |
| **The moment** | Mid-afternoon brain fog *in the middle of a workday*, in front of colleagues. Hormonal crashes. Weight that won't move. |
| **Fear** | Being seen as slipping. Losing her professional edge. |
| **What shifts** | Work stamina, mental clarity, staying sharp in meetings — not grandchildren. Don't sell her the grandkid angle; she doesn't have them yet. |
| **Tone** | Slightly faster, more competent, less cosy. She's not in a recliner; she's in a car park between errands. |

### Avatar C — **Robert** (~15% of budget · highest efficiency)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 60–72 |
| **Who** | Retired professional, veteran, or tradesman. Gmail/iCloud, not AOL. Same cities as Linda. |
| **Economics** | **ROAS 1.81 — the most efficient demographic in the account.** Higher AOV; buys $100+ bundles more often. Underweighted relative to performance. |
| **Core insecurity** | *"I'm becoming my father."* He watched his dad slow down, lose his edge, become dependent. He refuses. |
| **Motivation** | Recovery time, cognitive sharpness, physical performance, joint mobility, staying independent |
| **Tone** | Direct, unsentimental, evidence-forward. No emotional storytelling. Mechanism, dose, proof, done. Male creators 60–72, or a male doctor. |

> **Budget note:** Robert converts at 1.81 ROAS but is a minority of spend. Running dedicated Robert ad sets is the highest-confidence, lowest-effort win available in this account. Do it in week 2.

## Part C — The offer and the economics

### The bundle ladder

| Bundle | Bottles | Total | Per bottle | Per day | % of orders |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single | 1 | ~$30–38 | ~$30 | ~$1.00 | ~10% |
| Buy 1 Get 1 Free | 2 | ~$47–49 | ~$24 | ~$0.80 | ~20% |
| Buy 2 Get 2 Free | 4 | ~$82–86 | ~$21 | ~$0.70 | ~30% |
| **Buy 3 Get 3 Free** | **6** | **~$100–106** | **~$16.66** | **~$0.55** | **~40%** |

Anchor price: **$29.98/bottle.** Effective price on B3G3: **$16.66.** The math does the selling.

**Critical:** there are **no discount codes** — zero used across 4,770 orders. The "75% off" *is* the bundle structure. Never build a creative around "use code SAVE20." She doesn't hunt for codes; she responds to the anchor-vs-effective spread.

**The bundle is a trust signal disguised as a discount.** Her subconscious logic: *if this were fake, why would they give away three free bottles?* Frame it that way in bottom-of-funnel copy.

### The permission-structure phrases (use these verbatim)

- "$16.66 a bottle."
- "Fifty-five cents a day."
- "Six months of cellular energy for under $100."
- "Less than you spend on one coffee a week."
- "Three months from now you'll either feel different, or you'll have your money back."

### Blended economics you're working against

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Blended AOV | **$56–57** |
| NMN share of revenue | 96% ($494,802 / 5,366 orders) |
| Facebook CVR | 4.37% |
| Instagram CVR | 4.40% |
| Tablet CVR | 5.11% (highest) |
| Mobile CVR | 4.21% |
| Desktop CVR | 1.86% |
| Effective discount rate (stable since Feb) | 33–37% — she pays ~65¢ on the dollar |
| Best-demographic ROAS (Robert) | 1.81 |
| Repeat rate trajectory | 1.0% Jan → 7.8% May, doubling monthly |
| Natural reorder window | **90–120 days** |

### Your target numbers

Fill in your true COGS before launch. The formula:

```
Contribution margin per order  =  AOV × (1 − discount%) − COGS − shipping − payment fees
Breakeven ROAS                 =  AOV ÷ Contribution margin per order
Target CPA                     =  AOV ÷ Target ROAS
```

**Worked example** (replace with your real numbers):
At $56 AOV and 65% contribution margin after all costs → contribution ≈ $36 → breakeven ROAS ≈ 1.55.
Set a **target blended ROAS of 1.8–2.0**, which means a **target CPA of $28–$31**.

> **The number that changes everything:** repeat rate is doubling monthly and the reorder window is 90–120 days. If you have a Day-75 reorder flow live, your *real* allowable CPA is meaningfully higher than the first-order math suggests. Build the flow, then buy more aggressively. Without the flow, hold the line at first-order breakeven.

## Part D — Creative principles specific to a 62-year-old buyer

These are not generic best practices. They are consequences of who she is.

1. **Type must be legible at arm's length on an iPad.** Large, bold sans-serif. White on dark bars or heavy dark on light. Never thin weights, never low-contrast, never small caption text as the primary message.
2. **Captions always on, burned in.** She scrolls with sound off in the morning and sound on in the evening chair. The ad must work both ways.
3. **9:16 for Reels/Stories, 4:5 for Feed. Build natively in both.** Never letterbox a 16:9 video into a vertical slot.
4. **Warm, sunlit, real.** Sunbelt kitchens, backyards, garden, morning coffee. Not cold clinical stock. Not a white studio cyclorama.
5. **Cast to match.** Women 55–65 who look like her friends, not like models. Grey hair is an asset, not a problem.
6. **Pace slower than TikTok convention.** No 0.4-second jump cuts. She'll disengage from visual chaos. Hold shots 2–3 seconds.
7. **Apple Pay callout at the CTA.** She checks out in 4 minutes on an iPhone. Reduce every step.
8. **Trust furniture on screen by second 12** at the latest: 1000mg dose, third-party tested, review count, 90-day guarantee.
9. **Never condescend.** She is sharp. She was a teacher. Ads that talk to "seniors" like they're fragile insult her. Talk to her like a capable person having a specific problem.
10. **Say the hard thing.** The taboo lever is the strongest one available with this buyer, because nobody — not her doctor, not her husband — is validating that what she's experiencing is real.
