# 07 — Production SOP: The Factory Floor

Fraser Cottrell's rule governs everything here: **speed beats polish.** Your competitor is not a film studio. Your competitor is the next post in her feed.

The goal of this file is a repeatable process that turns 6 scripts into 24 finished assets in one filming day.

---

## Part A — Casting

### The brief

| Avatar | Cast | Absolutely not |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Linda** | Women 55–68. Grey or greying hair welcome. Real bodies. Warm, unhurried, sounds like a neighbour. | Models. Anyone under 50 playing older. Anyone with a "presenter" voice. |
| **B — Professional** | Women 48–56. Put-together but not glossy. Slightly faster energy. | Corporate stock-photo polish. |
| **C — Robert** | Men 60–72. Retired professional, veteran, tradesman look. Weathered hands are an asset. | Fitness models. Anyone who looks like they've never worked. |
| **Authority** | Genuinely credentialled pharmacist, RD, nutritionist, or physician. | **Anyone in a white coat who isn't one.** Non-negotiable. |

### Where to find them

1. **Your own customers.** Best source by a distance. Email the 5,128 reviewers: *"We'd love to feature your story — $200 and a year's supply for 30 minutes on video."* People who genuinely use it are unbeatable on camera.
2. **UGC creator marketplaces** — Billo, Insense, TwentyThree, Backstage, or the Meta Creator Marketplace. Filter hard on age; most platforms skew 25–35, so you'll need to search deliberately.
3. **Local:** community theatre groups, senior centres, church groups, retirement-community noticeboards in Phoenix, Tucson, Scottsdale, Las Vegas.
4. **Facebook groups** for over-55 communities in your Sunbelt cities.

### Rate guide

| | Typical range |
|---|---|
| UGC creator, 3 videos, unlimited paid usage | $150–$400 |
| Customer testimonial (your own buyer) | $150–$250 + product |
| Credentialled expert on camera | $500–$1,500 |
| Street interview day (8 interviews) | $800–$1,500 all in |

### Usage rights — get this in writing every time

Your creator agreement must include: **perpetual, worldwide paid-media usage rights across Meta, TikTok, YouTube, and the brand's owned channels; the right to edit, cut, and re-version; and the right to run the footage as paid ads.** A creator who only grants organic rights has sold you an asset you cannot use. This is the single most common and most expensive beginner mistake.

## Part B — The filming day

### Kit list (total: under $200)

- iPhone 12 or newer — **shoot 4K 30fps, rear camera, in 9:16**
- $25 tripod with a phone clamp
- **Lavalier microphone** (Rode SmartLav or a $20 clip-on). **This is the only piece of kit that genuinely matters.** Bad audio kills an ad; slightly imperfect video does not.
- A window. That's your lighting.
- Optional: $30 reflector or a piece of white foam board

### Location rules

Sunlit kitchen, patio, garden, porch, living room with a window. Film **between 9 AM and 11 AM or 3 PM and 5 PM** for warm directional light. Subject faces the window; camera between them and the window. Never film with a bright window *behind* the subject.

The set should look like her friend's house in Phoenix. Slightly lived-in. A fruit bowl. A dog bed. Real.

### Framing

- Vertical 9:16, subject's eyes on the top third
- Mid-shot: waist up. Close enough to read her face, wide enough to show the room
- Leave headroom for OST — **do not fill the top 20% of frame with her head**
- Product held at chest height when shown, label facing camera, held still for a full 2 seconds

### The batching principle — this is what makes it economical

**One creator, one day, one location = 12–15 assets.** Do not film one ad at a time.

**The shot list for a single creator day:**

```
BLOCK 1 — HOOKS (45 min)
  Film 8–10 different hooks, back to back, straight to camera.
  Same outfit, same framing, same spot.
  These become interchangeable openings for every body you shoot.

BLOCK 2 — BODIES (60 min)
  Film 3 complete script bodies (the 3–16s middle sections).

BLOCK 3 — CTAs (20 min)
  Film 3 CTA variations:
    · bundle-led  ("Buy 3 Get 3 Free, $16.66 a bottle")
    · guarantee-led ("90 days to change your mind")
    · urgency-led ("while the 6-bottle bundle is still on")

BLOCK 4 — B-ROLL (45 min) — shoot far more than you think you need
  · Taking capsules with water (3 angles)
  · Bottle on the counter, morning light
  · Turning the bottle to read the label
  · Coffee being poured
  · Walking outside, gardening, watering plants
  · Getting up from the floor / a low chair easily
  · Hands opening the delivery box
  · Phone in hand scrolling, then tapping
  · Wall clock at 3:00
  · Six bottles swept off a counter (for S-1 / Script 6)

BLOCK 5 — WILDCARD (20 min)
  Ask her unscripted questions and just let her talk:
    "What did you notice first?"
    "What did you think when you ordered it?"
    "What would you tell a friend who's sceptical?"
    "What didn't change?"
  → This block produces your best material about 40% of the time.
     The unscripted answer always beats the written line.
```

**Maths:** 10 hooks × 3 bodies = 30 possible combinations from a 3-hour shoot. You'll cut 12–15 that are actually worth running.

### Directing a non-actor (the part beginners get wrong)

- **Never let them read a script.** Give them the *idea* and let them say it their way. Reading is instantly visible and instantly kills trust.
- **Ask, don't instruct.** "Tell me about the afternoon thing" gets a better take than "say this line."
- **Take 3 is the good one.** Takes 1 and 2 are performance; by take 3 they've forgotten the camera. Always shoot at least four.
- **Let them stumble.** A small verbal stumble makes it real. Perfect delivery reads as an ad.
- **Never coach enthusiasm.** Overacted energy is the fastest way to look like a scam. Understated always wins with this buyer.
- **The pause is the ad.** In confession-style scripts (H01, H03, H07), tell them to say the line and then *stop.* The silence is what makes someone watch.

## Part C — The edit

### Rules

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Cut on the beat of the sentence | Use template transitions, glitch effects, zoom whips |
| Hold shots 2–3 seconds | Cut every 0.4s — she disengages from visual chaos |
| Burn in captions, always | Rely on Meta's auto-captions |
| Keep the raw handheld feel | Colour-grade it into a commercial |
| Music at 10–15% under the voice, or none at all | Loud trending audio (wrong demographic entirely) |
| Cut the very first breath so the hook starts on frame 1 | Let a 0.5s dead beat open the ad — that's your whole hook rate |

### Caption specification

- **Burned in, not platform-generated.** She scrolls sound-off in the morning.
- Large: ~60–72px on a 1080-wide canvas
- White text, black stroke or a semi-opaque bar behind it
- 3–5 words per line, positioned in the **middle third** — not at the bottom where Reels UI covers them
- Highlight the key word of each line in a contrasting colour (one colour only)

### Export settings

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Resolution | 1080 × 1920 (9:16) master; 1080 × 1350 (4:5) cutdown |
| Frame rate | 30fps |
| Codec | H.264, MP4 |
| Bitrate | 8–12 Mbps |
| Audio | AAC, 128kbps+, **normalised to −14 LUFS** |
| Length | Under 30s for Reels/Feed |
| File size | Under 500MB (Meta caps at 4GB but small files upload and process faster) |

### Free/cheap tools that are enough

CapCut (free, fastest for this format), DaVinci Resolve (free, more control), Descript (edit video by editing the transcript — excellent for cutting filler words), Canva (statics + simple video), Submagic or CapCut auto-captions (then fix them manually — auto-captions get supplement words wrong constantly).

## Part D — Versioning: one shoot, twelve ads

From a single creator day, build these systematically:

```
BASE ASSET:  Hook A + Body 1 + CTA 1

HOOK VARIANTS      Hook B + Body 1 + CTA 1     ← the highest-value test
                   Hook C + Body 1 + CTA 1
                   Hook D + Body 1 + CTA 1

CTA VARIANTS       Hook A + Body 1 + CTA 2 (guarantee-led)
                   Hook A + Body 1 + CTA 3 (urgency-led)

LENGTH VARIANTS    15s cutdown (hook + product + CTA only)
                   45s extended (add the mechanism section)

RATIO VARIANTS     every survivor in both 9:16 and 4:5

FORMAT VARIANTS    pull the strongest 3s of B-roll → make a static (S-8)
                   pull the best unscripted line → make a review static (S-5)
```

**One 3-hour shoot should yield 12–15 launchable ads.** If you're getting 3, you're filming one ad at a time — go back to the batching structure.

## Part E — The QA gate

Nothing goes live until every box is ticked. Use `templates/qa-checklist.md`.

**Technical**
- [ ] 9:16 and 4:5 both exported
- [ ] Captions burned in, correct spelling of *NMN*, *NAD+*, *Prime Ingredients*
- [ ] Audio normalised; no clipping; voice clearly above music
- [ ] First frame is visually interesting (it's your thumbnail)
- [ ] Nothing critical in the bottom 20% of the 9:16
- [ ] Filename follows the naming convention

**Message**
- [ ] Product name, 1000mg dose, and offer clear within the first 6 seconds
- [ ] A specific timeframe is stated ("around week three")
- [ ] Bundle + per-bottle price + guarantee all present in the CTA
- [ ] Trust element on screen for at least 2 continuous seconds

**Compliance** (see `11-compliance-guardrails.md`)
- [ ] No second-person assertion of the viewer's health, age, or condition
- [ ] No disease claim — nothing prevents, treats, cures, or reduces risk
- [ ] Every statistic substantiated and the source on file
- [ ] Testimonials genuine, unedited, and typical of real results
- [ ] Required disclaimer present

**The five-second test**
Show it to someone who's never seen it. After 5 seconds, ask: *"What's this about and who's it for?"* If they can't answer, the hook has failed and no amount of budget will fix it.
