The diagnosis, in three lines

CPM rose 38%. That is the signature of creative fatigue — the same few assets shown to the same Sunbelt women too many times. More spend buys more expensive impressions.

AOV fell 7%. The extra volume arrived on smaller bundles. The creative is selling a bottle, not the six-bottle commitment.

Cost per add-to-cart rose 80% ($20.62 → $37.17) while site conversion held flat at 3.5%. Click-through went up at the same time. That combination means one thing: curiosity clicks, not buying clicks.

Break-even, exactly Gross profit $77,060 Sales $131,967 Gross margin 58.4% Gross profit / order $77,060 ÷ 2,278 = $33.83 Current CPA $39.68 Gap −$5.85 per order −$5.85 × 2,278 orders = −$13,325 ← the reported loss

There is no hidden problem. We are simply paying $5.85 too much for every customer.

The prize Hold spend at $90,184/mo. Move CPA to $30 and AOV to $62: Orders $90,184 ÷ $30 = 3,006 (+728) Revenue 3,006 × $62 = $186,372 (+$54,405) Gross profit × 58.4% = $108,841 Less ad spend − $90,184 NET = +$18,657 / month

A $31,583/month swing at identical spend. Every rule in this playbook exists to move one of the five numbers above.

01The buyer

Every ad is written to one person

Her name is Linda. She's 62.

She lives in Phoenix. Retired elementary school teacher. Husband Bill is 66. Four grandchildren — 3, 5, 8 and 11. The 3-year-old is everything to her.

She wakes at 6:30 and does not feel rested. Vitamins with coffee. Walks the dog at 8. By 11 AM she is in her chair scrolling Facebook on an iPhone 14 Pro — 299 orders an hour, our single best window.

She sees the ad. "That's exactly how I feel." She taps. Four minutes on the page: Forbes logo, 5,128 reviews, two reviews from women who sound like her. She picks Buy 3 Get 3 Free. Apple Pay. Done.

At 3 PM her energy crashes. At 7 PM she is back in the chair, this time on Instagram. Second peak, 293 orders an hour.

03Non-negotiables

The twelve laws

Break any of these and the ad does not ship.

  1. One idea per ad. If you can't say what it is in five words, start over.
  2. Readable in one second, at arm's length, on a phone. Shrink the file to 15% and look at it.
  3. 4:5 and 9:16 only. Desktop converts at 1.86%. We don't design for it.
  4. Every ad carries social proof. Not most. Every one.
  5. The headline does 80% of the work. The image supports it, never the reverse.
  6. The bundle appears in the creative, not only on the landing page. This is how AOV reaches $62.
  7. Sunbelt light. Warm, bright, outdoors. Never snow, never corporate.
  8. Hands and faces are 55–70. The most-skipped rule, and worth more than any headline.
  9. No discount codes. Ever. She has never used one.
  10. Brand colour comes from the label. Familiarity between ad and bottle is a trust mechanic.
  11. Change one variable at a time. An ad you can't learn from is wasted spend.
  12. Clear compliance before shipping. A rejected ad costs a day; a restricted account costs the business.
04Copy

Twelve frameworks that need no copywriting skill

The full bank runs to 140+ headlines. These are the four that carry the most weight for this buyer.

Pain Point Gone

Name her exact struggle. Add the word "gone." Highest hit-rate in the set.

ENG3 PM energy crashes. Gone.
FOG"Where was I?" mid-sentence. Gone.
ENGTired in a way sleep doesn't fix. Gone.
GRDWatching the grandkids from the chair. Gone.

It's not [common belief], it's [unexpected cause]

The most powerful framework for this demographic. It lets her off the hook — her fatigue is not her fault and not inevitable. She has spent five years being told by her doctor and her husband that what she feels is normal. This is the first thing that agrees with her.

ENG · the account's proven hookMost women blame it on age. It's usually cellular energy.
FOGIt's not your memory. It's your mitochondria.
TRUIt's not that supplements don't work. It's that 64% of NMN fails lab testing.
ENGIt's not that you need more sleep. It's that sleep stopped working.

The Timeline Ad — the AOV weapon

Kills the "when will it work?" objection and makes the six-bottle bundle the logical purchase. A three-month promise needs a three-month supply. This is the single highest-priority framework for the $55.78 AOV problem.

Day 1Afternoon energy holds
Week 3Mental clarity comes back
Month 3Keeping up with the grandkids — effortlessly
Buy 3 Get 3 Free · 6 months supply · $16.66/bottle
The sleeper variant — test it Week 1 Probably nothing. That's normal. Week 3 The afternoons change first. Month 3 Where most women say "I feel 10 years younger."

Under-promising week one is the most credible thing you can say to a woman who has been scammed before.

The Testimonial Screenshot

She is skeptical of brands, not of women like her. Take a real review in her exact language and make it the entire ad. Mine your 5,128 reviews for four shapes, in priority order: skeptic-converted → specific-moment → time-anchored → third-party-noticed.

Non-negotiable

Real reviews only, verbatim, attributed to first name + last initial. No invented reviewers, no invented like counts, no stock headshot standing in for the customer. Fabricated reviews are unlawful under the FTC's consumer-review rule and violate Meta policy independently — and you have 5,128 genuine ones, which is more than enough.

Also in the bank

  • From [pain] to [dream outcome] — "From watching from the couch → to on the floor with your grandkids."
  • Do Not Buy This — "Don't buy this unless you're prepared to out-walk all your friends." Filters junk clicks, which is directly relevant to our cost-per-ATC problem.
  • Three Reasons Why · Us vs. Them (never name a competitor) · The Objection Checklist (best retargeting asset in the set)
  • The Breakup Letter — "Dear 3 PM, we had a routine. You'd take my afternoon, I'd blame my age…"
  • The iMessage Thread — the highest-emotion asset available: "mom you got on the floor with Emma today" / "I know" / "you haven't done that in like two years" / "I know 🙂"

Words to ban

Brand vocabulary she doesn't trust: unlock · biohack · optimize · game-changer · revolutionary · supercharge · secret · miracle · reverse aging — and any disease name, in any construction.

Weekly rhythm

Mon read the numbers, kill losers, mine ten new reviews · Tue ideation, twelve briefs · Wed–Thu AM build twelve creatives in both ratios, QA · Thu PM launch · Fri hands off · Sat–Sun peak days, scale winners only, no new launches.

File naming is the join key

NMN_[TERR]_[FRAMEWORK]_[FORMAT]_[RATIO]_v[NN] — and the ad name in Ads Manager must match the filename exactly. Without this you'll find winning ads but never learn which angle wins, and the angle is the compounding asset.

The colour trap

Avoid blue-on-black and blue/green text pairings. The aging lens yellows, which compresses blue discrimination specifically — a 62-year-old sees your navy-on-charcoal as mud. Warm tones outperform clinical blues here anyway: blue reads as pharmaceutical, and pharmaceutical reads as "the thing my doctor already told me is normal."

The people rule — the most-skipped, highest-value rule in this document

Every human in a Prime Ingredients ad is 55–70. Not 45 with grey hair. Check for: visible age markers, short natural nails, a wedding band worn thin, linen and cotton, natural silver hair, a patio or garden in warm morning light. If she sees hands that look like hers, she believes the product is for her.

Image models render people 15–20 years younger than you ask. Push the age language hard and re-roll until it's right.

07Media buying

Kill and scale rules, derived from this account

Three campaigns, no more. Testing at 15% of spend, scaling 70%, retargeting 15%. Broad targeting, 55–70, US, no interest stack — Meta already knows who buys NMN from 110K sessions of pixel data, and interest narrowing is a contributing cause of the +38% CPM.

Kill
  • $60 spent, 0 add-to-carts
  • $100 spent, 0 purchases
  • Outbound CTR <2.5% after 5,000 impressions
  • Outbound CPC >$3.50 after $50
  • CPA >$45 after $150 and ≥3 purchases
  • Frequency >2.5 and CPA rising 3 days running
Watch · review in 48h
  • CPA between $34 and $45
  • Outbound CTR 2.5–5.0%
  • Under $100 spend with 1–2 purchases — not enough data, don't kill early
Winner · promote to scale
  • CPA ≤ $30
  • Spend ≥ $150
  • Purchases ≥ 3
  • Outbound CTR ≥ 5.0%
  • All four true, or it isn't a winner

Scaling ladder: promote into the scale campaign at $100/day → hold 3 days → if CPA holds ≤$30, raise +20% → repeat every 3 days. If CPA breaks $34 for two consecutive days, roll back to the last working budget. Never raise more than 20% in a day.

Three things not to do

  • Don't kill before $60 spend. At a $30 CPA, one purchase is normal randomness. Killing at $25 is superstition.
  • Don't judge ROAS at the ad level. Use CPA — ad-level ROAS is distorted by which bundle happened to sell.
  • Don't edit a live winner. Any edit wipes accumulated social proof and re-enters learning. Duplicate instead.
On dayparting — the honest version

The data says 11 AM and 7 PM ET. But ad scheduling requires a lifetime budget, and restricting hours on your main campaign starves delivery and raises CPM — usually costing more than the timing gains. Control the launch timing instead, which is free: publish Thursday morning so ads exit learning by Saturday's peak. If you want the dayparted play, run one dedicated lifetime-budget ad set from 2–5 PM ET carrying only "3 PM crash" creatives — she is living the pain point in real time in that window — cap it at 10% of budget, and judge it on its own CPA.

Attribution — don't mix the two

Triple Whale runs Triple Attribution, lifetime window, and will disagree with Meta: Meta reported 1,502 purchases at $60.04 CPA while Shopify recorded 2,278 orders at $39.68 blended. Blended is truth for "are we making money." In-platform is the comparator for "which ad beat which ad." Never use them in the same sentence.

08Execution

The first thirty days

Week 0Setup. Confirm offer, trust assets and product facts. Pull label hex codes. Export all reviews. Build the trust-asset folder and product cutouts. Set up the AI Brain Trust. Build the three campaigns, paused. Build the six beginner Canva templates.
Week 1Prove the machine. Record the baseline Monday. Twelve briefs Tuesday. Build Wed–Thu. Launch twelve creatives Thursday PM. Three of them — Timeline ×2 and Bundle Maths — are the AOV assets, and they must ship in week one.
Week 2Find the first winner. Kill against the thresholds and log why — the reasons are the learning. Half of batch two must be variations of whatever is performing best. If nothing is under $34 CPA after 24 creatives, the problem is probably the landing page, not the creative.
Week 3Scale what works. Weight the territory quota toward the lowest-CPA angle. Launch the dayparted 2–5 PM ENG ad set now that you have proven ENG creatives to put in it.
Week 4Consolidate. Retire anything over 2.5 frequency with rising CPA; replace with headline-only variants. Ratio settles at 70% variations of winners, 30% net-new exploration — and holds there permanently.

09Risk

What actually gets you shut down

94% of this business's revenue runs through Meta. There is no fallback channel if the account goes down. This is operational risk guidance, not legal advice — have a regulatory attorney sign off on the claim set once, then this becomes the daily checklist.

Personal attributes — the most common rejection

Meta prohibits copy that implies knowledge of a person's health. The mechanical fix is moving from "you have X" to "people who have X" — third person, category-level, or customer-attributed. You keep the emotional hit and lose the exposure.

RiskyCompliant rewrite
"Feeling more tired as you age?""Most women over 60 blame it on age. It's usually cellular energy."
"Your brain fog isn't normal""Brain fog after 60 is common. It isn't inevitable."
"Struggling with post-menopause fatigue?""Post-menopause fatigue is one of the most common reasons women try NMN."
"You can't keep up with your grandkids anymore""Keeping up with the grandkids is the #1 reason our customers started."

The brand's existing hook sits right on this line and is currently tolerated. Test the third-person variant alongside it — if it performs comparably you've removed a standing risk for free, and you'll want it already proven if the original starts getting rejected at scale.

The other three

  • Disease claims. Structure/function only. Never name a disease — not even in a "not a treatment for" construction. Don't put the word in the ad.
  • Fabricated reviews. Unlawful under the FTC consumer-review rule with per-violation civil penalties, and a Meta violation independently. Real reviews only.
  • Unsubstantiated stats. Every number needs a document in a folder before the ad runs — including the "64% of NMN products fail lab tests" claim, which is the highest-value and highest-risk stat in the whole plan.
Know this before you scale

FDA has taken the position that NMN is excluded from the dietary supplement definition, which is what drove major marketplace delistings. The industry has contested it and the position has moved. It's not a reason to stop advertising — it's a reason to keep every claim boringly defensible and to have the substantiation folder built before spend rises. Verify current status with a regulatory advisor.
