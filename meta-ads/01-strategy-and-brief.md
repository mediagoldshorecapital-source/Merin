# 01 — Strategy & Creative Brief

The permanent brief. Every static ad is judged against this document.

## Part A — The account as it actually is (not as we'd like it to be)

Pulled from Triple Whale on 11 Aug 2026. Attribution model: Triple Attribution, lifetime window.

| Metric | Last 30d (12 Jul – 10 Aug) | Prior 30d (12 Jun – 11 Jul) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | $131,967 | $69,554 | **+90%** |
| Orders | 2,278 | 1,110 | +105% |
| **AOV** | **$55.78** | **$60.02** | **−7%** |
| Meta ad spend | $90,184 | $47,484 | +90% |
| Blended ROAS | 1.46 | 1.46 | flat |
| **Blended CPA** | **$39.68** | $42.82 | −7% |
| **Net profit** | **−$12,926** | −$5,784 | **worse** |
| CPM | $121.07 | $87.90 | **+38%** |
| Outbound CTR | 5.16% | 3.96% | +30% |
| Outbound CPC | $2.35 | $2.22 | +6% |
| **Cost per Add-to-Cart** | **$37.17** | $20.62 | **+80%** |
| Site conversion rate (pixel) | 3.47% | 3.45% | flat |
| Discount rate | 36.3% of gross | 34.5% | +2pts |
| New customers | 62.4% of orders | 69.8% | repeat base building |

### What this says in plain English

Spend doubled. Revenue doubled. **Profit got worse, not better.** Three things caused it:

1. **CPM jumped 38%.** That is the classic signature of creative fatigue plus audience saturation — the same few creatives being shown to the same Sunbelt women too many times. More spend chasing the same assets buys more expensive impressions.
2. **AOV fell 7%** ($60.02 → $55.78). The extra volume came in on smaller bundles. Creative is selling *a bottle*, not *the 6-bottle commitment*.
3. **Cost per add-to-cart rose 80%** while site conversion rate held flat at ~3.5%. The clicks got cheaper-quality: people are clicking and not adding. That is a creative-to-landing-page promise mismatch, not a checkout problem.

CTR going *up* while cost-per-ATC goes up 80% is the tell. The ads are getting more clicks from people who weren't really in market. Curiosity clicks, not buying clicks.

**This playbook is the fix for all three.** Volume and variety of new static creative attacks the CPM. Bundle-led messaging attacks the AOV. Angle-specific, objection-handling statics attack the ATC rate.

## Part B — The break-even maths (memorise these three numbers)

From the last 30 days:

```
Gross profit                 $77,060
Sales                       $131,967
Gross margin                   58.4%

Gross profit per order   $77,060 / 2,278  =  $33.83
```

| The number | Value | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Break-even CPA** | **$33.83** | Above this, every order loses money |
| **Break-even blended ROAS** | **1.71** | 1 ÷ 0.584 |
| **Current blended CPA** | **$39.68** | We are $5.85 over the line, on every single order |

$5.85 × 2,278 orders = **−$13,325**, which is almost exactly the reported −$12,926 net loss. The maths is clean. There is no hidden problem — we are simply paying too much per customer.

### The targets this playbook is built to hit

| Metric | Today | Target | Why that number |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blended CPA | $39.68 | **≤ $30.00** | Leaves $3.83/order contribution ≈ 7% net margin |
| Blended ROAS | 1.46 | **≥ 1.86** | The ROAS equivalent of the same thing |
| AOV | $55.78 | **≥ $62.00** | Achieved by leading with Buy 3 Get 3 Free in the creative itself |
| CPM | $121.07 | **≤ $95** | Achieved with creative volume — fresh assets buy cheaper impressions |
| Cost per ATC | $37.17 | **≤ $24** | Achieved by matching ad promise to landing page promise |

### The prize, stated as money

Hold spend at $90,184/month. Move CPA to $30 and AOV to $62:

```
Orders      $90,184 / $30      =  3,006 orders   (+728 vs today)
Revenue     3,006 × $62        =  $186,372       (+$54,405 vs today)
Gross profit  $186,372 × 58.4% =  $108,841
Less ad spend                  −  $90,184
NET                            =  +$18,657 / month
```

**Swing: from −$12,926 to +$18,657 per month. A $31,583/month turnaround at identical spend.**

That is the whole point of this document. Every rule below exists because it moves one of those five numbers.

> **One honest caveat that is outside creative's control:** the site bounce rate is 93.9% and pages-per-session is 1.12. Creative can raise the quality of the click, but if the landing page is not doing its job the CPA ceiling is hard. Worth a separate landing-page sprint after creative volume is flowing.

## Part C — Who she is (the one-page card)

**Print this. Tape it above the monitor. Every ad is written to this person.**

> **Her name is Linda. She's 62.**
>
> She lives in Phoenix. Retired elementary school teacher. Husband Bill is 66. Four grandchildren — 3, 5, 8, 11. The 3-year-old is everything to her.
>
> She wakes at 6:30 AM and does not feel rested. Vitamins with coffee. Walks the dog at 8. By 11 AM she is in her chair scrolling Facebook on an iPhone 14 Pro — **this is our single best hour, 299 orders/hr.**
>
> She sees the ad. *"That's exactly how I feel."* She taps. Four minutes on the page: Forbes logo, 5,128 reviews, reads two reviews from women who sound like her, picks Buy 3 Get 3 Free, pays with Apple Pay.
>
> At 3 PM her energy crashes. At 7 PM she is back in the chair, this time on Instagram. **Second peak, 293 orders/hr.**

### Hard facts to design against

| Attribute | Reality | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Age | 55–70, avg 62 | Presbyopia. Minimum 36px body text on a 1080px canvas. No thin fonts. No grey-on-grey. |
| Device | Mobile 4.21% CVR / **Tablet 5.11% CVR** / Desktop 1.86% | **4:5 and 9:16 only.** Never design for desktop. |
| Location | CA, FL, TX, NY, IL, OH, GA, AZ. Phoenix, Tucson, Las Vegas, Houston, San Antonio, Miami | Sunbelt. Warm light, outdoors, gardens, pools, walking paths. Not snow, not New York lofts. |
| Peak hours | **11 AM ET** and **7 PM ET** | Schedule delivery from 10:30 AM and 6:30 PM |
| Peak days | **Saturday** (742 orders), **Thursday** (732). Monday worst (575, −22%) | Push new creative Thu–Sat. Never launch a new ad set on Monday. |
| Purchase shape | ~40% buy 6 bottles, ~30% buy 4, ~20% buy 2, only ~10% buy 1 | **She is a bundle buyer.** Show the bundle, not the single. |
| Discount codes used | **Zero across 4,770 orders** | Never put a promo code in an ad. The offer *is* the bundle. |
| Gender | 96% of repeat customers female | Female-first imagery and voice; Robert is a secondary segment |

### The male buyer — "Robert"

60–72. Retired professional, veteran, tradesman. Gmail/iCloud. Same cities. Higher AOV, buys $100+ bundles more often. **ROAS 1.81 — the most efficient demographic in the account.** His core fear: *"I'm becoming my father."* He watched his dad slow down and lose his edge.

Robert is under-served. Allocate ~20% of creative volume to him. His angles: recovery, staying sharp, out-working men 20 years younger, still doing his own yard work.

## Part F — The offer, stated the way it must appear in every ad

```
Buy 3 Get 3 Free
6 bottles — $16.66 each          [CONFIRM total: ~$100–106]
Regular price $29.98/bottle
Free shipping                     [CONFIRM]
[XX]-day money-back guarantee     [CONFIRM length]
```

**Rules:**
- Never a discount code. She has never used one — zero codes across 4,770 orders.
- Always the per-bottle price ($16.66), because it makes a $100 purchase feel like a $16 decision.
- Always the anchor ($29.98) next to it. The maths does the selling.
- The bundle is not just a discount — **it is a trust signal.** If the product were fake, why would you give away three free bottles? Frame it that way in TRU-territory ads.

## Part H — The twelve laws

Break any of these and the ad does not ship.

1. **One idea per ad.** If you can't say what it is in five words, start over.
2. **Readable in one second, at arm's length, on a phone.** Test it: shrink the file to 15% and look at it. If the headline isn't legible, the headline is too small.
3. **4:5 (1080×1350) and 9:16 (1080×1920) only.** Desktop converts at 1.86%. We do not design for it.
4. **Every ad carries social proof.** Review count, star rating, press logo, or a real customer quote.
5. **The headline does 80% of the work.** The image supports it. Never the reverse.
6. **The bundle appears in the creative,** not just the landing page. This is how AOV gets to $62.
7. **Sunbelt light.** Warm, bright, outdoors, gardens. Never snow, never corporate.
8. **Hands and faces must be 55–70 years old.** If she sees hands that look like hers, she believes the product is for her. This is the single most-skipped rule and it is worth more than any headline.
9. **No discount codes. Ever.**
10. **Pull brand colour from the label.** Familiarity between the ad and the bottle is a trust mechanic.
11. **Change one variable at a time.** An ad you can't learn from is wasted spend.
12. **Run it through `08-compliance.md` before it ships.** A rejected ad costs a day; a restricted account costs the business.

## Part I — What "good" looks like, per ad

An ad is a **winner** when, after ≥$150 spend:
- CPA ≤ $30
- Outbound CTR ≥ 5.0%
- Outbound CPC ≤ $2.35
- ≥ 3 purchases

An ad is **dead** when:
- $60 spent, 0 add-to-carts
- $100 spent, 0 purchases
- Outbound CTR < 2.5% after 5,000 impressions

Full rules, including the scaling ladder, are in `06-testing-and-measurement.md`.
