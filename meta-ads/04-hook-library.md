# 04 — Hook Library: 60 Written Hooks, Ready to Film

Every hook below is written in Linda's, the Professional's, or Robert's actual register — plain, specific, unglamorous. Each has an ID you use in the ad name (`H01`–`H60`).

**How to use this file:** pick a hook, pair it with the matching visual, drop it into the script template in `05-script-library.md`. Do not rewrite a hook to sound "more marketing." The flatness is the point.

---

## Reading the columns

- **Visual** — what must be on screen during those 3 seconds. The hook is audible *and* visual. Both.
- **OST** — the burned-in on-screen text. Short. Large. High contrast.
- **⚠️** — flagged for the compliance rewrite in `11-compliance-guardrails.md`. Usable, but use the noted framing.

| H07 | "I turned down a trip with my daughter because I knew I'd slow everyone down." | Photo of a trip on a phone screen, then creator | The trip I said no to |
| H08 ⚠️ | "Every time I forget a word, there's about two seconds where I think: is this how it started for my mother?" | Creator at kitchen table, hands still | The two seconds nobody talks about |

## Hook testing protocol

1. **Test hooks, not ads.** Film the body and CTA **once**, then attach 3–5 different hooks. Same body, different first three seconds. This is the cheapest test in performance marketing and most beginners never do it.
2. **Judge a hook on hook rate alone** (3-second views ÷ impressions). If hook rate is under 20%, the hook is dead — the body never got a chance and tells you nothing.
3. **A winning hook is a portable asset.** When a hook beats the batch, immediately attach it to two *other* bodies. Hooks generalise; bodies don't.
4. **Retire on frequency, not on boredom.** You will be sick of a hook long before Linda is. Keep it until hook rate decays 25% from its peak or frequency exceeds ~2.5 in a 7-day window.
