The three CPA lines that govern everything

  • $52 — the working target. At 1.81 orders per customer (your June cohort's actual), lifetime contribution is ≈$63. A $52 CPA leaves ~$11 per customer and a real margin as retention extends.

Two things follow, and they are not optional.

First, the discount is doing more damage than the media buying. You are giving away $32.10 per order — nearly your entire contribution margin. Moving from 35% off to 25% off adds roughly $9.20 of contribution per order. That single change lifts first-order contribution from $34.73 to ~$44 and moves your break-even ROAS from 1.74 to 1.37 — which is within reach of what this account already produced in February. No creative or structure work delivers that much that fast.

Second, retention is now the asset. At 1.81 orders you are marginal. At 2.5 orders lifetime contribution is ≈$116 and a $52 CPA is a 2.2× return. Every point of subscription retention is worth more than a point of ROAS, because it raises the CPA you can afford — which in turn lets you win more auctions. Track cohort order-count monthly as a headline metric, not a footnote.

03

The 2026 operating model

The framework below is the corroborated Andromeda-era playbook — not the video's transcript, which I could not access. It is included because this account is a textbook case of the structure it argues against.

Meta rebuilt the retrieval layer that runs before the auction. The practical consequence is that targeting has moved upstream into the creative: the system infers who should see an ad from what the ad is, rather than from who you told it to find. Four principles follow.

Principle 1 — Creative is the targeting layer

You no longer segment audiences; you segment messages, and the system routes each message to the people it fits. A testimonial from a 68-year-old finds 68-year-olds. This is why your dayparted, one-ad-per-ad-set structure is actively harmful: you have given the router seven copies of the same signal and no variety to route.

Principle 2 — Variation, not iteration

The distinction that matters most, and the one this account fails: changing a hook on the same video is iteration. Changing the concept is variation. Ten UGC clips with different first lines read to the system as one creative. Your library is "Video Ad 1, 2, 3" plus copies — one casting call, not a film festival. The published guidance converges on 8–12 conceptually distinct concepts per campaign, 15–25 active ads per ad set, deliberately different in persona, format, emotional register, and pain point.

Principle 3 — Consolidation is a technical requirement

Fewer ad sets, each with more diverse creative, beat fragmented structures — one widely cited controlled test put a single ad set with 25 diverse creatives 17% ahead on conversions at 16% lower cost than a five-ad-set split. Consolidation is not a tidiness preference; it is how you get enough conversions per learner for the model to work at all.

Principle 4 — Signal quality gates everything

Pixel and Conversions API running together, deduplicated, with Event Match Quality above 7. A rebuilt structure on a degraded signal will underperform the mess you have now. Verify this before you touch anything else — I cannot see EMQ from Triple Whale, so it is an unknown in this plan and the first thing to check in Events Manager.

Where I'd diverge from the generic advice

The standard playbook says "upload your full catalogue so Andromeda has thousands of entry points." You have one product — NMN is 91% of revenue ($150,943 of ~$166k). Catalogue breadth is not available to you, so your entry-point diversity has to come entirely from concepts. That raises the creative bar for this brand specifically: the 8–12 concept minimum in §5 is a floor, not a target.

Creative masterplan

Structure gets you back to break-even. Creative is the only thing that scales you past it. You need to go from 3 concepts to 12, and the casting has to change.

Craft rules for a 55–75 audience

These are not general best practices; they follow from what this account's numbers and demographics say.

  • Cast the actual buyer. Women 55–75, not 35–45. This is the most common failure in supplement creative and the cheapest to fix.
  • Slow down. Your 3.9% hold rate on the highest-hook ad says the body loses people. TikTok jump-cut grammar does not work here. One idea per 5 seconds, not three.
  • Burn in captions, large and high-contrast. Sound-off viewing plus presbyopia. Minimum 40px equivalent, never thin weights over video.
  • Design for Facebook, not Instagram. Facebook Reels and Feed take 70% of spend. Instagram Feed is your worst major placement (0.83). Check every asset in FB Feed's 4:5 crop first.
  • Lead with the mechanism, not the vibe. This audience buys explanations. NAD+ decline with age is a genuinely persuasive, visualisable story.
  • Use comments as an asset. Run winners as post-ID ads and moderate — don't delete — the comment thread. Your post-shares beat fresh uploads by 16% on ROAS.

Each row is a genuinely distinct concept — different persona, entry point, and format. That is what "variation not iteration" means in practice. Build 3–4 per sprint; never let one concept exceed 25% of spend.

06

The SOPs

Seven procedures. The point of writing them down is that they constrain you on bad days — most of the damage in this account came from unstructured reactions to single-day numbers.

SOP-1

The morning check

Daily · 15 min · before 10am ET
  1. Open Triple Whale summary. Record yesterday: spend, blended ROAS, MER, new-customer CPA, orders.
  2. Compare against the trailing 7-day average, never against yesterday. Single days are noise at this volume.
  3. Check total spend pacing against the monthly plan. Flag if ±15%.
  4. Scan for delivery breakage only: ads in review, rejected ads, ad sets that spent $0, payment failures.
  5. Permitted actions today: fix breakage, pause an ad that meets the SOP-5 kill rule, answer comments. Nothing else.
  6. Forbidden: budget changes, new ad sets, audience edits, turning off a "bad day." Budget moves happen on Mondays only, under SOP-4.
SOP-2

Weekly creative council

Mondays · 60 min
  1. Pull last 7 days at ad level: spend, ROAS, new-customer CPA, hook rate, hold rate, outbound CTR, CPM, frequency.
  2. Sort by spend. For the top 10, classify each: Scale Iterate Kill using SOP-5 thresholds.
  3. Concentration check: if any single concept exceeds 25% of spend, that is a risk flag, not a success. Brief a replacement.
  4. Fatigue check: frequency above 1.4, or CPM up more than 20% week-on-week at flat CTR, means the pool is tiring. Accelerate the next batch.
  5. Diagnose winners properly — hook rate and hold rate separately. A high-hook/low-hold ad needs a new body; a low-hook/high-hold ad needs a new opening. Do not rebuild the whole ad.
  6. Brief 3–4 new concepts from the §5 matrix. Assign owner and delivery date.
  7. Confirm next batch launches Thursday (SOP-3).
SOP-3

Creative testing protocol

Weekly · launch Thursdays
  1. Batch of 4–6 conceptually distinct creatives into the Creative Test campaign. Never iterations — those go straight to ASC.
  2. One ad set per batch at ~$225/day. Broad US 45+. Do not split by creative.
  3. Run a minimum of 4 days and $600 per creative before any judgement. At a $56 CPA that is ~10 conversions — still thin, so read direction, not certainty.
  4. Read in this order: hook rate → hold rate → outbound CTR → CPA. An ad can fail on CPA and still be a winning hook worth harvesting.
  5. Graduate to ASC if new-customer CPA ≤ $58 or hook rate ≥ 45% with hold ≥ 12%. Move by post ID so engagement carries.
  6. Kill if spend > $600 with 0–1 purchases, or hook rate < 20%.
  7. Log every result — concept, hook, format, talent age, metrics — in the creative ledger. The ledger is the compounding asset, not any single ad.
SOP-4

Scaling protocol

Mondays only

Written specifically to prevent a repeat of 19 July, when a 38% single-step increase cost 21% of ROAS and never recovered.

  1. Scale only when all three hold on a 7-day basis: new-customer CPA ≤ $52, blended ROAS ≥ 1.30, frequency ≤ 1.30.
  2. Maximum +20% per campaign per week. No exceptions, including good news.
  3. After any increase, hold for a full 72 hours before judging. Meta re-enters partial learning on budget changes above ~20%.
  4. If CPA rises more than 15% after an increase, revert to the previous budget and hold 7 days. Do not push through.
  5. Scale creative before budget. Adding 4 fresh ads to ASC raises capacity; adding dollars to a tired pool raises CPM. When in doubt, ship creative.
  6. Descaling follows the same rule in reverse: −20% steps, never a hard stop, which resets learning.
SOP-5

Kill rules

Applied at SOP-1 and SOP-2
  1. Ad, in test: spend ≥ $600 with ≤1 purchase, or hook rate < 20% after 50k impressions.
  2. Ad, in ASC: 7-day spend ≥ $1,000 with new-customer CPA > $67 (the hard ceiling). Pause the ad, keep the ad set.
  3. Ad set: 14-day ROAS < 0.75 on ≥ $2,000 spend.
  4. Campaign: 30-day ROAS < 0.80 — this is why Astaxanthin (0.52) and N2 Testing (0.77) go now.
  5. Never kill on fewer than 3 days of data or fewer than 3 conversions of evidence.
  6. Pause, don't delete. Deleted history cannot be diagnosed, and post IDs with engagement are assets.
SOP-6

Launch checklist

Every new ad, before publish
  1. Captions burned in, high contrast, legible at arm's length on a phone.
  2. Talent age matches buyer age (55–75), or the concept is explicitly male-facing (#12).
  3. Checked in Facebook Feed 4:5 crop and 9:16 Reels — safe zones clear.
  4. Naming convention: CONCEPT_FORMAT_TALENT_HOOKID_vN. No nesting, no "Copy", no dates in parentheses.
  5. UTMs complete and consistent — Triple Whale attribution depends on it.
  6. Destination is /products/nmn with the correct offer pre-selected.
  7. Claims reviewed: structure/function language only, no disease claims. This is a supplement sold to an older demographic — the compliance and chargeback risk is real.
  8. Logged in the creative ledger before it goes live.
SOP-7

Monthly economics review

First Monday of the month · 90 min
  1. Rebuild the §2 contribution table from the last full month. COGS, discount depth, and shipping all move.
  2. Pull cohort order-count for every cohort from March. Is orders-per-customer rising or flat? This is the headline number of the business.
  3. Recalculate the three CPA lines. Reset the SOP-4 scaling threshold and the SOP-5 kill ceiling to match.
  4. Review subscription churn and the cancellation reasons in Loop. Retention buys you CPA headroom faster than optimisation does.
  5. Review discount depth against plan. It was 36.7% in August; it should be trending toward 25%.
  6. Recheck Event Match Quality and CAPI deduplication in Events Manager.

Read the funnel carefully: of every 100 sessions, 5.3 add to cart and 3.8 buy. Cart-to-purchase is 73% — that is strong; checkout is not your problem. The leak is session → add-to-cart. 94.7% of the traffic you pay $2.55 a session for never touches the button.

Lifting ATC from 5.27% to 7% — not a heroic target — would raise CVR to roughly 5.1% and cut effective CPA by about a third, from $63 to ~$47. That is larger than anything in §4 or §5.

  • Design for the 65+ mobile user. 94% of sessions are mobile and your median buyer is over 60. Body text at 18px minimum, buttons over 48px, high contrast, no thin greys.
  • Offer above the fold, decided in one screen. With a 96% bounce rate, most people see one viewport. The 3-bottle bundle and the subscription choice must both be visible without scrolling.
  • Desktop converts at 1.85% but has a $71 AOV. Something is broken on desktop specifically — likely bundle selection. Worth a session recording review even at 6% of traffic.
  • Tablet is your best-converting device (4.10%). Consistent with an older audience. Make sure the tablet breakpoint isn't an afterthought.

The discount is eating the business

Covered in §2, restated here because it belongs on the operations list: $32.10 of every $60.34 order is discount. At 36.7% off list in August it is the deepest it has been in six months, and it is roughly equal to your entire contribution margin. A 10-point reduction adds ~$9.20 per order and moves break-even ROAS from 1.74 to 1.37.

Test it as a real test: 28% for two weeks, hold everything else constant, and measure contribution per session rather than conversion rate. A discount that buys volume at negative margin is not a growth lever.
