Meta Ads · Creative Strategy

Ten ads that fill a real gap beat fifty that say the same thing in different fonts.

This is the complete system for building Meta ad creative that produces measurable revenue — written so someone who has never made an ad can execute it at an expert level. It is applied end-to-end to Prime Ingredients NMN Complex 1000mg.

The thesis

Creative strategy is not picking formats or copying competitors. It is finding the gaps in an ad account — unaddressed personas, unaddressed awareness levels, undiversified angles — and filling them with messages mined from the customer's own words. Everything here is machinery for doing that on a schedule.

The one failure that matters

The most common beginner mistake is skipping the research phase and going straight to writing ads. Ads written without the VoC Bank are guesses. Ads written from it are transcription — and transcription wins.

01

The system, end to end

Seven phases. Roughly 20 focused hours for the first cycle on a new brand, then about 4 hours a month to maintain. Phases are numbered because they are genuinely sequential — each one consumes the output of the last.

PH 0IntakeBrand facts, offer math, asset inventory, platform access2h
PH 1Research — the 3-part systemReputation Analysis · Review & Comment Mining · Account Gap Analysis8h
PH 2SynthesisPersonas → awareness ladder → pillars → angles → the Concept Grid3h
PH 3ConceptingHooks, ad architecture, scripts, static concepts4h
PH 4ProductionCasting, briefs, shoot, edit, compliance QAvaries
PH 5LaunchAccount structure, naming, budget split, timing1h
PH 6Measure & iterateDiagnostics, kill/scale, iteration laddersweekly

Five non-negotiables

  1. No concept ships without a Concept ID. Persona × Awareness × Pillar × Angle × Format. If you can't name the cell it fills, you're making an ad for no one.
  2. No hook is invented if a customer already said it better. Mine first, write second.
  3. One variable per iteration. Change the hook and the creator and the CTA, and a result teaches you nothing.
  4. Never launch a new ad set on Monday. Your own data: Monday runs 22% below Saturday.
  5. Compliance-check every script before shooting. A rejection costs a day. A restricted account costs the business.
02

Phase 1 — the three-part research system

This is the phase that separates elite accounts from average ones, and it is the phase beginners skip. Do not write a word of ad copy until all three outputs exist.

1.1 · Reputation Analysis — 3 hours

You are hunting for objections and skepticism, not compliments. Do it manually — AI summaries skip the weird, specific, useful material.

SourceWhat to doWhat it yields
Founder backstoryAbout page, podcasts, LinkedIn. Why does this product exist?Origin narrative + 3 credential proof points
Google page 1Search brand, +reviews, +scam, +complaints, is X legit, X vs YEvery negative headline a skeptical buyer will see
Amazon highest yieldRead all 1–3★ on the 3 closest competitors. Then use Ask Amazon AI: "Who buys this and why?" · "What do reviewers complain about most?" · "What did people try before this?"Free persona + objection breakdown synthesized across thousands of reviews
Redditsite:reddit.com [category] "waste of money"15 verbatim skepticism quotes — the exact phrasing of doubt
YouTubeSearch category reviews. Read the comments, not the video.10 quotes + which creator archetypes the audience trusts
PressVerify every mention is real and quotableBadge assets
Competitor ad librariesMeta Ad Library, filter to ads running 90+ days15–25 proven concepts. A long-running ad is a profitable ad — the only free performance data that exists.

1.2 · Review & Comment Mining — 3 hours

Build a bank of the customer's own words so your copy becomes transcription rather than invention. Dump everything into one sheet, one quote per row, then tag on four axes: emotion (fear / shame / hope / relief / pride / anger / grief), job-to-be-done, objection or benefit, and trigger moment.

Why emotion is the sort column

Sort by emotion and read each cluster together. A cluster of fifteen quotes about the same fear is not one ad — it's a content pillar that will support six.

What makes a quote a golden nugget

It needs at least three of: a specific time or place · a physical sensation rather than an abstraction · a comparison to a former self · a slightly embarrassing admission · plain unbranded language · quotable in under twelve words.

"I used to do everything. Now I sit while others do."Ten words · former self · admission · zero marketing language. That is a hook, unedited.

1.3 · Account Gap Analysis — 2 hours

The highest-leverage hour in the whole SOP, because a gap is a guaranteed source of incremental performance — you cannot cannibalize an audience you were never talking to. Export your top 30–50 ads by spend and tag each with persona, awareness level, pillar, angle, format, and creator demographic. Then run three diagnostics.

Gap A

Persona gaps

Who has money and no ads?

Compare Meta demographics by ROAS against creative volume by persona.

  • One persona gets >70% of creative
  • A high-ROAS segment gets <10% of spend
  • A segment appears in Shopify but nowhere in your creative log — you're winning them by accident
Gap B

Awareness gaps

Are you only harvesting?

The classic failure is 70%+ of spend at Product/Most-aware. That account harvests existing demand and hits a hard ceiling.

  • Unaware + Problem-aware · 40–50%
  • Solution-aware · 20–25%
  • Product-aware · 20–25%
  • Most-aware · 10–15%
Gap C

Diversity gaps

Score each persona out of five

Not a values exercise — a delivery-cost one. Different creative finds different pockets of the audience.

  • ≥3 distinct pillars in market
  • ≥2 angles per pillar
  • ≥3 formats
  • ≥3 creator demographics
  • ≥3 awareness levels

70% of orders are four or more bottles. She isn't buying a supplement, she's buying a three-to-six-month commitment. Creative that sells "try a bottle" is selling the wrong thing to the wrong person — it lowers AOV and it lowers belief.

She doesn't hunt for codes. Don't build creative around a promo code — build it around the anchor-versus-effective price, $29.98 → $16.66. The math is the offer. A code adds friction and invites her to leave the page looking for a better one.

The sharpest insight in the intelligence report

The Buy 3 Get 3 Free bundle is a trust signal disguised as a discount. If the product were fake, why would you give away three bottles? That reframe is an entire ad — and it converts the offer pillar from a bottom-funnel harvest into a proof asset.

Why "Is this a scam?" is priority zero

Insecurity #6 in the report reads: "she reads every word on your page. She's looking for reasons NOT to buy." An offer ad repels that person. A skepticism ad recruits her. It is the cheapest incremental audience in the account, and there is currently nothing serving it.

Roughly half of everything an ad will ever earn is decided in the first three seconds. Write fifteen hooks per concept, shoot five, test three. Bold is on-screen text, italic is the spoken line — they should never be identical, because that wastes a channel.

Compliance

Read before every shoot

Health is a restricted category on Meta. Two rules get beginners rejected or account-restricted: personal attributes and disease claims. A rejection costs a day. A restricted ad account costs the business.

Rule 1 — never assert you know something about the viewer

Meta prohibits copy implying knowledge of the viewer's health, age, or condition. The fix is nearly always the same: move it to first person.

Don't runRun instead
"Are you struggling with brain fog?""I was struggling with brain fog."
"Feeling tired all the time?""I was tired in a way sleep didn't fix."
"Women over 60: your energy is declining""At 62, here's what changed for me."
"Your NAD+ levels are dropping""NAD+ falls by roughly half by 60."
"You can't keep up with your grandkids""I couldn't get on the floor with my grandson."
"Is your 3pm crash ruining your day?""The 3pm crash used to end my day."
A note on the source material

The creative strategy document proposes "When brain fog and afternoon fatigue hit right in the middle of your workday" and "Feeling more tired as you age?" Both are second-person statements about the viewer's health and both carry rejection risk as ad copy. The product page can say them; the ad should not. They appear throughout this manual rewritten as "My 2:30 meetings were the problem" and "It's not just aging" — same insight, no exposure.

Rule 2 — structure/function claims only

Never saySay
prevents / delays dementia or Alzheimer'ssupports cognitive function
treats menopause or perimenopausesupports energy during midlife
cures chronic fatiguesupports healthy energy metabolism
reverses agingsupports healthy aging
clinically proven · doctor-recommendedonly with a real, citable, held study
The dementia line, specifically

The report's most emotionally loaded finding is "I'm terrified I'm losing my mind like my mother did." It is true, it is powerful, and it must never appear in an ad — naming a relative's diagnosis alongside a supplement is a disease claim and an implied treatment claim in one sentence.

Use the observable moment instead: "The word just wouldn't come. Mid-sentence. In front of my daughter." The fear is fully present. Nothing actionable was claimed.

The 60-second pre-shoot check

  1. Search the script for "you" or "your" beside any health word. Rewrite to first person.
  2. Search for any disease name. Delete it.
  3. Search for cure / treat / prevent / reverse / clinically proven / doctor recommended. Delete or substantiate.
  4. Confirm every number — 1000mg, 64%, review count, 60 days — is real and current.
  5. Confirm every document or screenshot shown on camera is genuine.
  6. Confirm the FDA disclaimer is present where required.

Dayparting — surgical, not everywhere

Your peaks are 11am EST (299 orders/hr, your single best hour) and 7pm EST (293/hr), with the 2–5pm crash window at 229–252/hr.

Do not daypart the scaling campaign

Meta requires a lifetime budget for ad scheduling, it fights Advantage+ delivery, and restricting hours on your main spend costs the algorithm room to find cheap conversions. You will usually lose more to constrained delivery than you gain from timing.

Do run one dayparted campaign

PI_MOMENT_3PM — ~$150/day, lifetime budget, scheduled 2–5pm EST, carrying only the 3pm-crash creative. It is the only asset in the account whose message is literally about the moment it's delivered in: she is living the pain point as she reads it. Nothing else gets that lift from timing, so nothing else earns the constraint.

The timing data also pays off with zero downside on launch timing, email/SMS sends (10:30am and 6:30pm EST), and budget increases — make them in the morning.

What this actually requires

The single biggest predictor of whether this plan works is whether you actually ship eight new concepts a month. At a 1-in-6 hit rate, eight concepts is 1.3 new winners per month — just enough to outpace fatigue. At three a month you are shipping half a winner, and the account declines no matter how good the strategy document is.

If capacity is the constraint, cut iterations before net-new concepts, shift the mix toward VO-montage and static concepts that need no creator booking, and cut Wave 3 depth before Wave 1 gap-fillers. Never cut C04, C06, or C03.

Your site converts well

4.38% on a $56 AOV supplement is genuinely strong. That means when an ad underperforms, the problem is almost always upstream of the page. Fix the ad, not the site.

The diagnostic decision tree

Run this on every ad that misses target. Thirty seconds, and it turns "the ad didn't work" into a specific, cheap, correct next action.

Hook < 20%The first 3 seconds failed.Nothing downstream matters. Shoot three new hooks against the same body — near-zero cost if you kept the raw footage.→ v1.1
Hold < 10%You stopped the scroll and lost her at 3–15s.Slow second beat, no turn, or the hook wrote a cheque the body didn't cash. Re-edit 3–15s only. Cut two seconds. Move the turn earlier.→ v1.2
CTR < 0.8%She watched and didn't act. You entertained her.No clear offer, weak CTA, or the ad never said what to do. New last eight seconds plus new primary text.→ v1.3
LPV < 70%Technical, not creative.Slow page, broken link, wrong destination. Check the URL and page speed.→ not a creative fix
CVR < 3%Message mismatch — the most-missed diagnosis.The ad promised something the page doesn't deliver. An ad about the 3pm crash should not land on a generic product page.→ match the page to the ad
All OKCheck frequency and overlap.Above 2.5 in the same audience is fatigue — retire it. If everything is clean, the offer isn't competitive for that audience. That's a strategy problem, not a creative one.

Row one is where the money is

A 22% hold rate behind an 18% hook rate means you have a genuinely persuasive ad that almost nobody watches. Three new hooks against existing footage costs a few hundred dollars and often doubles the ad's output. Beginners kill these. Don't.

Iteration

When a concept wins, do not go looking for a new idea. Strip-mine the winner — a single win should produce eight to fifteen assets.

StepIterationCostEffect
1Three new hooks, same body~$0 with archived footageHighest ROI move available
2Three new creators, same scriptOne booking cycleUnlocks new delivery pockets
3Length cuts — 15s / 30s / 45sEditing onlyDifferent placements reward different lengths
4Format port — video → static + carouselHalf a dayCheap incremental reach
5Awareness port — same angle, moved up or down the ladderNew scriptOpens a new audience
6Persona port — rewritten for Robert or DanaNew script + creatorOpens a new demographic

If C04 wins, that ladder yields nine assets — one production cycle's output, at a fraction of the cost and a far higher hit rate than nine cold concepts. This is the difference between an account that plateaus and one that compounds.

The rituals

Weekly — 60 minutes, Thursdays

Thursday is your launch day and your second-highest volume day, so you decide and ship in the same session.

MinStep
0–10Update the scorecard: spend, CPA, ROAS by concept
10–25Run the decision tree on every underperformer. Assign a fix code.
25–35Kill anything at $85 spent with zero purchases
35–45Scale +20–30% on anything under $28 CPA for three straight days
45–55Iterate — brief three iterations of the week's best concept
55–60Mine — log five new quotes from this week's ad comments

That last ten minutes is the compounding step. Your ad comments are a free, continuously refreshing source of the exact objections your current, colder audience holds. Most teams never read them.

Monthly — 2 hours, four pivot tables

Spend, CPA and ROAS by pillar (which territory to go deeper on), by awareness level (whether the funnel has drifted back to offers), by format (where production hours go), and by creator (who to rebook). All four are one filter away if you followed the naming convention. None are answerable if you didn't.

Then re-run the Gap Analysis — gaps reopen as you scale, and an audience you fully served last month can be under-served this month at 1.5× the budget. Refresh the Objection Ledger. Retire anything above 2.5 frequency. Recalculate the offer math: if AOV or site CVR moved, every kill threshold in this manual moved with it.

No pipelineSame four ads carrying 70%+ of spend for six weeks; everything feels like "creative fatigue"Testing budget too low. Get to 20%+ and eight concepts a month.

The fourth one is the dangerous one

Because the dashboard looks better while the business gets smaller. Offer ads to a warm audience will always show a good ROAS. They just run out of people.
