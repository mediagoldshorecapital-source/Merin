# 1. Phase 0 — The numbers that judge every ad

## 1.1 Offer math

| Input | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Meta site CVR (FB 4.37%, IG 4.40%) | **4.38%** | Shopify, 102,212 Meta sessions → 4,476 orders |
| Gross AOV | $92.21 | $494,802 ÷ 5,366 NMN orders |
| Effective discount rate | ~34% | Stabilized Feb–May |
| **Net AOV** | **$56.50** | Report; consistent with $92.21 × 0.66 |

```
Revenue per Meta session = 0.0438 × $56.50   = $2.47
Break-even CPC (1.0 ROAS)                     = $2.47
Max CPC @ 2.0× ROAS      = $2.47 ÷ 2          = $1.24
Target CPA @ 2.0× ROAS   = $56.50 ÷ 2         = $28.25
Target CPA @ 1.81× (Robert's actual)          = $31.22
```

**Print these four numbers and tape them above the desk of whoever buys media.**

Any ad running above **$1.24 outbound CPC** is not going to hit a 2.0× ROAS unless the
landing page converts above 4.4%. That is the fastest kill signal in the account and it is
available within hours of launch, long before purchase data is meaningful.

> **Missing input:** gross margin / COGS. Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ margin. At 70% margin
> break-even is 1.43×; at 80% it is 1.25×. Fill this in — it decides whether 1.81× is a good
> number or a mediocre one.

## 1.2 Bundle structure

| Bundle | Bottles | Paid | $/bottle | % orders |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buy 3 Get 3 Free | 6 | ~$100–106 | $16.66 | ~40% |
| Buy 2 Get 2 Free | 4 | ~$82–86 | $21 | ~30% |
| Buy 1 Get 1 Free | 2 | ~$47–49 | $24 | ~20% |
| Single | 1 | ~$30–38 | $30 | ~10% |

**70% of orders are 4+ bottles.** She is not buying a supplement, she is buying a
**3-to-6-month commitment**. Creative that sells "try a bottle" is selling the wrong thing to
the wrong person — it lowers AOV and it lowers belief.

**Zero discount codes were used across 4,770 orders.** She does not hunt for codes. Do not
build creative around a promo code; build it around the **anchor-vs-effective price**
($29.98 → $16.66). The math is the offer.

# 2. Phase 2.1 — The four creative tracks

Three prospecting personas and one lifecycle track. Everything gets built for one of these.

---

## P1 — "Linda B", 56–68 · THE RECLAIMER · *core persona, ~60% of creative*

**Identity.** 62. Phoenix/Tucson/Las Vegas/Houston/Miami. Retired or semi-retired — teacher,
admin, nurse, small-business. House with a yard. Husband, two adult children, four
grandchildren. AOL or Yahoo email she's had for 25 years. iPhone, and an iPad in the chair.

**Her week.** Wakes 6:30 unrested. Vitamins with coffee. Walks the dog at 8. By 11 she's in
the chair with Facebook. 3pm the floor drops out. 7pm she's back in the recliner with the TV
on and the phone in her hand.

**Her exact words (VoC):**
- *"I used to do everything. Now I sit while others do."*
- *"Tired in a way sleep doesn't fix."*
- *"I want to see my grandchildren grow up — be there, present, not fading."*

**Top insecurities (ranked):** #1 Becoming invisible · #4 Can't keep up with the grandchildren
· #6 Been scammed before · #10 Running out of time

**What she tried:** collagen, CoQ10, turmeric, a multivitamin. "Some worked a little. Most
didn't." She is ashamed of how much she spent.

**What would make her feel stupid for buying:** finding out it's underdosed, unlab-tested,
or that the "free bottles" were a trick. She reads every word looking for a reason *not* to buy.

**The scene:** 11:14 AM, iPhone, in a chair, sound off, Facebook feed, thumb moving.
Or 7:10 PM, recliner, TV on, Facebook Reels, sound sometimes on.

---

## P2 — "Dana", 48–55 · THE JUGGLER · *~15% of creative — currently unserved*

**Identity.** 51. Still working — full-time or serious part-time. Perimenopausal. Kids in
college or just launched. Possibly caring for her own aging mother. Gmail or a work domain.

**Why she's a separate persona.** Her fatigue has a *deadline attached to it*. Linda's tiredness
costs her a walk with her grandson; Dana's costs her a meeting at 2:30. She is not nostalgic,
she is **compressed**. The grandchildren angle does not land. The "career stamina" angle does.

**Her exact words (VoC / Tier-2 pain):**
- *"My body stopped listening to me."*
- *"My doctor says it's normal. Normal doesn't make it less terrifying."*
- *"I don't want to accept aging. I want my body back."*

**Top insecurities:** #5 Body stopped listening · #2 Am I losing my mind · #8 Falling behind
my friends · #3 Look older than I feel

**Hook territory:** brain fog *in the middle of the workday*, the 2:30 meeting, hormonal
crashes, "the great betrayal."

**The scene:** lunch break scroll (12–1 PM, 259–263 orders/hr), or the 2–5 PM window while
actively crashing.

---

## P3 — "Robert", 60–72 · THE REFUSER · *~15% of creative — your most efficient buyer*

**Identity.** 66. Retired professional, veteran, or tradesman. Same Sunbelt cities. Gmail or
iCloud. Buys $100+ bundles more often than Linda. **ROAS 1.81 — the most efficient
demographic in the account.** Your #1 VIP by lifetime spend (7 orders, $424) is in this group.

**His core insecurity:** *"I'm becoming my father."* He watched his dad slow down, lose his
edge, become dependent. He refuses.

**How he differs from Linda in creative terms:**

| | Linda | Robert |
|---|---|---|
| Emotional register | Validation, being seen | Refusal, control, defiance |
| Proof he wants | Reviews from women like her | Lab numbers, mg, mechanism |
| Tone | Warm, confiding | Blunt, unsentimental |
| Creator | Peer woman, unscripted | Peer man, or a credentialed male expert |
| Killer line | "It's not just aging" | "I'm not doing what my father did" |
| Objection | "Is this a scam?" | "Show me the dose." |

**This is your single biggest persona gap.** Best ROAS in the account, and the intelligence
report and the creative strategy doc both describe an account built almost entirely for
women. Every dollar of Robert creative you don't make is the cheapest revenue you're
leaving on the table.

---

## P4 — THE RETURNER · *~10% of creative — retention track*

Not a prospecting persona; a lifecycle stage with real money attached.

**The data:** repeat rate 1.0% → 2.4% → 3.0% → 3.9% → **7.8% (May, doubling MoM)**. The
Buy-3-Get-3 bundle lasts 90–120 days, so January's cohort reordered in April–May. Report
projects 12–15% by July–August *if a reorder flow exists*.

**Creative needed:** Day-75 / Day-85 / Day-95 retargeting assets, plus a "running low"
sequence. VIP pattern: $260–424 across 3–7 orders, $60–85/order. **They never downgrade the
bundle.** Never retarget a repeat buyer with the single-bottle offer.

### The awareness gap this fixes

Per the creative strategy doc, the account leans hard on **bottom-of-funnel, offer-driven
ads**. That ceiling is real: offer ads only convert people who already believe. **Target
distribution for the next 90 days:**

```
Unaware + Problem-aware   45%   ← currently the hole
Solution-aware            20%
Product-aware             25%   ← includes the "scam?" skepticism play
Most-aware                10%
```

> **This section is a worksheet, not a finding.** The META connector was not authorized in
> this session, so live Ads Manager data could not be pulled. Below are the eight gap

> brief for your next production cycle.

| # | Hypothesis | How to verify | Confirmed? | Concepts needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** | **Robert (men 60–72) has near-zero dedicated creative despite the best ROAS in the account (1.81)** | Ads Mgr → Breakdown → Age & Gender → sort by ROAS. Then count ads in your creative log that cast a male creator or use male-coded copy. | ☐ | 4 |

| **H4** | **No skepticism / anti-scam top-of-funnel exists** | Search ad names and copy for "scam", "lab", "tested", "truth". | ☐ | 3 |
| **H5** | **Creator casting skews younger than the buyer** | Watch your top 15 ads. Log apparent creator age. If median is under 50 while the buyer is 58–68, confirmed. | ☐ | 5 creators, not concepts |
| **H6** | **Tablet is the highest-converting device (5.11%) and no creative is built for it** | Ads Mgr → Breakdown → Device. Check whether 4:5 / 1:1 assets exist and whether text is legible at tablet distance. | ☐ | Re-export, not new concepts |
| **H7** | **No retention/reorder creative for the Day 75–120 window** | Check retargeting campaigns for a purchaser-based audience with reorder messaging. | ☐ | 3 |

### Where the evidence already points

Two of these are effectively pre-confirmed by the source documents:

- **H2** — the creative strategy document explicitly names over-reliance on
  bottom-of-funnel/offer-driven ads as the account's awareness gap.
- **H1** — the intelligence report devotes a full section to Robert, notes he is the most
  efficient demographic at 1.81 ROAS, and treats him as a discovery. Discoveries are, by
