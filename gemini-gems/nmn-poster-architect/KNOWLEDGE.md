### 1.2 LINDA — ENERGY · the tired one who used to run everything

62, female, sweet spot **58–68** [R]. American. Roughly 40% carry legacy email — AOL, Yahoo,
Comcast, Juno. First names cluster 1940s–1970s: Darlene, Polly, Janis, Peggy, Maureen. Retired or
working part-time. Married; her husband regards her as fine.

**Geography** [R]: Sun Belt and retirement corridor. CA 588 · FL 396 · TX 391 · NY 219 · IL 167 ·
OH 162 · GA 162 · AZ 157. Arizona is the premium market — Tucson $82 average order, Chicago $72,
Henderson $67, New York $66. Houston ($43) and Miami ($47) weakest.

**Her day, hour by hour**

| Time | What happens | Creative implication |
|---|---|---|
| 6:30 AM | Awake after eight hours, unrested | The single best problem-aware entry point |
| 7:00 | Coffee and six existing supplements she cannot attribute | Sell subtraction, never addition |
| 8:00–10:00 | Dog walk, errands, volunteering | — |
| 10:30 | Sits down and scrolls | Shorter cuts, static and carousel |
| 2:00–4:00 PM | The crash lands. She considers a nap | **The most ownable symptom in the category** |
| 6:00–7:00 PM | Recliner, TV on, tablet in hand, sound off. 291 and 296 orders — the two highest hours in the account [M] | Peak buying window |
| 11 PM–1 AM | 127–206 orders/hour [M] | Mechanism only. No offer, no urgency |

**Deep insecurities, ranked** [R]: I'm becoming invisible — she sits while others do · I can't keep
up with my grandchildren · my body stopped listening to me · I'm falling behind my friends · I've
been scammed before · I can't afford to waste money · my husband doesn't understand.

Surface pain: "I'm tired all the time." **Real pain: her body is betraying the person she still
believes she is.**

**Trigger moments.** Sitting down while everyone else keeps moving · the 3 PM crash · declining
something she'd once have said yes to · waking unrested after a full night · a grandchild's request
answered with "maybe later."

**Job to be done.** Get me back to being the one who does things, not the one who watches.

**Emotional register.** Grief, for the person she used to be, under a surface of tiredness. Not
sadness — **anger is the tell** when it surfaces.

**Casting and visual rules.** Female 58–66, never under 58. Register flat and faintly irritated —
telling you a fact about her day she has stopped questioning. Domestic locations, available light,
visible clutter. Plain beats polished.

### 1.3 LINDA — CLARITY · frightened of her own mind

⚠ A creative hypothesis, not a measured segment. Cannot be sized, costed or tied to revenue.
Survives because the fear it describes is the most acute in the research and the least addressed
in the creative — a reason to test it, not to believe it. **5% learning budget only.**

Demographically indistinguishable from Linda–Energy. The difference is motivation, not profile.
Her fear is worst at night, and overnight ordering is unusually high for this demographic.

**Deep insecurities** [R]: Am I losing my mind — she watched her mother, aunt or friend, and every
forgotten name lands as a cold wave · I'm running out of time · I'll become a burden to my children
· losing the word mid-sentence in front of people.

**Trigger moments.** The witnessed blank · the pause at the dinner table while everyone waits ·
blanking on a grandchild's name at a birthday · re-reading the same paragraph three times.

**Job to be done.** Give me a reason this is fixable and not the thing I watched my mother go
through.

**Casting and visual rules.** Female 58–66. Register: **searching, not panicking** — she is looking
for a word, not distressed. Dinner tables, kitchens, ordinary rooms. Never a clinical setting.
Never a worried family member in frame.

⚠ This persona carries the hardest compliance constraint in the whole system. See §6.

### 1.4 MARGARET — MIRROR · the best customer, barely marketed to

323 customers · 4.7% of buyers · lifetime value $98.44 (+38%) · repeat 26.9% (2×) · 4.70 units per
order. All [M]. The best-evidenced persona of the four, because buying Astaxanthin alongside NMN is
an observable event — everything about her is measured rather than inferred.

She does not usually arrive on a skin ad. She arrives on NMN and adds the second product later.
**The attach is the mechanic, not the acquisition.**

Her distinct moment is the mirror — catching her reflection in a shop window, or seeing a photo she
was tagged in. Not time-of-day bound, which makes her a retargeting opportunity.

**Deep insecurities** [R]: I look older than I feel inside — the mirror is lying, and the person she
sees does not match the person she feels like · I'm falling behind my friends · the same energy
fears as Linda, underneath.

**This is an identity crisis, not vanity.** Copy that treats it as vanity will miss — and the
failure mode is already visible in the brand's own comment threads, where a cosmetic-surgery-coded
spokesperson produced personally offended replies from exactly these women.

**Job to be done.** Make the outside match the person I still feel like inside.

**Emotional register.** Grief moving to hope. The mirror is the wound; the improvement is the reward.

**Casting and visual rules.** Female 58–68. No makeup styling, no ring light, no beauty grade, no
retouching. Register: mildly puzzled, working it out as she says it — if she sounds like she is
delivering good news, it is wrong. Her own bathroom, the real overhead light.
**Never a before/after. Ever, on this pillar.** Never lead with "tan" or "glow" — that angle returns
0.98 ROAS at $59.96 acquisition cost across 71 ads [M].

The commercial case: moving 10% of NMN-only customers into the attach adds roughly $27.34 each,
about $17,700 of lifetime value, on traffic already paid for.

### 1.5 ROBERT · spends more, stays longer, never advertised to

765 customers · 11.2% of named buyers, a floor not a count · average order $66.48 (+10%) · lifetime
value $82.55 (+16%) · 3.33 units per order. All [M].

60–72, retired professional, veteran or tradesman [R]. Gmail and iCloud rather than AOL and Yahoo.
Phoenix, Las Vegas, Houston, San Diego, Henderson.

**Deep insecurities** [R][I]: I'm becoming my father — he watched him slow down, lose his edge and
become dependent, and refuses to let it happen · I've lost a step and I'm the only one who has
noticed · I won't be a burden · recovery takes days now.

**His register is refusal, not repair.** He will not describe any of this as fear.

**Trigger moments.** A task that used to be easy taking noticeably longer · watching a peer decline
· the Saturday job costing him Sunday · reading a supplement facts panel and finding it short.

**Job to be done.** Keep me capable and independent, and don't make me ask for help.

**His language** [R] — research input for your own copy, never publishable as social proof:
"I ran out two months ago. I'm not that guy anymore." · "Stopped for 2 months. I felt tired. I felt
old." · "I can put my pants on without holding on to something." · "I felt old." · "I'll be 57 and
I can do more pushups than at 18."

**Casting and visual rules.** Male 60–72, non-actor, audibly and visibly someone who has done
physical work. Register flat, unsentimental, slightly reluctant — stating a fact he would rather
not state. No warmth. **Workshop, garage, boat trailer, ladder. Nothing shot in golden hour.
Nothing aspirational.**

**Spec converts him where story converts her.** Milligrams, purity, form, format, in the first
beat. Give him something to read, uninterrupted — that silence is the qualification.

## 4 · The three measured copy structures

From the 1,220-ad teardown. Seven of twelve all-time winners open with a direct symptom question,
three use category-quality contrast, two use a warning tied to a named mechanism. **None uses the
soft-educational or the first-person-story angle.**

### Structure 1 — direct symptom question · 7 of 12 winners
```
[Symptom question, second person] + "?"
"It's not just [dismissive explanation]…"
"[Mechanism] is [committed verb]"
"[Strong verb] with [product] and regain [benefit 1], [benefit 2] & [benefit 3]"
"[Explicit CTA] →"
```
Rotatable: symptom (tired / drained / foggy / slow to recover / waking unrested / crashing at 3 /
losing the word) · dismissal (just aging / just stress / just a busy season / what your doctor
called normal) · benefit-triad order · CTA verb.

### Structure 2 — category-quality contrast · 3 of 12 winners · BEST COST-PER-THOUSAND IN THE ACCOUNT
```
"Not all [category] is created equal."
"Most cheap ones are [specific failure: underdosed, unstable, unverifiable] — a waste of money."
"Meet [product] — [specific spec]"
```
It disqualifies aggressively: a reader who does not already believe supplements vary in quality
opts out at line one, so you stop paying for their click. **$57.47 cost-per-thousand against
$98.37 for the volume workhorse — 71% more impressions for the same money.** It has only ever run
on the 4%-of-revenue product and **never once on NMN, which is 93.5% of revenue.** The single
biggest untested opportunity in the account. Maps directly onto template T6.

### Structure 3 — warning + named mechanism · 2 of 12 winners · use sparingly
```
"WARNING: [symptom] is not just [dismissive explanation]!"
"Did you know [named mechanism] [specific decline]?"
"[Strong verb] with [product] and feel [triad] again!"
```
The winners are the ones that name a **specific** mechanism inside the warning. Broad "you're not
getting older, you're just running out of energy" variants sit materially lower.

### What loses
- **Soft educational** — 436 ads, $54,582 spent, 0.92 ROAS, **zero winners and nine losers**. Its
  click-through rate is nearly identical to the winning angles, which is exactly why it survived:
  it looks fine on engagement and fails on revenue. Hedged verbs are the disease.
- **First-person story** — 0.83 ROAS, worst on every measure. Retire it.
- **"75% OFF" in a headline** — lower conversions-per-click in five months out of five.

**The operative difference is commitment.** A claim the reader either accepts or rejects qualifies.
"…naturally drop… helps support those levels" is hedged into meaninglessness; nobody self-selects
out, so you pay for clicks from people with no urgency.

## 6 · The claim ladder

### ✅ Claimable — usable in brand voice
Supports cellular energy · supports healthy NAD+ levels (never "restores", "boosts", "raises") ·
steady energy / less fatigue, always framed as subtraction · clarity and focus, "brain fog lift" ·
exercise recovery — the most defensible territory available, named by a hostile expert as the
clearest benefit for healthy people · mood and stress support · physical capacity · morning dosing
guidance (instruction, not claim) · comparative on **specification only** — dose, form, purity,
format, cost per gram.

### ⚠ Testimonial only — attributed customer quote, never brand voice
Joint and old-injury pain · skin appearance, elasticity · sleep quality (bidirectional — pair every
mention with the timing warning) · strength and countable performance · verbal fluency and
word-finding.

### 🔴 Do not use — in any voice, including attributed
Anything naming a relative's diagnosis beside the product · anything implying replacement or
reduction of a medication · perimenopause symptom relief as stated — needs medical review before it
exists in any form · metabolic or insulin sensitivity · hair regrowth or greying reversal ·
eyesight · allergy, breathing, hangover, appetite · chronic-illness-adjacent language · "The
supplement they tried to ban."

### The verb ladder
```
restores / reverses / boosts / treats / cures / prevents   →  REJECT
improves / increases                                       →  TESTIMONIAL ONLY
supports / helps maintain                                  →  CLAIMABLE
```

### 🔴 The absolute stop — naming a diagnosis
The most powerful sentence in the entire body of research is *"I'm terrified I'm losing my mind like
my mother did."* It is true, it is powerful, and it must never run — in any format, in any voice,
attributed or not.

Naming a relative's diagnosis beside a supplement is two violations in one sentence: a disease
claim and an implied treatment claim. No disclaimer repairs it. No attribution repairs it. Putting
it in a customer's mouth makes it worse, because it becomes a testimonial to a disease outcome.

Use the observable moment instead: *"The word just wouldn't come. Mid-sentence. In front of my
daughter."* The fear is fully present, nothing actionable has been claimed.

**Name the moment, never the diagnosis:**

| ✅ The moment | 🔴 The diagnosis |
|---|---|
| The witnessed blank | dementia |
| The chair | frailty |
| The stiffness getting out of bed | arthritis |
| The lie-down after work | chronic fatigue |
| The 3 PM crash | any metabolic condition |

Every pair carries the same emotional payload and a completely different risk profile.

### Claims already flagged as failing review
| As written | Verdict | Rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| "Slows biological cellular aging" | ❌ reads as a disease/structure claim | "Supports cellular energy production" |
| "Enhances cellular insulin sensitivity" | ❌ a health claim regardless of verb | Cut entirely from ad-facing copy |
| "NAD+ drops up to 50% by middle age" | ⚠ contested inside the research corpus itself | Only with a citation you can display. Same rule covers the 64% lab-failure figure |

**A number you cannot cite on screen is a number you do not say.**

### Never say
David Sinclair's name or any of his framing · any blood NAD+ number as hero proof · "the supplement
they tried to ban" · "immediate absorption" or any unsubstantiated delivery claim · any disease
name, in any voice · anything implying medication replacement · a percentage discount in a headline.

## 8A · Differentiation axes — the working vocabulary

Concrete values to rotate between, so a batch diverges on substance rather than on adjectives.
Axes 1–5 are visual and carry almost all the perceptual weight; 6–8 are copy.

### 1 · Dominant colour field
| Value | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Light ground | `#F7F5F7` field, purple `#553C7A` type, product photographed on the surface. The cleanest read |
| Purple panel | Solid `#6B4E96` block carrying reversed white type, photograph confined to the remainder |
| Full-bleed photo | Photograph edge to edge, one floating card or one type block over it. Highest realism |
| Split field | Photograph one side, flat colour the other, seam running vertically |

Rotating this axis alone changes the thumbnail more than any other single move.

### 2 · Human presence
| Value | Note |
|---|---|
| No person — product still life | Object-led. Reads as a catalogue shot unless the staging is genuinely domestic |
| Hands only in frame | Her hands on a counter, holding the bottle, on a steering wheel. Strong: the hands must read 58–68 |
| Person soft-focus in background | Product sharp in front, her out of focus behind. The transcript's default |
| Person mid-ground, engaged | She is doing something and the product shares the frame. Highest warmth risk — hold the flat register |

### 3 · Crop and scale
Wide room (context, furniture, doorways) · mid-shot at counter or standing height · tight tabletop
(the product and two objects) · overhead flat lay (desk, planner, keys, bottle from directly above).

### 4 · Layout skeleton and bottle anchor
Template T1–T6, paired with where the bottle sits: centred · right-aligned · lower-right floating ·
inside a left column · straddling a split seam. A different template with the bottle in the same
place is only half a change.

### 5 · Setting and clock time
From §8. Never repeat a setting inside a batch, or across the previous batch in the same
conversation. Time of day should move with it — a 7:30 AM kitchen and a 2:30 PM living room are two
different pictures; a 7:30 AM kitchen and an 8:00 AM breakfast nook are one picture twice.

### 6 · Copy structure
The three measured skeletons from §4: symptom question · category-quality contrast · warning +
named mechanism. Never the soft-educational or first-person-story shapes.

### 7 · Headline shape
Short declarative, 3–5 words · question · long, 8–12 words · direct comparison. A batch where every
headline is a question is a batch with one headline in it.

### 8 · CTA verb
Shop · Discover · Reclaim · Fuel · Start · Simplify · Join · Build · Support. One per concept.

## 9 · Production rules, with the number behind each

| Rule | The measured number |
|---|---|
