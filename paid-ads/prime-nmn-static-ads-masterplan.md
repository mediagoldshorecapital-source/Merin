# PART I — THE MASTERPLAN

## 1. The strategic picture

### 1.1 What you're actually selling

Nobody over 50 wakes up wanting nicotinamide mononucleotide. The category sells **a felt return to a previous version of yourself.** NMN is the mechanism; the product is the feeling of being reliably capable again at 3pm.

Three ladders, in order of how well they convert this demo:

| Ladder | Example | Converts |
|---|---|---|
| **Ingredient** ("500mg NMN, 99% pure") | Spec-first | Weakest alone — but essential as *proof* |
| **Mechanism** ("NAD+ powers your cells' energy — and it falls with age") | Science story | Strong — this demo respects a *reason why* |
| **Outcome** ("Still going at 4pm, without the crash") | Life change | Strongest — but hollow without the other two |

**The rule: lead with Outcome, justify with Mechanism, close with Ingredient.** Most failing supplement statics do it backwards.

### 1.2 Why static, and why this audience

Static creative is *under*-rated for the 45–65 female cohort and over-dismissed by teams optimizing for a younger feed. Reasons it works here:

- This cohort reads. They will stop and consume a 60-word static; younger cohorts will not.
- Facebook feed and right-column placements still index heavily to this age group.
- Statics are cheap to iterate — you can ship 20 variants a week with one designer. Video can't match that test velocity.
- Text-heavy statics carry the *reason-why* science that this audience demands before buying an unfamiliar molecule.

**Strategic implication:** your competitive edge is **volume of tested angles**, not production polish. A brand shipping 20 rough-but-sharp statics a week beats one shipping 3 beautiful ones. Build the machine in §7 accordingly.

### 1.3 The 90-day objective

| Horizon | Goal |
|---|---|
| Days 1–30 | Find 2–3 *angles* that beat control on CTR and CPA. Establish baseline CPA and MER. |
| Days 31–60 | Find the winning *format* for each winning angle. Build the variant tree. Scale spend on proven pairs. |
| Days 61–90 | Systematize: a repeatable weekly output of 15–20 statics, a stocked winner library, and a documented iteration loop that runs without you. |

## 3. Compliance guardrails — NON-NEGOTIABLE

**Read before writing a single line.** In supplements the compliance failure mode isn't a rejected ad — it's an FTC letter, a chargeback cascade, or a disabled ad account with your winners inside it.

Three separate rulebooks apply at once: **FDA** (what a supplement may claim), **FTC** (substantiation and testimonials), **Meta** (what may be shown to a user).

### 3.1 FDA — structure/function only

You may describe how an ingredient affects the **normal structure or function** of the body. You may **not** claim to diagnose, treat, cure, prevent, or mitigate a **disease**.

| ❌ Never | ✅ Instead |
|---|---|
| "Reverses aging" | "Supports healthy aging" |
| "Prevents Alzheimer's / dementia" | "Supports cognitive function" |
| "Treats chronic fatigue" | "Supports cellular energy production" |
| "Cures brain fog" | "Supports mental clarity" |
| "Lowers your blood pressure" | *(omit entirely — cardiovascular = disease territory)* |
| "Fixes menopause symptoms" | "Formulated for women in midlife" |
| "Clinically proven to…" | "Studied for…" / cite the actual study |
| "Repairs your DNA" | "Supports cellular repair processes" |

Any structure/function claim requires the **disclaimer** on the landing page: *"These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."*

**Naming a disease anywhere in the ad — even in a testimonial, even in a review screenshot — converts a structure/function claim into a drug claim.** Screenshot review ads are the most common way brands accidentally do this. Scrub every review you feature.

### 3.2 FTC — substantiation and testimonials

- **Every claim needs competent and reliable scientific evidence, held before you run the ad.** Keep a claims-substantiation file mapping each headline to its source.
- **Testimonials must be typical, or disclosed as atypical.** "Results not typical" in 6pt grey is not a defense. If your average customer doesn't experience it, don't feature it as the hook.
- **Disclose material connections.** Paid, gifted, affiliated, employee — all must be disclosed clearly in the ad itself.
- **No fabricated reviews, no invented ratings, no fake press logos.** Featuring a logo implies endorsement; you need an actual mention and ideally permission.
- **Don't imply a study on an ingredient is a study on your product** unless your product was the thing studied. Say "NMN has been studied for X," not "Prime NMN is clinically proven to X."

### 3.3 Meta — personal attributes and health policy

The rule that kills the most supplement ads: **you may not assert or imply knowledge of a user's personal attributes** — age, health condition, medical status, or negative self-perception.

| ❌ Rejected | ✅ Approved |
|---|---|
| "Are you a woman over 50 struggling with fatigue?" | "Women over 50 are talking about NAD+." |
| "Your energy is declining." | "NAD+ levels decline with age." |
| "Tired of feeling old?" | "A daily ritual for healthy aging." |
| "Fix your brain fog" | "Support mental clarity" |
| "You don't have to feel this way" | "There's a reason afternoons get harder." |

**The mechanical fix: move from second person to third person or a general statement of fact.** "You are tired" → "Afternoon energy dips are common after 40." Same message, passes review.

Also banned in this category:
- **Before/after imagery** of any kind, and images implying body transformation.
- **Zoomed body-part close-ups** (skin, midsection).
- Depicting the product as producing an unlikely or guaranteed result.
- Scare imagery or shock framing.

### 3.4 The honesty rule (this is a performance rule, not just an ethics one)

**Give a real timeline.** Set the expectation at 4–8 weeks of consistent daily use, not "days." Overpromising on speed is the #1 driver of refunds and chargebacks in this category, and refunds destroy the MER that lets you scale. The guarantee window and your stated timeline should be consistent: if you promise results in 30 days, your 90-day guarantee reads as confidence; if you promise in 3 days, it reads as an escape hatch.

### 3.5 Pre-publish compliance gate

Nothing goes live without a second person confirming all six:

- [ ] No disease named, in headline, body, image text, **or featured testimonial**
- [ ] No second-person assertion of a personal attribute
- [ ] No before/after, no body-part close-up
- [ ] Every factual claim traced to a source in the substantiation file
- [ ] Testimonials are real, typical, and connections disclosed
- [ ] Landing page carries the FDA disclaimer and matches the ad's claim

### F1 — The Founder Letter
**What:** A block of text on a plain or lightly textured background, formatted as a personal note, with a small photo of the founder and a signature.
**Why it works:** This demo's highest-trust unit is a named human taking responsibility. It also legitimately outperforms designed ads because it doesn't look like an ad.
**Build:** Left-aligned body text, 4–6 short paragraphs, generous line spacing. Real signature (scan one). Small circular founder photo, top-left or bottom-left. Off-white background — never pure white, it flares on OLED.
**Length:** 80–140 words in-image. Yes, that's a lot. She'll read it.

> **Example — A4 × F1**
>
> **Headline (in-image):** Why I spent [X] months on a molecule most people can't pronounce
>
> **Body:**
> I'm [NAME]. I started [BRAND] after watching my mother describe her own energy as "running on 60%" — and being told by three doctors that her labs were fine.
>
> They were fine. That was the point. What declines with age often doesn't show up on a standard panel.
>
> NAD+ is a coenzyme every cell uses to make energy. Levels fall as we get older. NMN is the most direct precursor the body uses to make more of it.
>
> So we made one: [X]mg per serving, third-party tested every batch, and I'll show you the certificate.
>
> Give it 8 weeks. If you don't feel the difference, I'll refund you — [X] days, no questions.
>
> — [NAME], Founder
>
> **Primary text:** The certificate of analysis for every batch is on our site. Most brands don't publish theirs. →
> **CTA:** Learn More

### F2 — The Editorial / Advertorial Still
**What:** Designed to look like a magazine or health-publication article — headline in serif, a byline, a dateline, a lead image, and the first paragraph of body copy, often with a "continue reading" cue.
**Why it works:** Highest CTR format in this category for cold traffic, because it reads as *content*, not commerce. This demo grew up on print.
**Build:** Serif headline (Georgia, Tiempos, Freight). Byline line: `By [Name] · Health · [Month] [Year]`. Two-column feel or single wide column. Muted editorial palette. Photo of a real woman 50–60, unstyled, mid-activity.
**⚠️ Critical:** Must lead to an actual advertorial page, not a PDP. Ad-to-page congruence is the whole mechanism. And it must be **visually distinguishable from a real publication** — don't clone a masthead. That's a trademark and an FTC deception problem.

> **Example — A2 × F2**
>
> **Kicker:** CELLULAR HEALTH
> **Headline:** The molecule behind the "3 o'clock wall" — and why it declines after 40
> **Byline:** By [Name] · Reviewed by [Credential] · [Month] [Year]
> **Lead paragraph:** Researchers studying cellular energy have focused increasingly on NAD+, a coenzyme found in every human cell. Its role is fundamental — and its levels are known to fall with age. Here's what that means for the afternoons that used to be easy…
> **CTA:** Read the full article

### F3 — The Review Card
**What:** A single customer review, presented cleanly: 5 gold stars, a pull-quote at large size, the reviewer's first name + last initial + age, a small photo, and a "Verified Buyer" badge.
**Why it works:** Peer proof from a demographic match is the strongest closer for this audience. She trusts Diane, 56 more than any expert.
**Build:** ONE review, not three. Pull-quote at 40–56px. Stars in gold `#F5A623`, never yellow. Photo must be a real customer — if you don't have permission, use no photo rather than a stock face, because stock faces are recognizable and it reads as fake.
**⚠️ Compliance:** Review must be real, typical, disease-free in its language, and connection-disclosed if incentivized. Scrub it against §3.

### F4 — The Comparison Chart
**What:** A two- or three-column table. You in the left/highlighted column, "Typical NMN supplement" and optionally "NAD+ IV drip" beside it. Green checks and grey dashes.
**Why it works:** Converts the product-aware and closes the price objection by reframing the comparison set. Also does compliance-safe differentiation — you're comparing *specs*, not making claims.
**Build:** Your column visually elevated (border, tint, slight scale). 5–7 rows maximum. Rows must be **objectively verifiable** — dose, testing, price, form, guarantee. Never a subjective row like "Effectiveness."
**⚠️ Compliance:** Do not name competitor brands unless every stated fact is documented and current. "Typical NMN supplement" is safer and nearly as persuasive.

### F8 — The Timeline
**What:** Week 1 → Week 4 → Week 8 → Week 12, as a horizontal or vertical progression with what to expect at each stage.
**Why it works:** Directly answers "how long until I know?" and — critically — **sets honest expectations that reduce refunds.** A performance format that pays you back on the retention side.
**Build:** Four milestones. Progression bar or numbered nodes. **No before/after imagery** — this is a timeline of *feeling*, described in text only.
**⚠️ Compliance:** Frame as typical experience, not guarantee. "Most people report…" not "You will…"

> **Example — A9 × F8**

> **Headline:** What to expect — honestly

> - **Week 12** · Consistency compounds. This is a foundation, not a fix.

### F12 — The Handwritten Annotation
**What:** A clean base layer (product shot, chart, label) with handwritten-style arrows, circles and margin notes over it.
**Why it works:** Reads as a human explaining something to you personally. Breaks the corporate-ad pattern hard.

### F13 — The Ritual / Lifestyle Flat-Lay
**What:** Product in an authentic morning context — coffee, reading glasses, a book, morning light on a kitchen counter.
**Why it works:** Sells the identity and the ease. Low-friction, and it's your best-performing *warm* audience creative.
**Build:** Real home, not a studio. Natural light. Signs of life — a used mug, a crease in the newspaper. **Avoid the glossy stock-photo look; this demo detects and distrusts it.**

### F14 — The Stat Card
**What:** One large number or fact, dominating the frame, with a single line of context beneath.
**Why it works:** Maximum stop power at minimum production cost. Your cheapest test unit — you can ship five in an hour.
**Build:** The number at 25–40% of frame height. One supporting line, one source line in small type. High contrast, restrained palette.
**⚠️ Compliance:** The stat must be real and cited, and it must be about the **molecule or the population — never about the viewer.** Keep the source line in the image.

> **Examples — A2 × F14**

**What:** The objection as the headline, in quotes, with the answer beneath.
**Why it works:** Disarms by naming the skepticism first. Signals confidence. Converts the informed sceptic who is otherwise your hardest-won customer.

# PART II — THE SOPs

## 7. SOP-01: Weekly creative production

**Cadence:** Weekly sprint, Monday → Friday. **Output: 15–20 finished statics per week** (≈4–5 concepts × 3–4 variants).

**Roles:** Growth Lead (angles, briefs, launch) · Copywriter (may be the same person) · Designer · Compliance Reviewer (must be a *second* person)

### Monday — Research & Angle Selection (2h, Growth Lead)

1. Pull last week's performance from the tracker (§12). Identify top 3 and bottom 3 by CPA.
2. Mine fresh voice-of-customer — **30 minutes minimum, every week, non-negotiable:**
   - New product reviews since last Monday
   - Support tickets and refund reasons (refund reasons are the most valuable input you have)
   - Comments on your own live ads — *this is where the objections are*
   - Amazon reviews of competing NMN products (1★ and 3★ especially — 3★ reviews are the honest ones)
   - Reddit: r/Supplements, r/longevity, r/Menopause
3. Add any new verbatim phrases to the §2.2 language bank.
4. Select the week's build list: **60% new angles, 40% variants of proven winners.** Fill cells in the §4.2 grid.
5. Write concept briefs (template in §16.1). One per concept.

### Tuesday — Copy (3h, Copywriter)

1. For each concept, write in this order: **in-image headline → in-image body → primary text → CTA.** The in-image headline is the ad; everything else supports it.
2. Write **3 headline options per concept.** These become your variants — same design, different headline is the cleanest test you can run.
3. Self-check against §3 before handing off. Mark each headline 🟢/🟡.
4. Fill the copy doc (§16.2). Hand to Designer.

### Wednesday–Thursday — Design (2 days, Designer)

1. Build the 1:1 master first. Get it right at 1080×1080 before anything else.
2. Resize to 4:5 and 9:16 — **reflow, don't scale.** A scaled crop loses the headline.
3. Apply §8 standards. Run the legibility check: view at 25% zoom on a phone. If the headline isn't readable, the type is too small.
4. Export per §10 naming.
5. Drop into `/creative/[YYYY-WW]/` for review.

### Friday — QA, Compliance & Launch (2h)

1. Compliance Reviewer runs §9 checklist on every asset. **Reviewer must not be the person who made it.**
2. Growth Lead uploads, names per §10, sets up the test per §11.
3. Launch **Friday afternoon or Monday morning** — avoid launching into a weekend without eyes on it if budgets are meaningful.
4. Log every asset in the tracker with its angle, format, and hypothesis **before** it has results. Logging after the fact biases the record.

### The velocity rule

> If you're producing fewer than 10 statics a week, your bottleneck is process, not creativity. The most common cause is designers building from scratch each time. **Fix it with templates:** build a master file per format (§5) with locked layout and swappable text/image layers. A new stat card should take 10 minutes, not 90.

## 8. SOP-02: Design standards

### 8.1 Specs

| Ratio | Pixels | Placement | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1:1** | 1080 × 1080 | Feed (FB + IG) | Build first |
| **4:5** | 1080 × 1350 | Feed — takes more vertical real estate | Build second |
| **9:16** | 1080 × 1920 | Stories, Reels | Build third |

- **Format:** PNG for text-heavy, JPG (q85) for photo-led. Under 1MB.
- **Safe zones (9:16):** keep all text within the middle 1080×1420. Top 250px and bottom 250px get covered by UI.
- **Color profile:** sRGB. Always.

### 8.2 Typography — calibrated for 45–65 eyes

This is the single most-neglected lever for this audience. **Presbyopia is near-universal past 45.** Design for a phone held at arm's length in imperfect light.

| Element | Minimum size (at 1080px) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| In-image headline | **64px** | 72–96px preferred |
| Subhead | **44px** | |
| Body copy | **32px** | Never below 28px |
| Disclaimers / source lines | **20px** | Must still be legible — this is a legal requirement, not a design choice |

- **Line height 1.4–1.5.** Tight leading is the #1 readability killer.
- **Line length: 30–45 characters.** Long lines lose the eye on return sweep.
- **Contrast ratio ≥ 7:1** for body text. Not 4.5:1 — this audience needs more.
- **Never** light grey on white, never text over a busy photo without a scrim (60%+ opacity).
- **Sans-serif for UI-mimicking formats** (F6, F7), **serif for editorial** (F1, F2). Serif reads as credible and print-native to this cohort.

### 8.3 Imagery

- **Show women 50–62.** Not 35-year-olds with grey wigs. Not stock "active senior" imagery with the arms-crossed pose. This audience is chronically mis-portrayed and notices instantly.
- Natural light, real settings, minimal retouching. **Visible age markers are trust markers here.**
- No before/after. No body-part close-ups. (§3.3)
- Product photography: label legible at thumbnail. Test it — shrink to 150px and check.

### 8.4 Color

- Two brand colors + one accent maximum per ad.
- **Avoid clinical white-and-blue** — it reads pharma, and pharma reads "side effects" to this demo. Warm neutrals, deep greens, muted golds outperform.
- Stars always gold `#F5A623`.
- Off-white backgrounds (`#FAF8F5`), never `#FFFFFF`.

### 8.5 The 3-second test

Show the ad to someone for 3 seconds, then take it away. Ask: *what was it about?* If they can't answer, the hierarchy is broken. Most commonly the headline is competing with the product shot. **Pick one hero per ad.**

## 11. SOP-05: Testing

### 11.1 Account structure

Keep it boring. Complexity destroys signal.

```
CAMPAIGN: Prospecting — Creative Testing (CBO, $XXX/day)
  └── AD SET: Broad — US, Women, 45–65
        └── 8–12 ads (this week's batch + last week's controls)

CAMPAIGN: Prospecting — Scaling (CBO)
  └── AD SET: Broad — US, Women, 45–65
        └── proven winners only

CAMPAIGN: Retargeting
  └── AD SET: 30-day site visitors + ATC, excl. purchasers
        └── F3, F9, F15, offer creative
```

**Targeting:** Go **broad** — US / Women / 45–65 / all placements. Meta's delivery system will find the buyer better than your interest stack will, and **your creative is the real targeting.** An F14 stat card about NAD+ self-selects its audience.

Test detailed interests only as a deliberate experiment, not as a default.

### 11.2 Test design

- **One variable at a time.** Same design + 3 headlines = a clean headline test. Different design *and* different headline = an uninterpretable result.
- **8–12 ads per test ad set.** Fewer and you learn slowly; more and budget fragments below significance.
- **Always include 1–2 controls** — last cycle's winners. Without a control you can't tell a bad week from a bad ad.

### 11.3 Budget

- Testing budget: **20–30% of total paid spend.**
- Minimum per ad before judgment: **3× target CPA in spend, or 5,000 impressions**, whichever comes first.
- Don't judge on day 1. **Give it 3 days minimum** — the learning phase distorts early numbers, and this audience has a longer consideration window than the platform's default attribution assumes.

### 11.4 Decision rules

Apply after the minimum threshold in 11.3.

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| CPA ≤ target **and** spend ≥ 3× CPA | **Scale** → move to Scaling campaign, build variant tree (§13) |
| CPA 100–150% of target, CTR strong | **Hold** — give it 3 more days, or test a new hook on the same design |
| CTR strong, CPA bad | **Landing-page problem, not creative.** Ad did its job. Check ad-to-page congruence. |
| CTR weak, CPA bad | **Kill.** The hook failed. |
| Spend ≥ 3× target CPA, 0 purchases | **Kill.** No exceptions, no sentimentality. |
| Frequency > 2.5 in prospecting | Creative fatigue → refresh |

**The discipline that makes this work:** kill fast, and kill on rules rather than feelings. The ad you personally love and the ad that works are uncorrelated, and everyone learns this the expensive way.

## 12. SOP-06: Reading results

### 12.1 Benchmarks

Directional, for US women 45–65, supplement, Meta feed static. **Calibrate to your own account within 30 days** — your baseline beats any published benchmark.

| Metric | Weak | OK | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPM | >$55 | $30–55 | <$30 |
| Outbound CTR | <0.8% | 0.8–1.5% | >1.5% |
| Outbound CPC | >$3.00 | $1.50–3.00 | <$1.50 |
| LP view rate | <70% | 70–85% | >85% |
| ATC rate | <5% | 5–10% | >10% |
| Blended MER (target) | <1.5 | 1.5–2.5 | >2.5 |

*Note: CPMs for the 45–65 US female cohort run higher than platform averages — it's a competitive, high-value demographic. Don't read a $40 CPM as failure.*

### 12.2 Diagnostic table

Read symptom → cause → fix. This is the table that turns data into next week's briefs.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Low CTR, low CPM | Hook isn't landing | New angle entirely (§4.1) |
| Low CTR, high CPM | Poor creative-audience fit; Meta is struggling to place it | Change format, check imagery is demographically accurate |
| **High CTR, low LP view rate** | Slow page or bad mobile load | Fix page speed — this is not a creative problem |
| High CTR, high LP view, low ATC | **Ad-to-page mismatch** — most common failure | Make the landing page's headline echo the ad's headline |
| High ATC, low purchase | Checkout friction or price shock | Surface shipping/guarantee earlier |
| Good week 1, decay by week 3 | Creative fatigue | Variant tree (§13); check frequency |
| Strong CPA, won't scale | Narrow appeal | Broaden the angle, test new formats on the same message |
| High refund rate | **Overpromise** | Audit hooks against §3.4 — you have a speed-of-results problem |

### 12.3 The weekly tracker

One row per ad. Columns:

`Week | Ad name | Angle | Format | Hook# | Ratio | Spend | Impr | CTR | CPC | ATC | Purch | CPA | ROAS | Decision | Learning`

**The `Learning` column is the point of the whole exercise.** One sentence, written the week you kill or scale the ad. "Stat cards with a dollar figure beat stat cards with a percentage." Six months of these is a genuine strategic asset and the thing a competitor can't copy.

## 13. SOP-07: Iterating a winner

When an ad hits Scale (§11.4), do **not** just raise its budget and move on. Winners are seeds.

### The variant tree — 6 variants from every winner

Change **one** thing per variant:

1. **Hook swap** — same design, new headline (from §6). *Highest hit rate. Always do this one first.*
2. **Format swap** — same message, different format (A2×F14 → A2×F11)
3. **Proof swap** — swap the featured review, stat, or badge
4. **Visual swap** — same layout and copy, new photo or background
5. **Offer swap** — lead with the guarantee vs. the subscribe discount
6. **Length swap** — condense a text-heavy winner, or expand a sparse one

### Fatigue management

- Watch **frequency** and 3-day rolling CTR. When CTR drops 25%+ from its peak, refresh.
- Refresh means **ship variants**, not pause. Pausing a winner loses its delivery history.
- Keep 3–5 winners live at all times so no single ad carries the account.

### The escalation ladder

```
Winning ad → Winning ad + 6 variants → Winning FORMAT (apply to 4 other angles)
           → Winning ANGLE (apply across 6 formats) → Winning MESSAGE
           → becomes the brand's positioning on-site and in email
```

**The end state:** your best-performing ad angle should eventually rewrite your homepage. Paid social is the fastest message-testing lab you own — most brands never carry the learning back upstream.
