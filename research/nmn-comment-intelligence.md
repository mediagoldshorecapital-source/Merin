## 1. Read the room

This corpus is the comment section of NMN **debate** videos — Sinclair explainers, Thomas DeLauer sponsorships, Dr Brad Stanfield debunkings, third-party purity tests, FDA-ban news.

**They are not unaware — they are adjudicating.** They already use `NAD+`, `precursor`, `salvage pathway`, `TMG`, `sublingual`, `third-party tested`. They cite Igarashi 2022, Yi 2022, Yoshino 2021 by name. Awareness-stage advertising ("did you know NAD+ declines?") talks beneath them.

**The dominant emotion is betrayal, not hope.** They bought in on Sinclair, then watched him get accused of lobbying to make NMN a prescription drug. They turned to the skeptic, then watched his own multivitamin get accused of failing a lab test.

> "Dr Stanfield you have a shop too so why should we believe you when you have a financial interest too?"
> "It's all grifters calling out grifters to sell their own grift."

**Consequence:** credibility cannot be borrowed from a scientist, doctor or influencer in this category. Every human authority here has been publicly compromised in front of this exact audience. What survives is documents — lab reports, batch numbers, named labs, plain arithmetic.

## 3. Seven kinds of skeptic

Skepticism is not one objection. A creative that answers one can inflame another.

### 1. The Reductionist — 279 comments
> "NR and NMN are extremely expensive sources of NA and NAM. Nothing more."

Believes gut bacteria break NMN into plain niacin, so it's 15× overpriced B3.
**Don't** argue enzymology in a feed ad — you can't win the NAMPT-bottleneck debate in 30 seconds.
**Do** move to ground you can win: the flush. Many have *tried* megadose niacin and hated it. "No flush, no 3-gram megadose, no rash" is verifiable tonight.

### 2. The Counterfeit Cynic — 167 comments
> "Testing revealed the majority of NMN sold on Amazon is counterfeit or contains virtually no active ingredient."

Cites the ChromaDex figures from memory — 64% under 1% claimed content, 14% with zero.
**Most winnable objection in the category and the least served.**
**Do** make purity the hero, not longevity: batch number on bottle, QR to the actual COA, name the lab, show the assay, publish failures too.

### 3. The Evidentialist — 438 comments
> "Ok but has any study on humans shown that higher NAD actually means people are better off?"

Mice aren't people. Blood NAD+ isn't tissue NAD+. Raising a biomarker isn't a benefit.
**Do** cite honestly *including the limits*. This buyer converts on calibration, not enthusiasm. A "what we can't claim yet" section out-credentials every competitor instantly.

### 4. The Conflict Detector — 127 comments
Anyone who profits is disqualified. Sinclair, Stanfield, DeLauer, the sponsored reviewer — all compromised.
**Do** pre-empt in your own voice: say you sell it, say what the margin pays for, point to a third party you don't control.
**Never** use a white coat, a stethoscope, or an unnamed "leading longevity researcher."

### 5. The Disconfirmed Buyer — 37 comments
> "I've taken this for months and I feel nothing at all. It may work for some, but I will not be buying again."

**Do** give them a mechanism, never a contradiction: dose too low, no methyl support, degraded product, already-sufficient NAD+, wrong window. Then refund without a fight — the corpus shows refunded customers still argue *for* the category.
**Asset:** "Five reasons NMN did nothing for you." This page does not exist anywhere.

### 6. The Placebo Self-Doubter — 96 comments
> "It's not placebo — I didn't notice the benefits until I stopped taking it."

They invented their own test: **stop taking it and see what comes back.** It recurs constantly and is the market's own gold standard of proof.
**Do** name the test and invite it: "Take it 60 days. Then stop for ten. That's the only review we care about."

### 7. The Contrarian — 166 comments (tailwind, not barrier)
The five highest-liked comments in the whole corpus are FDA-ban posts — 1,000 / 615 / 610 / 586 / 574 likes.

> "When I heard the FDA wanted to ban it, I knew instantly it would be effective."
> "The fact they're trying to convert it into a prescription says a lot about its potential."

**Do** use availability and supply framing — "still available," "we hold inventory," "stock up."
**Careful:** don't make the conspiracy claim yourself. Meta rejection risk and a brand ceiling.

## 4. The psychology underneath

**The verification vacuum.** NMN asks a buyer to accept three unverifiable things at once: they can't feel it, can't see it, and can't confirm the powder is the molecule on the label. No other supplement stacks all three. Every anxiety here routes back to it. *The brand that collapses the verification gap owns the category.*

**Authority collapse.** Enthusiasm now reads as a sales tell; specificity reads as truth. This is why "Thomas, you never actually said what happened to *you*" is one of the most-liked patterns in the set (558 likes on one instance).

**Folk-science proof rituals.** Denied trustworthy institutions, the market built its own evidence formats — and these are the native language of proof:
- **Stop-and-restart** — "when I stop taking it, the arthritic pain comes back"
- **Physical benchmark** — "I'm 57 and can do more pushups than at bootcamp at 18"; mile times, stairs, pickleball, grip
- **Before/after bloodwork** — A1c, ApoB, plasma NAD+, biological age
- **The pet as control** — "the dog can't have a placebo effect," used sincerely and repeatedly

**Age as identity gate.** "You're too young to need this" is a put-down; being visibly old is the thing they're fleeing. Copy must let them claim decline without claiming oldness. Say "NAD+ falls roughly 50% between 40 and 60." Never say "for seniors."

**Protocol anxiety after purchase.** Refrigerate or not, sublingual or swallow, with fat or empty, TMG or not, cycle or daily. These fire *after* the card is charged — they drive non-adherence, churn and refunds. Protocol content is a retention lever with a revenue number, not customer-service overhead.

**Public cost-per-gram arithmetic.** They do the math out loud and accurately: "$50 for 60 caps at 250 mg = 15 days at Sinclair's dose = $120/month." "Sinclair's own brand is 15–20× the cost of third-party tested NMN by content weight." Publishing cost-per-gram makes premium brands lose the comparison for you.

## 6. Nine ideas worth building on

1. **Sell the certificate, not the molecule.** Batch number on every bottle, QR to the actual PDF, named lab, assay result, heavy metals, mycotoxins. Not a badge — the document.
2. **Reframe "does NMN work" to "are you a responder."** Sidesteps an unwinnable argument, is more honest, and is already how the smartest commenters talk. Operationalise it: defined trial period, specific protocol, stated signals, no-argument refund.
3. **Tell an honest timeline.** Reported onset clusters at 2–8 weeks; sleep and morning alertness first, recovery and stamina later. Under-promising lowers refunds and reads as truth.
4. **Own "why you felt nothing."** Zero brands have this page. Highest-intent skeptical search traffic in the category.
5. **Publish cost-per-gram.** Turns their arithmetic habit into your acquisition channel.
6. **Be the stack coach, not the pill seller.** TMG, timing, fat, storage, cycling. Highest-volume unanswered questions in the data; makes bundle attach a consequence, not an upsell.
7. **Make the cold chain visible.** Shipping heat is a live, unaddressed fear with real refund consequences. If the product is heat-stable, that's a headline.
8. **Recruit the stop-and-restart testimonial.** Ask reviewers one question: "what happened when you ran out?"
9. **Segment creative by decade.**

| Band | Their frame | Proof they want | Hook territory |
|---|---|---|---|
| 38–49 | "I'm slipping and nobody warned me" | Gym recovery, mile time, 3pm focus | Performance, brain fog, day after the workout |
| 50–64 | "I want my thirties energy back" | Stairs, sleep, soreness, bloodwork | Stop-and-restart, "the beast came back" |
| 65+ | "I want to stay independent" | Gait speed, grip, gardening, grandkids | "Getting my life back," mobility, morning ramp-up |

### Three UGC scripts

**A · The stop-and-restart**
1. *0–3s* "I ran out of my NMN for nine days. That's how I know it works."
2. *3–10s* Age + one specific baseline. "I'm 54. Before this I was done by two in the afternoon."
3. *10–20s* What came back during the gap — named, physical, unglamorous. Stairs. The 3pm wall. Waking up foggy.
4. *20–28s* "Four days back on it and it settled again."
5. *28–36s* Bottle in hand, batch number visible. "A full gram, and I can pull up the lab report on the label."
6. *CTA* "Try it 60 days, then stop for ten. That's the test."

**B · The certificate**
1. *0–3s* "Most NMN on Amazon isn't NMN." Hold up an unbranded generic bottle.
2. *3–12s* Cite the third-party testing findings. Under 1% content. Some with zero.
3. *12–22s* "So here's the only thing I check now." Scan the batch code, show the COA loading on the phone.
4. *22–32s* Point at three lines: identity, potency, heavy metals. Say the lab's name out loud.
5. *CTA* "Don't take my word for it. Take the assay."

**C · The niacin argument**
1. *0–4s* "Every NMN video has the same comment: just take cheap niacin."
2. *4–12s* Concede properly — niacin raises NAD+ and it's cheap. Say so. Conceding buys the next ten seconds.
3. *12–24s* The flush. The megadose. The reason people quit it. Personal and specific.
4. *24–34s* "So this is what I do instead. One gram, no flush, tested."
5. *CTA* "If niacin works for you, genuinely — keep going. If you bounced off it, this is the other road."

### Product page order
1. Dose per serving in mg, above the fold; servings per container beside it
2. Cost per gram, calculated for them
3. Batch number + live COA link (not a badge)
4. "Do I need TMG?" answered on the page
5. Storage and shipping-heat answer, explicitly
6. Timeline expectation: what shows up when
7. Refund terms in one sentence a skeptic can trust

### Post-purchase flow (Klaviyo)
- **Day 0** — How to take it: timing, food, storage
- **Day 2** — The TMG question, answered honestly either way
- **Day 7** — "You probably don't feel anything yet. Here's why that's expected." Biggest churn-prevention email available
- **Day 21** — What to watch for, in their language: morning ramp-up, the 3pm wall, day-after soreness
- **Day 45** — Introduce the stop-and-restart test as the brand's own confidence
- **Day 60** — Review request with one question: "What changed, and what came back when you ran out?"

### Test first
Three tests only: **certificate-led** vs **full-gram/dose-math-led** vs **stop-and-restart-led**. They map to the three largest distinct clusters in the data and will tell you which axis the brand should own.

## 8. Guardrails

**The most persuasive quotes in this corpus are unusable.** The highest-emotion testimonials describe reversing Alzheimer's, resolving lung scarring, curing arthritis, and recovering from cancer treatment. Running any of them turns a supplement into an unapproved drug claim for the FTC and FDA.

Keep to structure/function language: *supports cellular energy*, *supports healthy NAD+ levels*, *supports healthy aging*. Screen every piece of UGC for disease language before it goes live, including comment replies and creator briefs — a claim made by your paid creator is your claim.

- **Substantiate testimonials.** Keep raw review, date and consent on file for anything run as an ad. Add typicality language where results are unusual.
- **Don't run the conspiracy.** FDA reactance is a real tailwind, but "Big Pharma is hiding this" in your own copy is a Meta rejection risk and a brand ceiling. Use availability framing.
- **Don't borrow Sinclair.** Naming him is now roughly as likely to cost trust as build it with this audience, and it invites the lobbying argument into your comments.
- **Only claim what your COA says.** "99% purity" and "third-party tested" are powerful because they're checkable — which means they must survive being checked, every batch.
- **Health-claim review before scale.** Have counsel review the evidence page and responder-guarantee wording once.
