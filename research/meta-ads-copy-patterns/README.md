# Competitor Meta Ads Copy Teardown — Longevity & Supplements

Analysis of **562 unique ads** (203 distinct copy blocks) from **22 advertisers**, pulled
from the Facebook Ad Library across 12 exports.

**Purpose:** find the repeatable copy frameworks these brands actually spend money on, so
Prime Ingredients can build ads on proven structures instead of guessing.

## 2. Structural fingerprint of the category

Measured across all 203 unique copies (and weighted by number of live ads):

| Feature | % of copies | % of ads |
|---|---:|---:|
| Second person ("you / your") | **72.9%** | **78.2%** |
| Mentions NAD+ | 44.8% | 41.4% |
| Contains a % or hard number | 24.1% | 25.5% |
| Question in the hook line | 21.7% | 25.8% |
| Offer / promo code | 18.2% | 16.7% |
| Emoji checkmark stack (✅ / ❌ / ⚡) | 17.7% | 26.1% |
| Proof credential (clinical, third-party, COA, NSF) | 16.3% | 15.3% |
| "Declines with age" mechanism stated | 13.3% | 17.2% |
| Explicit CTA line in body | 10.8% | 12.4% |
| Hashtags | 10.3% | 6.2% |
| Borrowed authority (TIME, Vogue, Huberman, MD) | 8.4% | 8.9% |
| **First-person testimonial voice** | **7.9%** | **9.7%** |
| Competitor takedown | 2.5% | 2.7% |

**Length:** median **342 characters**, mean 380. The winners cluster between **200 and 600
characters** — roughly 2–5 short paragraphs. Only 11% go over 600, and those are almost all
creator/advertorial posts.

**Non-negotiable mechanics every long-runner shares:**

1. **Line 1 is a standalone sentence, then a blank line.** Meta truncates at ~125 characters
   behind "See more." Every 60+ day ad puts the entire idea before that cut.
2. **One idea per ad.** Not one ad covering five benefits — five ads covering one benefit each.
3. **Emoji are functional, never decorative.** ✅ = benefit, ❌ = what's wrong with competitors,
   ⚡⏳🧠💪 = visual bullets, 👉 = the CTA line. Never mid-sentence.
4. **Compliance verbs.** "Supports", "helps support", "designed to", "studied for",
   "may help." Never cures/treats/prevents. Elysium and NOVOS append the FDA disclaimer;
   NOVOS even discloses "AI-generated performer — not a real person" and adds
   "individual results will vary" to testimonial-style claims. Copy this — it's cheap
   insurance for the ad account.
5. **Volume is the strategy.** Renue runs ~90 distinct copies across ~6 SKUs. Omre runs
   4 core hooks across 40+ creative permutations. They are testing hooks, not polishing one.

## 3. The frameworks

Ten repeatable structures, ranked by how much money the category clearly puts behind them.

---

### Framework 1 — The Mechanism Ladder ★ the category's workhorse

**Used by:** Jinfiniti, Omre, Wonderfeel, Elysium, Renue, NOVOS. This is the single most
repeated structure in the dataset.

The insight: the customer knows the *symptom* but not the *cause*. You sell them the cause,
and the product becomes the only logical response.

```
1. SYMPTOM     Name 2–3 specific, physical, daily symptoms in their own words
2. ABSOLVE     Tell them it isn't their fault / isn't discipline
3. MECHANISM   Reveal the hidden biological cause (name the molecule)
4. STAKES      "When it drops, nothing else you do matters"
5. RESOLVE     Product addresses it at the source
6. CTA         Soft next step
```

**Live example — Jinfiniti (ran 33 days, plus 3 near-identical variants):**

> You've done everything the health experts told you to do. And you still hit a wall every
> afternoon that three cups of coffee can't fix.
>
> You still wake up tired after a full night of sleep.
>
> Your brain feels like it's wading through fog before noon.
>
> Here's what nobody talks about: as we get older, our cells stop producing the energy they
> used to. Not because you're doing something wrong. Because there's a cellular fuel called
> NAD+ that depletes naturally with age.
>
> When it drops, it doesn't matter how disciplined you are. Your body just can't keep up.
>
> The people who fix this don't work harder. They find out what's actually causing it.
>
> Click the link to see how easy it is to measure your NAD+ levels and get back your energy.

**Live example — Omre (16 creative variants, their highest-volume copy):**

> Feeling slower, foggier, and more tired as you age? That's declining NAD+ at work.
> Omre's NMN + Resveratrol gives your cells the fuel they need to bounce back.
> ✅ Boosts energy ✅ Sharpens focus ✅ Support healthy aging
> Thousands of customers say it changed their life. Will you be next?

Note Omre compresses the same six steps into 40 words. Same ladder, short form.

**Why it works:** it converts a vague complaint ("I'm tired") into a specific, measurable,
*fixable* thing. And the absolution step ("not because you're doing something wrong")
removes shame, which is the actual barrier to purchase in the aging category.

---

### Framework 2 — The Absolution Hook ★ highest-longevity hook in the set

A sub-move of Framework 1, but so powerful it earns its own slot. **Jinfiniti's version ran
94 days — the longest-running non-creator ad in the dataset.**

```
[Symptom] isn't [the normal explanation].
It's not "[excuse people give themselves]."
It's not "[the other excuse]."
It's [your mechanism].
```

> **The afternoon crash isn't normal. It's not "just being a mom." It's not "just getting
> older." It's your cells running on empty.**
>
> A free 2-minute quiz from Jinfiniti Labs shows you exactly what's draining your energy and
> what to do about it 👉 [link]

Second version, 62 days:

> Brain fog. Bad sleep. No energy. Joints that complain. You're not lazy. You're not "just
> stressed." Something measurable is happening inside your cells.

**The mechanic:** naming and then *rejecting* the excuse the reader has been telling
themselves. It's pattern-interrupt plus permission. Note the quotation marks — they're
quoting the reader's inner voice back at them.

### Framework 3 — The Category Callout (differentiate through distrust)

**Used by:** ProHealth (124 days), Renue (multiple, 7–70 days), Tru Niagen, NOVOS.

The buyer has been burned or suspects they will be. You don't argue you're good — you argue
the *category* is bad, then prove you're the exception.

```
1. ATTACK      "Most [category] doesn't live up to the hype"
2. ❌ STACK     3–4 specific, checkable failures
3. ✅ STACK     Your mirror-image proof, same order
4. STAKES      "If you take your health seriously, this should matter"
5. RISK OFF    Guarantee that removes the last objection
```

**ProHealth, 124 days:**

> Most NAD supplements don't live up to the hype—and they're often built on zero real science.
>
> ❌ Fake purity certificates
> ❌ Cheap fillers that dilute potency
> ❌ Ingredients that don't match the label
> ❌ Only support NAD in one way
>
> At ProHealth Longevity, we do things differently:
>
> ✅ Real-time purity reports you can read yourself
> ✅ No fillers—just research-backed ingredients
> ✅ Formulated by top-tier longevity scientists and doctors
> ✅ Boosts NAD in three distinct ways for maximum impact
>
> If you take your health seriously, this should matter.
>
> Try NAD Triple Boost Ultra™ risk-free for 100 days. Not satisfied? Return it—**even if the
> bottle's empty**—for a full refund.

**Renue's softer variants of the same idea:**

> Line up two NMN products and they may look the same on the outside. The difference is
> everything you can't see.

> These days, it's easy to make things look legit on the surface, but when you cut into them,
> surprise—they might just be fake (or cake!). Your NMN shouldn't pull the same trick.

> How pure is the NMN you're taking? Most people pay premium prices without really knowing
> what's in the bottle.

**Critical detail:** the ❌ items are *specific and verifiable* (fake certificates, filler,
label mismatch), not vague ("low quality"). Specificity is what makes the ✅ side credible.
And the empty-bottle guarantee is the single strongest risk-reversal in the whole dataset —
it ran 124 days and nobody else in the set copied it.

### Framework 4 — The Proof Stack (credential dump)

**Used by:** Tru Niagen (their entire strategy), Elysium, NOVOS.

For an expensive, skeptical, research-y purchase. One line of thesis, then a wall of
verifiable credentials, then the offer.

```
1. THESIS      One line: "you've heard of us — here's why it's worth it"
2. CLAIM       The hard number, up front
3. ✅ STACK     Award / clinical claim / study count / certification / eligibility
4. QUOTE       A named authority who bought it themselves
5. OFFER       Subscription discount
```

**Tru Niagen (25 days), the fullest expression:**

> Most NAD+ supplements make big claims. Tru Niagen backs them. Trusted by leading
> researchers, pro athletes, and healthcare professionals worldwide.
>
> Clinically proven to raise NAD+ levels by up to 150%, supported by 45+ peer-reviewed human
> studies, NSF Certified for Sport, and backed by a Nobel Prize-winning biochemist — it's the
> rare supplement where the science is transparent and verifiable.
>
> "I do take Tru Niagen — they don't pay me, I buy it." — Andrew Huberman, Huberman Lab
> podcast. Dr. Rhonda Patrick takes it too.
>
> 🏆 Healthy Aging Ingredient of the Year '24
> ✔️ Clinically proven to increase NAD+ by up to 150%
> ✔️ Activates cellular energy and DNA repair
> ✔️ Backed by 45+ human clinical studies & 25+ years of science
> ✔️ The most efficient, safe, well-researched NAD+ booster
> 💊 HSA/FSA eligible
> 🔁 Subscribe to get the best discount + free shipping on every order

**The best line in the entire dataset:** *"they don't pay me, I buy it."* An endorsement that
pre-empts the exact objection it triggers. If you can ever get a real one of these, it's worth
more than the rest of the ad.

**Elysium's restrained version** shows the same skeleton at half the length:

### Framework 5 — Borrowed Authority / Third-Party Verdict

**Used by:** Wonderfeel (this *is* their paid strategy), Elysium, Tru Niagen.

You don't claim to be the best. You quote someone else calling you the best, verbatim, with
the @handle, and then thank them. The gratitude framing makes the brag socially acceptable.

```
1. THE VERDICT   "[Publication] named us [superlative]"
2. THE QUOTE     Verbatim pull-quote in quotation marks
3. THE EXPERT    A named MD/PhD attached to the verdict
4. THE THANKS    "Thank you @publication"
5. HASHTAGS      Category tags
```

**Wonderfeel, 14 days, one of many:**

> "The best of the best according to longevity experts," says @Vogue about Wonderfeel Youngr™
> and calls it "the best NMN-based NAD supplement."
>
> Vogue further notes that "(they) love the patented blend of NMN, vitamin D, resveratrol, and
> olive antioxidant super-ingredient hydroxytyrosol."
>
> "The brand has an impressive list of medical advisors and scientific experts on board," says
> Amanda Kahn MD, a medical expert consulted by Vogue for this article.
>
> Thank you, @Vogue and @amandakahnmd for the kind words.

Wonderfeel ran this same shape for Vogue, New York Post, Entrepreneur, mindbodygreen, the
Innocos Longevity Summit award, and Inc. 5000. **One PR win = a whole quarter of ad creative.**

**The creator channel is the other half of this framework.** The longest-running single ad in
the entire dataset (**117 days**) is a whitelisted MD creator post for Wonderfeel:

> The importance of NAD for energy and mitochondrial health for women in menopause
> […] My favorite is Youngr by Wonderfeel. Two morning tablets deliver 900 mg of NMN…
> This video is sponsored by Wonderfeel, but all content is my own.

Dr. Caroline Leaf (27d), Doctor Motley (27d), and Biohack-it (26d) run the same pattern:
podcast-episode framing, expert teaches the mechanism, product mentioned as "my favorite,"
sponsorship disclosed. Renue does the beauty-creator equivalent (Iryna, Lena, Sav.aesthetics,
20 days each) — routine-walkthrough posts with @renueblue tagged and hashtag blocks.

### Framework 6 — The Concrete Analogy (make an invisible mechanism visible)

**Used by:** Renue (their signature), NOVOS.

The hardest problem in this category: nobody can *feel* a molecule. So every abstract
mechanism gets one vivid, concrete image. This produced some of Renue's longest runners.

```
[Familiar, physical, slightly absurd comparison]
→ [therefore the product logic is self-evident]
→ [product]
```

The best of them:

| Copy | Mechanism it makes visible |
|---|---|
| "**130 bottles of wine = 1 clinical dose of resveratrol.** Your liver would quit before your cells got benefits." (70d) | Dose adequacy |
| "Getting these nutrients from whole foods alone would mean eating **5,000 strawberries, 1,000 cups of blueberries, 20+ pounds of leafy greens** — every single day. We'll pass." (NOVOS) | Dose adequacy |
| "**Nobody insures a house after the fire.** We protect valuables and prepare for the trip before we take it, because protection only counts when it comes first." | Why prevention, not treatment |
| "**You wouldn't choose between food and water.** So why choose between NMN and NR?" | Why a combo formula |
| "Choosing one precursor is like choosing whether your house should have **a roof or a foundation**." | Why a combo formula |
| "Think of your body like **a city** — NMN supports the Focus District, trigonelline is favored by the Performance District…" | Multi-pathway coverage |
| "It's easy to make things look legit on the surface, but when you cut into them, surprise — they might just be **fake (or cake!)**." | Purity testing |
| "**Your skin can't write a shopping list.** But if it could, science would probably make the cut." | Ingredient selection |
| "Your skincare routine stops at your skin's surface… **That's why you drink it. Drink your skincare.**" (NOVOS, 83d) | Oral vs topical |

**Why it works:** it turns a claim the reader must *trust* into an argument they can *check
themselves*. "130 bottles of wine" is arithmetic, not marketing. This is the highest-skill
framework in the set and the one with the least competition.

### Framework 7 — The Objection-as-Headline

**Used by:** Renue, extensively (~10 variants of one question).

Take the single question your category's buyers google most, make it the hook, then dissolve
the choice so your product is the only answer.

> **NMN or NR for boosting NAD+?**
> A better question: Why choose between them?
> There are four biochemical pathways that can help your cells generate NAD+…
> That's why we've included all of them in NAD+ Complete. Because we care about your biology,
> not hyping one ingredient over another.

> **Tired of trying to figure out if NMN or NR is better for your daily longevity routine?
> You don't have to choose anymore.**

> **Stop guessing when it comes to NAD+ supplements.** Instead of thinking NMN or NR, think
> NMN *and* NR. And trigonelline. And direct NAD+.

They ran this shape a dozen ways over months. The final beat is always the same: *reframe the
either/or as a false choice your product already solved.*

### Framework 8 — Segment-Targeted Opening (same product, different door)

**Used by:** ProHealth (122 days, 14 variants), Omre, NOVOS, Wonderfeel.

The first six words name a specific person, not a benefit. Same SKU, different entry point,
**matched to a different landing page.**

- "**For men who care about staying sharp and active past 40**, ProHealth NMN isn't hype…" (122d, their biggest bet)
- "**For high performers**, even small slips are hard to ignore." (Omre)
- "**On GLP-1?** By the time skin looks like it has changed, the change has been quietly underway…" (NOVOS)
- "**Over 25?** This is when things start to shift." (Wonderfeel)
- "The **athletes still performing at high levels in their 40s** aren't doing different training."
- "**If you're serious about performance**, your stack probably keeps growing…" (Renue)

Omre's execution is the one to steal: one product, four segment hooks, four matching landing
pages — `/first-order-energy`, `/mobility-and-independence`, `/first-order-fitness`,
`/7-reasons-you-start-losing-your-edge`. The hook and the LP tell the same story. That's why
one product can support 80 live ads.

### Framework 9 — Offer as News (reason-why pricing)

**Used by:** ProHealth (122–124 days), Double Wood (63–133 days), Tru Niagen, Renue.

A discount without a reason reads as desperation. A discount *with* a mechanism reads as news.

> The only thing that just changed? **The price. We cut it by up to 25% — effective
> immediately.** If you've been waiting for the right time to start — this is it! (122d)

> And now, for the first time, it just got a lot more affordable. **We renegotiated our supply
> agreement and passed the savings directly to you.** Up to 25% lower prices — effective
> immediately. **Same formula. New price.** (19d)

Two details worth copying:

- **"Effective immediately" + "Same formula. New price."** kills the "did they cheapen it?"
  objection in five words.
- **Promo codes are named per product line, not generic:** `AGELESS` (NMN), `GLOWDEEPER`
  (serum), `BERB10` (berberine), `HEALTHYAGING` (resveratrol). The code itself carries
  positioning and lets you attribute revenue per angle.

Double Wood's simplest version — `Use Code DWS2025 for 25% OFF your first order!` as the
literal first line — ran 63 days across three product feeds. For retargeting and DPA, offer-first
beats story-first.

### Framework 10 — The Qualification Funnel (soft CTA / micro-commitment)

**Used by:** the ED telehealth cluster (Medvi, Clark Reilly, Modern Men's Health), and
Jinfiniti in the longevity set.

The most transferable finding in this whole teardown: **for cold traffic, the highest-longevity
ads don't ask for a purchase. They ask for a quiz.**

- "👉 Tap below to take the **assessment** and see if **you qualify**." (58 days, 19 variants)
- "Take the quick **qualification quiz** 👇" (Medvi)
- "👉 **See if you qualify** in 3 minutes." (Modern Men's Health)
- "A **90-second quiz** from Jinfiniti Labs reveals which of your systems are aging fastest…
  You'll get a personalized profile at the end. **Free.**" (86 days)
- "A free **2-minute quiz**… shows you exactly what's draining your energy." (94 days)

The structure of the ED cluster's testimonial (58 days, run across 19 creative variants) is
worth reading as a whole, because it's a masterclass in sequencing:

> **I honestly can't believe how fast this worked.** ← result first, no setup
>
> One dose under my tongue and 10 minutes later I felt like I was 25 again. ← specific time
>
> The assessment took a few minutes and I was approved right away. No doctor visit, no
> pharmacy, nothing. ← process is easy
>
> Delivered straight to my door in a plain package. ← removes shame
>
> My wife says it's like the first night we met 😏 ← third-party validation
>
> 👉 Tap below to take the assessment and see if you qualify. ← soft CTA

Every line does one job, in the order a skeptical buyer needs them. Notice the CTA is a
*qualification*, not a purchase — it converts the click into "am I eligible?" curiosity rather
than "do I want to spend money?" resistance.

**Why the quiz wins:** it's a lower-friction conversion event, it feeds Meta's algorithm more
signal, it segments the lead before you sell, and the personalised result becomes the sales
argument. Three of the longest-running ads in the dataset (94d, 86d, 58d) all end in a quiz.

## 4. The gaps — where the whole category is weak

These are unoccupied positions. Each is an opportunity for Prime Ingredients.

| Gap | Evidence | The opening |
|---|---|---|
| **First-person testimonial copy** | Only **7.9%** of longevity copies use "I / my". The ED advertisers do, and theirs ran 52–58 days across 19 variants. | Real customer-voice ad copy in the supplement category is nearly vacant. Renue outsources it entirely to creators. |
| **Time-to-result specificity** | Almost nobody promises a window. Renue says "Try it for 30 days" once. The ED cluster says "10 minutes" and runs forever. | "Here's what week 1, week 4, week 12 look like" — nobody is doing the honest timeline. |
| **Risk reversal** | Exactly **one** guarantee in 203 copies (ProHealth's 100-day empty-bottle) — and it ran 124 days. | A strong, specific guarantee is nearly free differentiation here. |
| **Cost-per-day / price anchoring** | Only Omre ("Under $2 a day") and NOVOS ("5,000 strawberries"). | Anchor against the alternative — a latte, a facial, a collagen powder that under-doses. |
| **Founder story** | Zero in the dataset. | Origin story = the one thing competitors literally cannot copy. |
| **Ingredient education as the hook** | Everyone leads with the molecule's *decline*; almost nobody teaches *how to evaluate* one. | "How to read a collagen label" style ads own the top of funnel and pre-frame you as the honest option. |

## 6. How to deploy this

1. **Build the hook bank first, not the ad.** Write 20 hooks (line 1 only) across
   frameworks 1, 2, 3, 6 and 8. Kill on hook performance before you write full bodies.
2. **One idea per ad.** Five ads about one benefit each beats one ad about five benefits.
3. **Front-load everything into the first 125 characters.** That's the "See more" cut.
4. **Match hook → landing page.** Omre's 80 live ads are one product with four segment
   doors and four matching pages. Segment-hook ads that dump into a generic PDP leak.
5. **Test the soft CTA against the hard one.** Quiz/assessment CTAs hold three of the top
   spots for longevity in this dataset. Worth a dedicated test cell.
6. **Ship the guarantee.** One competitor in 203 copies has a real risk reversal and it ran
   124 days. Cheapest differentiation available.
7. **Start the PR/creator flywheel.** The single longest-running ad here (117 days) is a
   whitelisted MD creator post. Whitelisting a credible practitioner outperforms brand-voice
   ads on duration by a wide margin in this category.
8. **Keep the compliance verbs.** "Supports", "helps support", "designed to", "studied for."
   Add the FDA disclaimer on any structure/function claim, and "individual results will vary"
   on anything testimonial-shaped.
